Registering a trademark that describes the product itself—or one that originated as a common market term—seems impossible at first glance, and in fact the INPI rejects signs without inherent distinctiveness. But there is a path recognized by law, by ordinance, and in the Trademark Manual: **acquired distinctiveness** (or *secondary meaning*). When consolidated use makes the public begin to see the sign as a trademark, the INPI may grant registration.

This guide explains what this legal concept is, at which points in the process the INPI accepts the request for examination, how to prove the acquisition of distinctiveness in practice, and what has changed on the Institute's agenda in 2026—including the discussion on formal guidelines for opinion surveys.

## What is acquired distinctiveness (secondary meaning)

The Industrial Property Law (Law No. 9.279/1996) requires that a sign be distinctive to be registrable as a trademark ([art. 122](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm)). Items II, VI, VII, VIII, XVIII and XXI of art. 124 prohibit the registration of signs that do not meet this requirement by nature: generic, necessary, common, vulgar or merely descriptive terms, necessary product shapes, isolated letters and numbers, isolated colors, and advertising slogans.

The exception is precisely acquired distinctiveness. According to the [INPI Trademark Manual, section 5.9.10](http://manualdemarcas.inpi.gov.br/projects/manual/wiki/5%C2%B79%C2%B710_Distintividade_adquirida), in exceptional situations a sign originally lacking distinctiveness may acquire, through use, sufficient distinctive effect for registration. The phenomenon is described as a "semantic addition": to the primary meaning—the direct relationship with the product or service—a secondary meaning is added, that of the trademark. The consumer stops reading the sign as a description and begins to identify it as an indicator of origin.

This concept is not to be confused with **well-known status** (art. 126 of the LPI) or **high renown** (art. 125 of the LPI): these are protections with their own requirements and purposes. In acquired distinctiveness, what is proven is the change in public perception regarding that specific sign.

One structural point is worth noting: the INPI has expressly recognized acquired distinctiveness since [INPI/PR Ordinance No. 8/2022](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/legislacao), whose chapter XVI-A governs the matter, and the Trademark Manual details the procedure in section 5.9.10, with its own electronic service (Service 3021). In other words: this is not an end-of-process argument, but rather a formal request with specific procedural windows.

## When the INPI accepts the request for examination

The examination of the acquisition of distinctiveness may only be requested at five moments, all requiring a clear and express statement:

1. **On the filing date** of the registration application, by means of a declaration attached to the filing petition;
2. **Within 60 days of the publication** of the application, by means of a statement attached to the manifestation petition;
3. **On the filing date of an appeal** against a refusal based on lack of inherent distinctiveness (checking a specific box on the appeal form);
4. **On the filing date of a response to an opposition** based on lack of distinctiveness;
5. **On the filing date of counterarguments** (contrarrazões) to an administrative nullity proceeding based on lack of distinctiveness.

The request **will not be considered** if it is made more than once in the same proceeding or outside these scenarios—for example, after a refusal decision without filing an appeal, or after the final decision on the appeal, when the administrative proceeding has already ended. In cases of appeal and counterarguments, the applicant must indicate on the form at which point the INPI should assess the acquisition of distinctiveness.

## Step by step to request the examination

The practical flow, consolidated from the Trademark Manual and Ordinance 8/2022:

1. **Identify whether the sign is eligible.** Descriptive marks, overly evocative marks, product shapes, and three-dimensional signs without inherent distinctiveness are the typical cases—provided there is already consolidated use in Brazil.
2. **Conduct a prior-art search** before any action, to understand the risk of conflicting with third-party trademarks.
3. **Submit the statement within the applicable procedural window** (filing, 60 days from publication, appeal, opposition, or nullity—depending on the stage of the proceeding).
4. **File Service 3021 within 60 days** of the request for examination. The service is called "Submission of documents to prove acquired distinctiveness" and is the vehicle for the evidentiary set.
5. **Monitor the proceeding.** The evidentiary set may be supplemented by an amendment to the petition or in response to an office action from the INPI, subject to deadlines. The examination decision may be appealed.

Fees (GRU) follow the currently valid INPI Trademark Services Fee Schedule—amounts change frequently and should be checked on the official portal before paying.

## What the evidence must demonstrate

The supporting documentation must demonstrate two cumulative elements:

- **Substantially continuous use of the mark during the 3 years immediately preceding** the date of the examination request; and
- **Recognition by a relevant portion of the national consumer public** of the sign as a trademark, capable of identifying the products or services and differentiating them from competitors.

When analyzing the evidence, the INPI observes points that in practice defeat many applications:

- **National scope.** Evidence from abroad or obtained via the internet is only considered if it demonstrates that the audience reached is Brazilian. Registration is valid for the entire national territory, so the acquisition of distinctiveness must be demonstrated in all regions of the country.
- **Market survey as the most appropriate instrument.** The Trademark Manual considers the opinion survey the most suitable means to prove that the public recognizes the sign as a trademark.
- **Supporting evidence.** The applicant may attach details of the volume of investments over the years, linking the mark and investments to the claimed products, with media type and geographic reach.
- **Decision on the sign as a whole.** The INPI assesses whether the sign under examination—not isolated components—has acquired sufficient distinctiveness for the requested specification.

The examples in the Manual illustrate the standard. In a hypothetical case of a trademark for shirts, annual website accesses without identification of origin, numbers of social media likes without user origins, sales reports from Europe and the USA, and advertising spending in Latin America as a whole were presented—a set considered **insufficient**, because it did not demonstrate the perception of the Brazilian public. In turn, for "macrobiotic foods," evidence of monthly accesses to the national website broken down by state, monthly sales reports in all state capitals and in the Federal District, and a national market survey were treated as **adequate**.

## What to watch for in 2026: guidelines for opinion surveys

In August 2026, at the Meeting with Interested Parties of the Directorate of Trademarks, Industrial Designs and Geographical Indications (DIRMA), the [INPI announced that it is working on guidelines for opinion surveys on acquired distinctiveness](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias/encontro-promove-dialogo-e-apresenta-novidades-no-exame-de-marcas), as well as improvements in the examination of three-dimensional and position marks. Also under discussion is a proposal to change the examination flow for trademark applications, with a new examination stage and a change in the period for filing oppositions.

In practice, this means that proving secondary meaning tends to gain more objective parameters in the coming months—which is good for those who need to plan the registration of weak marks: a well-designed opinion survey, with a sample of the national consumer public, should follow the roadmap the INPI publishes. Three-dimensional and position marks, which often depend on acquired distinctiveness, are in the same package of examination improvements.

## Conclusion

Acquired distinctiveness is neither a shortcut nor a guarantee: it is a legal institution with a formal procedure, rigid procedural windows, and a high evidentiary standard—continuous use for 3 years and national recognition by the public. But for signs that already have a real market trajectory, it is the legitimate path to turning use into rights. With the opinion survey guidelines the INPI is preparing, proof should become more predictable—and those with a descriptive or three-dimensional mark consolidated in Brazil should start organizing their evidence file now.
