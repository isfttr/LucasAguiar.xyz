An electronic game is, legally, a complex work: source code, art, music, narrative, and brand coexist in the same product, and each of these elements is protected by a different form of intellectual property. There is no single "game registration" in Brazil — and it is precisely this gap that INPI began discussing in August 2026, with the [Public Consultation No. 02/2026 on the registration of electronic games](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias/inpi-abre-tomada-publica-de-subsidios-sobre-registro-de-jogos-eletronicos).

While the consultation does not become a rule, Brazilian game developers need to know how to put together a protection strategy using the tools that already exist. This guide shows, modality by modality, what to protect, how to do it, and how much it costs — from software registration to patent filing.

## What is an electronic game under Brazilian law?

There is no single category in the Brazilian legal system that "protects the game." What exists is a set of laws that protect each layer of the product:

| Game layer | IP modality | Legal basis | Registration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source code / engine | Computer program (copyright) | Law 9.609/98 | INPI (optional, recommended) |
| Title, logo, franchise name | Trademark | LPI, arts. 122-128 | INPI (mandatory for exclusivity) |
| Characters, art, UI | Industrial design + copyright | LPI, arts. 95-96; Law 9.610/98 | INPI (DI) / automatic (copyright) |
| Music, script, narrative | Copyright | Law 9.610/98 | Automatic |
| Innovative mechanics and algorithms | Invention patent (specific cases) | LPI, arts. 8-11 | INPI (substantive examination) |
| Mechanics, algorithms, data | Trade secret | LPI, art. 195 | None (confidentiality) |

The logic is simple: what is expression (code, art, text, music) is protected by copyright; what identifies the product in the market (name, logo) is protected by trademark; what is form applied to an object (three-dimensional character, layout) is protected by industrial design; and what is a new technical solution (a rendering system, a matchmaking method) can, in theory, be patented.

## 1. Software registration (computer program) — the fundamental step

The game's source code is automatically protected as a computer program by [Law 9.609/98](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9609.htm), from the moment of creation — just as with literary works. Automatic protection, however, has a weak point: proving **who** created it and **when**. That is where INPI registration comes in.

Computer program registration at INPI is **declaratory**: the Institute does not examine the code, it only stores a hash of the program and publishes the registration in the RPI, creating a timestamp of authorship and ownership. In practice, it is the most solid evidence available in Brazil for judicial and commercial disputes (licensing, publishing contracts, investment due diligence).

The process is done through the [e-Software](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/programas-de-computador) system, costs **R$ 210.00** (fee code 730) and takes a few days until publication. The complete step by step is in our [guide to registering computer programs at INPI]({{< relref "posts/guia-registro-programa-computador-inpi/" >}}).

**Best practices:** register the software before publicly disclosing the game, keep the hash and deposit updated for each relevant version, and register in the name of the company (not the individual developer) to avoid ownership disputes with partners and collaborators.

## 2. Trademark: protect the game's title and identity

The game's name, logo, and franchise name are protected by **trademark registration** at INPI, governed by the LPI (Law 9.279/96). Without registration, any competitor can use an identical or confusingly similar name — and the game's title, by itself, does not create exclusivity.

For electronic games, the relevant classes are usually:

- **Class 9** — computer programs, downloadable video games, and recorded media;
- **Class 41** — entertainment services, publishing, and operation of online games;
- **Class 28** — board games, toys, and physical merchandise.

The filing cost in 2026 is **R$ 880.00** (with a pre-approved specification) or **R$ 1,720.00** (free-form specification), with a **50% discount** for MEI, ME, and EPP — amounts from Ordinance GM/MDIC No. 110/2025, detailed in our [complete table of trademark costs at INPI]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}).

**Attention:** before filing, perform a [trademark prior art search]({{< relref "posts/busca-anterioridade-marcas-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}) — a game title already registered by a third party in a conflicting class can block the registration and force a rebranding at the worst possible time. Trademark protection lasts **10 years**, renewable for equal periods.

## 3. Industrial design: characters and art as a registrable asset

Characters, creatures, vehicles, and art elements with three-dimensional form or ornamental pattern can be registered as **industrial design** (LPI, arts. 95-96). Registration guarantees exclusive use in Brazil for **10 years**, renewable for up to 3 periods of 5 years (maximum 25 years).

The filing is done through the e-DI system. It is worthwhile for characters that will become franchises, merchandise, and physical products — a mascot registered as an industrial design is an asset that can be licensed independently of the game.

For two-dimensional art (illustrations, concept art, UI), protection is through copyright (see below), not through industrial design — industrial design protects the three-dimensional form or ornamental pattern applied to an object.

## 4. Copyright: the automatic (and underestimated) layer

Music, script, dialogues, illustrations, and the game's audiovisual work are protected by **copyright** under [Law 9.610/98](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm), **automatically**, without mandatory registration — Brazil is a signatory to the Berne Convention. There is no fee or bureaucracy: protection is born with creation.

What automatic protection lacks is proof. Therefore, the recommended practice is to document creation: version files with dates, keep copyright assignment contracts with artists, musicians, and screenwriters (in writing, with a clause of total and definitive assignment), and register with INPI whatever is a computer program. Poorly drafted assignment contracts are the most common cause of ownership disputes in independent studios.

## 5. Patents: what can (and cannot) be patented in a game

Here lies the biggest confusion. **Article 10 of the LPI** states that the following are **not** considered inventions: *computer programs in themselves* (item V) and *rules of games* (item VII). In other words: "patenting the game" or "patenting a game mechanic" is not possible in Brazil.

But this does not close the door to technical innovation. INPI examines **computer-implemented inventions** (CII): when software produces a **technical effect** on something physical or a process — a compression method for streaming, a real-time rendering system, virtual reality hardware, a multiplayer synchronization algorithm — the patent application may be viable, provided it meets the requirements of novelty, inventive step, and industrial application (LPI, arts. 8-11).

In practice, for studios:

- **Game mechanic, rule, business method** → not patentable; protect as a trade secret (LPI, art. 195) with NDAs and access controls;
- **Technical solution implemented by software with a technical effect** → consider filing a patent;
- **Source code** → computer program registration (not a patent).

If your innovation falls into the second category, see the step-by-step in our [patent filing guide at INPI]({{< relref "posts/guia-deposito-patente-inpi-2026/" >}}).

## 6. INPI's public consultation on electronic games

On August 25, 2026, INPI published the [Public Consultation No. 02/2026](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias/inpi-abre-tomada-publica-de-subsidios-sobre-registro-de-jogos-eletronicos) to receive contributions on **regulatory options related to the registration of electronic games**. The consultation was also published in the Notices section of [RPI No. 2903](https://revistas.inpi.gov.br/rpi/).

The practical points for those who want to participate:

- **Deadline:** 60 days, counted from publication in the Official Gazette of the Union No. 159;
- **How to participate:** exclusively through the [Brasil Participativo Platform](https://brasilparticipativo.pb.gov.br/), addressing the registration of electronic games;
- **What is at stake:** the possible creation of a specific registration procedure for games — today, as this guide shows, protection is fragmented among software, trademark, industrial design, and copyright.

For studios and industry associations, the consultation is an opportunity to influence the future rule. For those who just want to protect the game today, the path remains the combined strategy described above.

## Conclusion

Protecting an electronic game in Brazil is not a single act; it is a **layered strategy**: software is registered at INPI as a computer program, the title and logo as a trademark, characters as industrial design, and artistic expression is born protected by copyright. Patents are reserved for specific technical innovations — never for the game "in itself," which the LPI expressly excludes.

The public consultation opened by INPI in August 2026 may, in the future, create a more direct path for the industry. Meanwhile, studios that register early, document creation, and formalize contracts enter the market with a much more valuable — and defensible — asset.
