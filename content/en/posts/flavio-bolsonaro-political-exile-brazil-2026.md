The narrative portraying Flávio Bolsonaro as a "political exile" must be examined against the results of Brazil's 2026 general elections. His party, the Liberal Party (PL), a major right-wing political force closely associated with former President Jair Bolsonaro, elected 121 members to the Chamber of Deputies and will hold 29 seats in the Federal Senate in 2027, making it the largest party in both chambers of Brazil's National Congress. The PL also won six state governorships.

## The Bolsonaro family grew stronger at the polls

The Bolsonaro family itself expanded its political representation: Carlos Bolsonaro, Flávio's brother, was elected senator for Santa Catarina; another brother, Jair Renan Bolsonaro, was elected federal deputy for the same state; Michelle Bolsonaro, his father's wife and Brazil's former first lady, was elected senator for the Federal District, which includes the national capital, Brasília; and his uncle, Renato Bolsonaro, was elected federal deputy for São Paulo. These results demonstrate that Bolsonaro's political movement, commonly known as *Bolsonarismo*, continues to enjoy substantial opportunities to participate in democratic elections and exercise political power.

## What the TSE and the Meta AI episode actually show

The Superior Electoral Court (TSE), Brazil's highest electoral authority, responsible for overseeing national elections and enforcing electoral legislation, is presided over by Justice Kassio Nunes Marques, with Justice André Mendonça serving as vice president. Both were appointed to Brazil's Supreme Federal Court (STF), the country's highest constitutional court, by Jair Bolsonaro himself. On October 15, the two justices met with representatives of Meta, the parent company of Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp, to discuss artificial intelligence and WhatsApp.

There were even allegations that Meta had treated Flávio Bolsonaro more favorably than his political opponents: tests conducted by *Folha de S.Paulo*, one of Brazil's leading newspapers, found that [Meta AI]({{< relref "posts/muse-meta-termos-de-uso-problemas-responsabilidade/" >}}) generated campaign propaganda supporting Flávio while refusing equivalent requests involving President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, commonly known as Lula, his principal political adversary. The TSE ordered equal treatment of candidates. Meta denied deliberately favoring anyone, and the court did not establish that intentional manipulation had occurred. Consequently, this episode does not support the portrayal of Flávio as a candidate systematically silenced by Brazilian institutions.

## The investigations: distinct allegations, due process

The investigations involving Flávio Bolsonaro concern distinct allegations, each requiring independent examination. They include suspected irregularities involving online betting companies and lawyer Willer Tomaz; alleged defamation of President Lula; and possible irregularities in the financing of *Dark Horse*, a film project reportedly funded by banker Daniel Vorcaro.

The investigation into the film was authorized by Justice André Mendonça himself, following a request from Brazil's Federal Police and with the approval of the Office of the Prosecutor General (PGR), the independent federal institution responsible for bringing criminal charges in cases within its jurisdiction, including proceedings before the Supreme Court. Flávio denies wrongdoing and is entitled to due process and the presumption of innocence. An investigation is not equivalent to a criminal conviction. His departure from Brazil occurred after authorities ordered him to surrender his passports, an order that was not complied with.

## The precedent: Bolsonaro allies seeking refuge abroad

Seeking refuge abroad while facing criminal proceedings or convictions is not unprecedented among prominent figures associated with Bolsonaro's political movement.

Former congresswoman Carla Zambelli left Brazil after receiving a ten-year prison sentence for her involvement in the unlawful intrusion into the computer systems of the National Council of Justice (CNJ), the institution responsible for administrative oversight of Brazil's judiciary. She subsequently sought protection in Italy, claiming political persecution. She also received a separate sentence of five years and three months for unlawful possession of a firearm and illegal coercion arising from an incident in which she pursued a journalist while armed.

Alexandre Ramagem, a former federal congressman and former director of Brazil's national intelligence agency (ABIN) under Jair Bolsonaro, was sentenced to 16 years, one month, and 15 days in prison for his participation in the alleged coup plot aimed at overturning Brazil's democratic order. He traveled to the United States and requested political asylum, alleging judicial persecution.

Silvinei Vasques, former director of Brazil's Federal Highway Police (PRF), was sentenced to 24 years and six months in prison for his involvement in the same coup-related conspiracy. After violating court-imposed precautionary restrictions, he was arrested in Paraguay in December 2025 while attempting to travel to El Salvador using documents belonging to another person. Paraguayan authorities subsequently expelled him and handed him over to the Brazilian Federal Police.

Eduardo Bolsonaro, another of Flávio's brothers and a former federal congressman, had already been living in the United States since February 2025, before his conviction, while publicly claiming that Brazil had become an authoritarian regime. In June 2026, he was sentenced to four years and two months in prison for attempting to obstruct judicial proceedings through pressure intended to interfere with the criminal trial of his father, former President Jair Bolsonaro. An arrest warrant was issued against Eduardo in October.

## Different cases, different motivations

The distinctions between these cases are important. Not everyone left Brazil after being convicted, and neither criminal responsibility nor identical personal motivations should automatically be attributed to all those involved. In Zambelli's case, for example, Italian judicial authorities rejected the extradition request relating to the CNJ cyberattack case after raising concerns about the impartiality of Justice Alexandre de Moraes, the Brazilian Supreme Court justice overseeing the proceedings. A separate extradition request concerning the armed pursuit of the journalist remains under consideration. This distinction matters because it would be equally misleading to dismiss every objection to Brazilian judicial proceedings as inherently fabricated or politically motivated.

## Privileged jurisdiction vs. presidential immunity

Flávio Bolsonaro will leave elected office in February 2027, after approximately 24 years holding elected positions. However, leaving office does not automatically eliminate the special jurisdictional protections afforded to certain public officials under Brazilian law, known as *foro por prerrogativa de função*, often loosely translated as "privileged jurisdiction." Under the Supreme Federal Court's interpretation, the Court may retain jurisdiction over offenses committed during the exercise of public office and directly connected to official duties, even after the official's term has ended. This is a procedural rule determining which court has jurisdiction, not immunity from investigation, prosecution, or conviction.

His electoral defeat nevertheless prevented him from acquiring the distinct, temporary constitutional protection afforded to a sitting Brazilian president against criminal liability, during the presidential term, for acts unrelated to presidential functions. These are two different legal mechanisms and should not be confused.

## What can — and cannot — be concluded

Against this background, it is entirely legitimate to question the political circumstances surrounding Flávio Bolsonaro's departure from Brazil while facing personal criminal investigations, although those circumstances alone cannot establish his exclusive motivation for leaving. What can be documented is a recurring pattern among prominent Bolsonaro allies of seeking refuge abroad while confronting Brazilian criminal proceedings or convictions. What cannot reasonably be sustained is the assumption that receiving protection or asylum in the United States automatically constitutes proof that the individual is a victim of political persecution. Such a claim requires independent scrutiny of the underlying facts, the judicial proceedings, and the applicable legal standards, rather than treating an American decision as an unquestionable certificate of innocence or political victimhood.
