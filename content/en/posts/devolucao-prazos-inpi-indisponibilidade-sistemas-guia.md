When INPI's e-Patentes, e-Marcas or filing systems go down, anyone with a pending case asks the same question first: *what about my deadlines?* Not every outage triggers deadline reinstatement — but outages lasting longer than a set window have an automatic reinstatement rule. This guide explains exactly how Ordinance INPI/PR No. 049/2021 works, when you can count on reinstatement, and what to do to avoid losing a deadline on an unstable day.

## The 60-minute rule: what Ordinance 049/2021 says

The basis for deadline reinstatement due to technical unavailability sits in **art. 6 of Ordinance INPI/PR No. 049, of December 3, 2021**. The rule is objective: if the unavailability of the electronic systems lasts for **more than 60 continuous minutes**, there is **automatic reinstatement of the deadlines that fall due on that date of the occurrence**.

In other words, once the continuous 60-minute threshold is crossed on the same day, any deadline that expires on that date is automatically carried over — you do not need to request anything or file a reinstatement petition at that first stage.

In practice, INPI applied this rule, for example, during the occurrence of **October 19 and 20, 2026**, when prolonged instability made reinstatement of the deadlines falling on those two days mandatory.

## Why it is "reinstatement", not "extension"

It is important to separate two concepts that are often confused:

| Concept | What it means |
|---------|---------------|
| **Deadline reinstatement** | A deadline that would expire on the outage date is moved to the next business day. The rule is automatic when the outage exceeds 60 continuous minutes. |
| **Deadline extension** | A lengthening of the deadline for another legal reason (e.g., the half-deadline extension when a decision comes by official gazette). This is not what happens due to unavailability. |

Reinstatement, therefore, does **not** extend the original deadline: it only shifts the due date that fell on the problem day. If you still had several days of deadline left, you gain no extra days because of the outage.

## When reinstatement is guaranteed: what to check

To know whether a specific deadline was reinstated, check three points:

1. **Did the outage exceed 60 continuous minutes?** Only that triggers the automatic rule.
2. **Did the deadline fall exactly on the occurrence date?** Deadlines due on other days are not affected.
3. **Did INPI publish a notice in Section I of the RPI?** That is the official vehicle formalizing the reinstatement and indicating the new date.

> ⚠️ **Pitfall:** short outages (restored the same day, under 60 continuous minutes) do **not** generate automatic reinstatement. They follow the normal deadline flow.

## What to do when the systems go down

If you have a deadline falling on a day when the systems are unstable, the safe workflow is:

1. **Document the outage.** Save screenshots, access attempt times, and the link to INPI's official notice about the occurrence.
2. **Confirm it passed 60 continuous minutes.** Automatic reinstatement only exists in these cases.
3. **Wait for the Unavailability Report.** After normalization, INPI publishes the report formalizing the reinstatement and recording the interruption period. This is the official document of the occurrence.
4. **Check the notice in the RPI (Section I).** This is where the new due date formally appears.
5. **If your deadline was not reinstated and you believe it should have been**, file a petition attaching the unavailability report and evidence, on the next business day.

## How to follow up on your case

After an occurrence, check whether the new date was applied to your case. Do not assume the reinstatement was applied automatically without confirming. Tracking and filing procedures are handled through INPI's petitioning system.

## What to watch going forward

- **Recurring outages are common.** INPI faces periodic instability. Having a critical deadline on an occurrence day requires active monitoring.
- **The 60-minute rule is objective and stable.** It does not depend on INPI's discretionary decision — it is Ordinance 049/2021's trigger. So this guide remains valid for future occurrences.
- **Keep evidence.** In a deadline dispute, the official unavailability report is the strongest proof available. Saving screenshots and links is cheap and prevents headaches.

## Conclusion

Deadline reinstatement due to unavailability at INPI is an **automatic and objective** rule when the instability exceeds 60 continuous minutes: deadlines due on the occurrence date are carried over without any request. The habit that separates a smooth experience from a stressful one is **documenting the occurrence, waiting for the Unavailability Report and checking the RPI notice** before assuming any new date.
