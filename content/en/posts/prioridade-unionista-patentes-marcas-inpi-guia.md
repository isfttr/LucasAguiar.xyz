The right of priority under the Paris Convention (CUP) is usually the most misunderstood — and, at the same time, the most strategic — aspect of filing patents, trademarks and industrial designs in Brazil. Many applicants lose fatal deadlines, claim priority by mistake, or simply don't know that, used well, this tool preserves the original filing date for up to 12 months.

This guide explains, based on Law 9.279/96 (Brazilian Industrial Property Law — LPI) and the Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property (CUP), what the priority right is, what the deadlines are per IP type, how to prove priority before the INPI, and how the tool connects to the PCT system.

## What is the unionist priority right

The priority right originates in **Article 4 of the Paris Convention (CUP)**, an international treaty Brazil has signed since 1884. The mechanism guarantees that anyone who files an application in a member country of the Union can file the same application in another member country **within a set period counted from the date of the first filing**, and have that first filing date recognized as the priority date in the new country.

In practice the effect is twofold:

- **Preservation of novelty**: events occurring between the first filing and the foreign filing (such as publications, commercial use or third-party filings) do not prejudice the later application, as long as they happen within the period.
- **Date anticipation**: for novelty and inventive step analysis in Brazil, the original filing date counts, not the date of the national filing.

In Brazil, the right is secured by **art. 16 of the LPI**.

## Priority periods by IP type

Deadlines vary by IP type and are set by the CUP:

| IP type | Priority period (CUP art. 4) |
|---------|------------------------------|
| Patent (invention) | 12 months |
| Utility model | 12 months |
| Industrial design | 6 months |
| Trademark | 6 months |

## How to claim priority before the INPI

The procedure is set out in art. 16 of the LPI. The claim must be made **at the time of filing**, indicating the country of origin and the number of the priority application. For trademarks and industrial designs, the INPI e-systems (e-Marcas, e-DI) have specific fields for declaring priority.

Additional priorities can be supplemented **within 60 days** of the Brazilian filing date (art. 16 § 1).

### Under the PCT system (patents)

For [PCT filings]({{< relref "posts/entrada-fase-nacional-pct-brasil-guia-2026/" >}}), the priority is claimed in the international application and carried into the [national phase]({{< relref "posts/entrada-fase-nacional-pct-brasil-guia-2026/" >}}). The 30-month deadline to enter the Brazilian national phase is counted **from the earliest priority date**, not from the international filing date — a crucial detail that often causes errors.

## How to prove priority (art. 16 § 2 and § 3)

The priority claim must be proven, not just declared. The document must contain the application number in the origin country, filing date, title, description and, where applicable, claims and drawings — accompanied by a filing certificate or equivalent document with a simple translation of the identifying data.

Proof deadlines:

| Situation | Deadline |
|-----------|----------|
| Proof not made at filing | Within **180 days** of the Brazilian filing |
| PCT international applications | Translation within **60 days** of national phase entry |
| Priority acquired by assignment | Document within **180 days** (or 60 days in national phase) |

**Failure to prove priority within the deadline causes loss of priority** (art. 16 § 7).

## Internal priority (art. 17 LPI)

Beyond international priority, the LPI provides a domestic mechanism. Art. 17 allows a patent or utility model application filed in Brazil without priority, and not yet published, to secure priority for a later application on the same subject, **within 1 year**, by the same applicant or successors.

## Common mistakes

- Losing the 12/6-month deadline
- Failing to prove priority within 180 days
- Counting the PCT national phase deadline from the wrong starting point
- Claiming priority for new matter not present in the original application

## Practical strategy

To protect an invention both in Brazil and abroad, the most cost-effective route is to file first in Brazil, then enter the PCT system within 12 months, and never postpone proof beyond 180 days.
