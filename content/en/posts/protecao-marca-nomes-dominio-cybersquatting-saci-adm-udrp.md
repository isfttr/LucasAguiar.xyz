A competitor registers `yourbrand.com.br` before you do. Another buys `yourbrand.com` and tries to sell it back at a high price. This is the classic **cybersquatting** scenario: registering a domain name in bad faith to exploit a third party's trademark or identity. The good news: there are specific administrative routes — faster and cheaper than litigation — to recover these domains, both under `.br` and for international extensions. This guide explains how each system works, what it costs and how to choose the right route.

## What cybersquatting is and why it affects your brand

Cybersquatting is the practice of registering a domain name that reproduces or imitates a third party's trademark, without legitimate interest, with the intent to profit — whether by selling the domain to the trademark owner, diverting traffic, or tarnishing the brand. Common cases include:

- **Typosquatting** — registering misspelled variations (`facebok.com`).
- **Reverse defensive registration** — registering the domain before the legitimate trademark owner.
- **Mass registration** — one person registers hundreds of names for resale.

In Brazil, a trademark registered at the INPI gives the owner the right to prevent third parties from unauthorized reproduction or imitation of protected signs, under Articles 129 and 130 of the [Industrial Property Law (Law 9.279/1996)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm). But domain name disputes have their own mechanics: they are resolved not only at the INPI but in the administrative dispute-resolution systems — SACI-Adm for `.br` and UDRP for generic extensions.

## Two administrative routes, two domain universes

Before choosing a route, you must know where the domain is registered:

| System | Applies to | Administered by | Nature |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|--------|
| **SACI-Adm** | `.br` domains (registered after Oct/2010) | Registro.br / NIC.br | Administrative arbitration |
| **UDRP** | gTLDs (`.com`, `.net`, `.org`, etc.) | ICANN, with accredited providers (e.g., WIPO AMC) | Administrative procedure |

### SACI-Adm: the route for .br domains

The **Administrative System for Internet Conflict Resolution concerning ".br" domain names** (SACI-Adm), managed by [Registro.br](https://registro.br/dominio/saci-adm/), resolves disputes between a `.br` domain holder and a third party contesting the legitimacy of the registration. It is the direct Brazilian counterpart of the UDRP, with its own rules set out in the [SACI-Adm Regulation](https://registro.br/dominio/saci-adm/regulamento/), in force since October 2022.

For the trademark owner to prevail, the regulation broadly requires that:

1. The domain name is **identical or sufficiently similar** to the complainant's trademark.
2. The domain holder has **no rights or legitimate interests** in the name.
3. The domain was registered and is being used **in bad faith**.

If all three requirements are met, the panel orders the **transfer** or **cancellation** of the registration. The procedure is faster and cheaper than litigation, and is the main route to recover an improperly registered `.com.br`.

### UDRP: the route for gTLDs

The **Uniform Domain-Name Dispute-Resolution Policy** (UDRP) was created by [ICANN](https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/help/dndr/udrp-en) and applies to generic extensions (`.com`, `.net`, `.org`, and the new gTLDs). The procedure is run by accredited providers — the [WIPO Arbitration and Mediation Center (WIPO AMC)](https://www.wipo.int/amc/en/domains/) being the most used worldwide.

The UDRP criteria are virtually the same as SACI-Adm: identity/similarity with the trademark, absence of legitimate interest by the registrant, and registration/use in bad faith. When the dispute is international or involves domains outside `.br`, UDRP is the right route — with decisions typically delivered within months and predictable costs.

## How to choose between SACI-Adm, UDRP and litigation

The choice depends on where the domain is registered and the urgency:

- **`.br` domain registered by a third party** → SACI-Adm at Registro.br.
- **gTLD domain (`.com`, `.net`...) registered by a third party** → UDRP via an accredited provider (e.g., WIPO).
- **Complex dispute requiring damages or broader relief** → litigation, which can run in parallel.

Important: **the trademark must exist and be protected.** Both SACI-Adm and UDRP require the complainant to show prior rights — ideally a trademark registered at the [INPI](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br) (for `.br`) or at a trademark office in a Madrid Protocol country (for gTLDs). Those who have not yet registered their mark should consider doing so before starting a dispute, to strengthen their position.

## Practical step-by-step to recover a domain

1. **Confirm domain ownership** — run a `whois` to identify the registrant and registrar.
2. **Gather trademark evidence** — registration certificate at the INPI or a filed application; if applicable, proof of use and distinctiveness.
3. **Identify the route** — `.br` → SACI-Adm; gTLD → UDRP.
4. **File the complaint** — complete the provider's form (Registro.br for `.br`; WIPO or another accredited provider for gTLDs), attaching evidence and paying the fee.
5. **Follow the procedure** — the holder has a deadline to respond; a panel (single or three-member) decides.
6. **Enforce the decision** — if favorable, the domain is transferred or cancelled.

## Costs and timelines

Fees vary by provider. Under SACI-Adm, Registro.br charges depending on the number of domains claimed; under UDRP, each provider (WIPO, ADNDRC, etc.) has its own schedule, also scaled by the number of domains. In general, costs are significantly lower than an IP lawsuit, and the procedure usually concludes within **weeks to a few months**, versus years in traditional litigation.

> **Prevention tip:** the cheapest cost is defensive registration. Whoever is about to launch a brand should register the `.com.br` domain and relevant variations (`.com`, `.net`, common typos) **before** publicizing the brand — ideally in parallel with the INPI registration.

## What to watch

- **Consolidation of SACI-Adm** — the 2022 regulation modernized the procedure; follow Registro.br's administrative case law.
- **UDRP expansion** — with the new gTLDs, case volume keeps growing; WIPO decisions are widely recognized.
- **Trademark + domain integration** — domain disputes increasingly start with trademark verification; owners who register first and in good faith protect their digital assets best.
