Since 2026, INPI has published a semi-annual report called **Economic Scenario of IP in Brazil**, which cross-references the Brazilian macroeconomic situation with the dynamics of industrial property rights filings and grants. The 2nd edition, from October/2026, presents for the first time projections of demand for IP assets until 2030 — useful material for anyone needing to plan brand, patent, and software strategy in Brazil.

This guide explains what the report measures, the methodology behind the projections, and, most importantly, what the numbers indicate for those deciding to file or register IP assets with INPI in the coming years.

## What is the Economic Scenario of IP report

The report is produced by INPI's **General Coordination of Economy and Innovation (CGEI)**, on a semi-annual basis. The aim is to offer an "integrated reading" between the recent performance of the Brazilian economy and the behavior of IP applications, generating subsidies for public policy evaluation — such as the National Intellectual Property Strategy (ENPI).

It is divided into two main parts:

1.  **Retrospective analysis (2016-2025):** how the 2014-2016 recession, the pandemic shock, and the recent recovery affected patents, trademarks, industrial designs, and computer programs.
2.  **Prospective analysis (2026-2030):** projections of demand for IP assets, anchored in the market expectations of the [Relatório Focus do Banco Central](https://www.bcb.gov.br/publicacoes/focus).

The report can be accessed for free on the dedicated page at [INPI Data — Cenário Econômico da PI](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/inpi-data/relatorios/cenario-economico-da-pi), along with the other economic publications of the agency.

## The retrospective: how the economic cycle shaped IP in Brazil

The period 2016-2025 shows three distinct cycles, and each left different marks on IP filings:

-   **2016-2019 (post-recession recovery):** GDP grew modestly (around 1.4% p.a. between 2017 and 2019), with a depressed industry and investment near 15% of GDP. This was the period when the growth of e-commerce and services boosted trademark filings.
-   **2020-2022 (pandemic shock):** GDP fell 3.3% in 2020, with a strong rebound in 2021 (+4.8%) and 2022 (+3.0%). Forced digitalization sustained demand for trademarks and computer programs.
-   **2023-2025 (expansion with deceleration):** GDP of +3.2% (2023), +3.4% (2024), and +2.3% (2025), with a highlight on agribusiness (+11.7% in 2025). Investment rose to about 16.8% of GDP in 2025, still below what is needed for sustained growth.

On the institutional side, the report highlights two milestones: the **Patent Backlog Combat Program** (intensified in 2020), which increased grants by recomposing the technical staff, and the **accession to the Madrid Protocol** (trademarks) and the **Hague Agreement** (industrial designs), which expanded the international presence of Brazilian applicants.

## INPI's projections until 2030 (by asset)

The most relevant point of the 2nd edition is the deposit projections for 2026-2030. In summary:

| IP Asset | Filings 2025 | Projection 2030 | Projected Growth (p.a.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patents | 29.532 | 37.288 | +4,9% |
| Trademarks | 504.313 | 508.288 | +0,4% |
| Industrial designs | 10.004 | 11.134 | +2,2% |
| Computer programs | 7.232 | 24.479 | +28,9% |

The readings per asset:

-   **Patents (+4.9% p.a.):** INPI projects continuous growth, reflecting the continuity of companies' technological strategies even with a less dynamic GDP. Patents remain the most "resilient" asset to the economic situation.
-   **Trademarks (+0.4% p.a.):** the major inflection point. After growing more than 11% p.a. between 2021 and 2025, the report indicates a **structural break in the series since the end of 2025**, attributed to regulatory changes that drastically reduced the pace of new filings. This is the asset to watch closely.
-   **Industrial designs (+2.2% p.a.):** moderate growth, in line with industry and FBCF, driven by the consolidation of design as a differentiation factor.
-   **Computer programs (+28.9% p.a.):** the most aggressive projection. The report associates the pace with the accelerated digitalization of economic activity, with filings jumping from ~7 thousand (2025) to ~24 thousand (2030).

## The methodology behind the projections

INPI uses **SARIMAX** models (seasonal autoregressive integrated moving average with exogenous variables) — an evolution of ARIMA models that incorporates seasonality and observable economic factors. Each asset has its explanatory variables:

-   **Patents:** export volume, real exchange rate, and the IBC-Br (BCB's monthly activity indicator).
-   **Trademarks:** GDP, investment (FBCF), industrial production, and a *dummy* for the structural break at the end of 2025.
-   **Industrial designs:** FBCF, industrial production, and a *dummy* related to the Hague System statistics.
-   **Computer programs:** IBC-Br, given that economic activity accelerates digital transformation.

The models are estimated by maximum likelihood, with selection by AIC/BIC criteria and out-of-sample validation (the report indicates that models with exogenous variables outperform univariate ones). In other words: the projections are conditional on the Focus macroeconomic scenario — if the economy deviates from the baseline scenario, the trajectories change.

## What this means in practice

For those working with IP, the report is a free and official source for basing decisions:

1.  **Portfolio planning:** if the trademark projection is for stabilization (and not decline), the bottleneck ceases to be volume and becomes portfolio management — including with the new examination rules brought by the [atualização do Manual de Marcas]({{< relref "posts/inpi-manual-marcas-alto-renome-protocolo-madri/" >}}).
2.  **Patent strategy:** the projected growth of 4.9% p.a. reinforces the importance of conducting a [busca de anterioridade bem feita]({{< relref "posts/inpi-credenciamento-busca-anterioridade-patentes/" >}}) before filing, as competition for examination also grows.
3.  **Software as an asset:** the +28.9% p.a. projection for computer programs is the biggest sign that software protection is becoming mainstream in Brazil — it is worth comparing with the [diferenças entre patente de software e registro de programa de computador]({{< relref "posts/patente-software-vs-registro-programa-computador-brasil/" >}}).
4.  **Costs:** with growing portfolios, the [custo de registro de marca no INPI]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}) remains the most common question for those starting out.

The report is semi-annual, so the ideal is to monitor it twice a year. The next edition should bring not only the update of the actual data from 2026 but also new projections conditioned on the 2027 macroeconomic scenario.
