Every trademark application filed at Brazil's patent office (**INPI**) requires the applicant to show **legitimacy**: the mark must be tied to a real economic activity of whoever files it. In 2026 this went from a formality to a systematically enforced requirement — the INPI now scans batches of applications for legitimacy problems and issues **office actions in bulk**. This guide explains what the law requires, why the INPI expanded its checks, which Nice classes are under scrutiny, and how to actually prove legitimacy without stalling your application.

## What is applicant legitimacy?

The legal basis is **Article 128, § 1 of Brazil's Industrial Property Law (Law 9.279/1996)**: individuals may only request trademark registration for an activity they are **actually carrying out**, proven by any means admitted by law. The same requirement appears in **Article 27 of INPI Ordinance 08/2022** and **item 5.5 of the Trademark Manual**.

In practice you cannot register a mark "out of the blue": you must demonstrate real activity compatible with the products or services claimed. For a **legal entity** with an active CNPJ, legitimacy is usually presumed by the corporate purpose. Problems typically appear for:

- **Individuals** — they must prove the activity by other means;
- **MEIs** (Brazil's individual micro-entrepreneur regime) — the CNPJ must be regular and the activity compatible with the class;
- **Holders with an inactive CNPJ** — such applications are flagged automatically.

## The Trademark Examination Optimization Plan

The INPI uses **batch merit office actions** to handle, at once, groups of applications that appear to have legitimacy problems. Instead of examining each case individually, it identifies patterns (e.g. an individual with many applications across many classes; a holder with an inactive CNPJ; specifications containing illicit items) and issues generic office actions for all of them.

The first phase (applied since 10 February 2026) split checks into five categories:

| Category | Situation checked |
|----------|-------------------|
| Individual/MEI in sensitive classes | Classes 3 (cosmetics) and 5 (pharmaceuticals) |
| Individual/MEI with multiple applications | Many applications in several classes |
| Holder with inactive CNPJ | Cancelled/deregistered CNPJ |
| Specification with illicit items | Prohibited products/services |
| Marks containing the word "cachaça" | Specific word element |

In October 2026, the INPI **expanded** this enforcement, organizing new situations into three subcategories that reveal how the examination now works:

- **Whole classes** — every application in the class must provide proof;
- **Whole classes with exceptions** — the requirement applies as a general rule, but with defined product/service exceptions;
- **Selected activities** — the requirement applies only to specific items inside the class.

### Which classes entered the radar in 2026

| Subcategory | Classes |
|-------------|---------|
| **Whole classes** | 1 (chemicals), 2 (paints and varnishes), 3 (cosmetics), 5 (pharmaceuticals), 7 (machines and engines), 13 (weapons/ammunition), 19 (building materials) |
| **Whole classes with exceptions** | 4 (oils and fuels), 10 (surgical, medical, dental, veterinary instruments), 11 (lighting and heating), 12 (vehicles), 34 (tobacco and cigarettes) |
| **Selected activities** | Specific parts of classes 36 (banking/insurance), 37 (mining, shipbuilding), 38 (telecommunications), 39 (energy/water/space transport), 40 (energy production, pharmaceutical manufacturing), 41 (universities, betting, zoos), 44 (hospital and laboratory services) |

The common thread is clear: the INPI is prioritizing **regulated activities** — those requiring specific **authorizations or qualifications** (pharmaceuticals, weapons, tobacco, financial services, health, energy, telecom). The full list with exceptions is annexed to the official notice.

## How to prove legitimacy

If you receive a legitimacy office action, your answer must show that the activity is **real, current and compatible** with the claimed class. The accepted means are broad ("any means admitted by law"). The most common:

- **Individuals:** service contracts, sales invoices, professional registrations (accounting, law, medical registers etc.), licenses and permits, income records, advertising, active store/social presence linked to the name;
- **MEIs:** file the annual MEI declaration (DASN-SIMEI) on time, keep the CNPJ active, and present sales receipts or the MEI receipt compatible with the activity; plus evidence the activity is actually exercised;
- **Legal entities:** articles of incorporation and an active CNPJ usually suffice, but applications outside the corporate purpose may draw an office action.

The "actually exercised activity" requirement means **timing matters**: showing mere future intention is usually not enough.

## Choosing the right class before filing

Many legitimacy office actions stem from a wrong class choice — the applicant picks a broad or incompatible class. Do two things before filing:

1. Read the [Nice Classification guide]({{< relref "posts/classificacao-nice-marcas-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}) to map which products/services belong to each class;
2. Run a [prior-art trademark search at the INPI]({{< relref "posts/busca-anterioridade-marcas-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}) to confirm registrability and see how similar applications were treated.

If you are an individual or MEI registering a mark tied to cosmetics, pharmaceuticals or another regulated activity, it is best to prepare the supporting documentation in advance — or gather it so you can answer as soon as the office action arrives.

## What to watch next

The Optimization Plan is continuous: the classes listed today tend to expand, and the INPI says it will keep refining examination based on results. If you have a pending application:

- **Track the process** — the office action arrives through the [INPI case-status tracker]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}});
- **Answer within the deadline** — there is a legal deadline to respond (with possible extension);
- **Do not ignore an inactive CNPJ** — applications of holders with a deregistered CNPJ are flagged automatically and, without regularization, may be shelved.

## Conclusion

Applicant-legitimacy verification has moved from a one-off requirement to a **systematic control** at the INPI. For individuals and MEIs, the biggest risk is not the office action itself — it is reaching it without documentation to answer. Picking the right class, keeping the CNPJ regular and gathering proof of activity up front are the three most efficient ways to keep a simple trademark application from dragging on for months.
