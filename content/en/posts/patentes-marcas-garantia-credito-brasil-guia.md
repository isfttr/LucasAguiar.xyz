Global investment in intangible assets — software, data, brands, R&D, and organizational capital — surpassed US$ 10 trillion for the first time in 2025, growing more than three times faster than investment in tangible assets, according to [WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/pressroom/en/articles/2026/article_0011.html). Brazil is the 7th largest economy in this ranking, with US$ 312 billion invested in intangibles in 2023 (7.6% of GDP), according to an INPI report in partnership with WIPO and the Luiss Business School.

The Brazilian paradox, however, is familiar to anyone who deals with industrial property: a company's most valuable intangible asset is, in practice, one of the hardest to turn into credit. Banks accept real estate, machinery, and receivables as collateral, but rarely patents or trademarks. It was to change this scenario that the [INPI Strategic Plan 2027-2036](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/governanca/planejamento-estrategico/plano-estrategico) elevated the monetization of IP assets to the status of a formal strategic objective, with a specific strategy to enable the use of IP as bank collateral.

This guide explains what Brazilian law already allows today, what INPI plans for the next ten years, and how a company can, in practice, begin monetizing its intellectual property assets.

## Why IP is difficult to use as collateral

Before understanding the instruments, it is necessary to understand the credit market's resistance. Four characteristics of IP assets explain why traditional banks view them with caution:

| Characteristic | Problem for the lender |
|----------------|------------------------|
| **Subjective valuation** | There is no liquid, transparent market that prices patents and trademarks the way there is for real estate |
| **Risk of invalidation** | A patent can be annulled in a nullity proceeding; a trademark can lapse due to non-use |
| **Periodic obligations** | Unpaid annuities and fees silently extinguish the right |
| **Complex enforcement** | In the event of default, the bank would have practical difficulty selling or exploiting the asset |

Therefore, in Brazil, IP rarely functions as primary collateral. The realistic path involves structuring the asset so it generates receivables — royalties — and using those receivables as backing for credit operations, complemented by the recording of encumbrances with INPI.

## What the law already allows: the legal basis in the LPI

Law 9.279/1996 (LPI) already offers the essential monetization instruments — what is missing is a culture of use. The main ones:

**Patents**
- **Assignment** — the application or patent can be assigned, in whole or in part (art. 58). INPI records the assignment and any limitation or encumbrance that falls on the application or patent (art. 59), and the recording produces effects vis-à-vis third parties upon publication (art. 60).
- **Voluntary license** — the owner may enter into a license agreement for exploitation (art. 61), and the agreement must be recorded with INPI to produce effects vis-à-vis third parties (art. 62). In the absence of an agreement on remuneration, the parties may request INPI to arbitrate (art. 65).

**Trademarks**
- **Assignment and encumbrances** — INPI records the assignment, limitations, and encumbrances on the application or registration (art. 136), with effects vis-à-vis third parties upon publication (art. 137).
- **Use license** — the owner may license the trademark while maintaining control over specifications and quality (art. 139), with mandatory recording for effects vis-à-vis third parties (art. 140).

**Technology transfer**
- Contracts involving technology transfer, franchising, and similar agreements must be registered with INPI to produce effects vis-à-vis third parties, with a decision within 30 days (art. 211).

The practical point: the [LPI](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm) does not expressly regulate the pledge of IP rights, but arts. 59, II, and 136, II, provide for the recording of "any limitation or encumbrance" — the legal gateway for registering with INPI guarantees constituted over the asset. In market practice, the most commonly used structure is the **fiduciary assignment of royalty receivables**: the recorded license agreement generates a predictable payment flow, and that flow is what the bank can assess and pledge.

Contract registrations and recordings are made through INPI's **e-Contratos** system, which received a new version of its forms in August 2026 — it is worth checking the [official service page](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/contratos-de-tecnologia-e-de-franquia) before filing.

## What INPI plans: Strategic Objective 6

The [Strategic Plan 2027-2036]({{< relref "posts/inpi-plano-estrategico-2027-2036-guia-completo/" >}}) dedicates Strategic Objective 6 to monetization: "to stimulate the monetization of Intellectual Property rights for the strategic use of intangible assets". Five strategies deserve attention from companies and financial institutions:

1. **Technical advisory in technology transfer** (6.1) — modernization of recording and registration processes, with new support services for the economic use of IP and improvement of the interactive contract data panel.
2. **IP asset valuation service** (6.2) — INPI wants to become a market reference in valuation, with guidelines, a governance model, and practical tools such as valuation calculators.
3. **Studies, observatories, and indicators** (6.3) — economic analyses to support public policies and decisions by economic agents.
4. **Monetization ecosystem** (6.4) — valuation methodologies and tools, training and certification of valuers, and market awareness.
5. **IP as bank collateral** (6.5) — coordination with financial regulators, banks, and the credit system to create regulatory mechanisms and incentives that enable the recognition of IP assets as collateral in banking operations.

If fulfilled, these strategies directly attack the four barriers in the table above: standardized valuation, risk indicators, market education, and regulatory framework together with the Central Bank and the CMN. Coordination with the financial system, however, requires long-term staying power — the plan has a ten-year term and the implementation horizon is years, not months.

## Practical step-by-step guide to monetizing IP in Brazil

While the ecosystem matures, a company that wants to use IP as a financial asset can start today:

**1. Regularize the asset.** The starting point is having the application/registration in good standing with INPI, with annuities and fees up to date — an extinguished right has no value as an asset. Track the status using the [guide to checking the status of INPI proceedings]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}}).

**2. Value the asset.** Commission a valuation using a recognized method: replacement cost, market comparison, or discounted cash flow (income). For trademarks, the income method considers the royalty a third party would pay to use the mark; for patents, one projects the royalty stream or the cost savings generated by exclusivity.

**3. Formalize contracts and record them with INPI.** Use licenses, assignments, or technology transfer agreements must be registered in e-Contratos to produce effects vis-à-vis third parties. It is the recorded agreement that turns the asset into a verifiable revenue stream — and it is what the bank can analyze.

**4. Use receivables as backing.** With the recorded license agreement generating predictable royalties, it is possible to structure a fiduciary assignment of receivables and present the flow as accessory collateral in credit operations, complementing traditional guarantees.

**5. Monitor regulatory milestones.** Track the implementation of INPI's valuation service, public consultations on the topic, and any bills on intangible collateral — discussions on the [improvement of the LPI]({{< relref "posts/lpi-30-anos-propostas-aperfeicoamento-inpi/" >}}) could bring express provisions for these instruments.

## What to watch

IP monetization in Brazil has three signs of maturation to watch: (1) the effective implementation of INPI's valuation service and the promised calculators; (2) coordination with the Central Bank, CMN, and banks to recognize IP assets as collateral — strategy 6.5 of the plan; and (3) the movement of development funds and banks toward credit lines backed by intangibles.

For the average Brazilian company, the realistic path is not "handing the patent to the bank," but turning the patent into contractual revenue and using that revenue as collateral. The Strategic Plan 2027-2036 signals that INPI wants to build the infrastructure — valuation, certification, regulation — so that this path ceases to be the exception. Companies that already have recorded agreements and documented valuation will be ahead when IP-backed credit becomes a reality.
