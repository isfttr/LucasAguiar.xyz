The global investment in intangible assets — software, data, brands, R&D, and organizational capital — surpassed US$ 10 trillion for the first time in 2025, growing more than three times faster than investment in tangible assets, according to [WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/pressroom/en/articles/2026/article_0011.html). Brazil is the 7th largest economy in this ranking, with US$ 312 billion invested in intangibles in 2023 (7.6% of GDP), according to an INPI report in partnership with WIPO and the Luiss Business School.

The Brazilian paradox, however, is well known to those who deal with industrial property: a company's most valuable intangible asset is, in practice, one of the hardest to turn into credit. Banks accept real estate, machinery, and receivables as collateral, but rarely patents or trademarks. It was to change this scenario that the [INPI Strategic Plan 2027-2036](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/governanca/planejamento-estrategico/plano-estrategico) elevated IP asset monetization to the status of a formal strategic objective, with a specific strategy to enable the use of IP as bank collateral.

This guide explains what Brazilian law already permits today, what INPI plans for the next ten years, and how a company can, in practice, begin to monetize its intellectual property assets.

## Why IP is hard to use as collateral

Before understanding the instruments, it is necessary to understand the credit market's resistance. Four characteristics of IP assets explain why traditional banks view them with caution:

| Characteristic | Problem for the lender |
|----------------|------------------------|
| **Subjective valuation** | There is no liquid, transparent market that prices patents and trademarks the way there is for real estate |
| **Risk of invalidation** | A patent can be annulled in a nullity proceeding; a trademark can lapse for non-use |
| **Periodic obligations** | Unpaid annuities and fees silently extinguish the right |
| **Complex enforcement** | In the event of default, the bank would have practical difficulty selling or exploiting the asset |

Therefore, in Brazil, IP rarely works as primary collateral. The realistic path involves structuring the asset so that it generates receivables — royalties — and using those receivables as backing for credit operations, complemented by the annotation of liens at INPI.

## What the law already allows: the legal basis in the LPI

Law 9.279/1996 (LPI) already offers the essential monetization instruments — what is missing is the culture of use. The main ones:

**Patents**
- **Assignment** — the application or patent may be assigned, in whole or in part (art. 58). INPI records the assignment and any limitation or lien on the application or patent (art. 59), and the record produces effects against third parties from publication (art. 60).
- **Voluntary license** — the owner may enter into a license agreement for exploitation (art. 61), and the agreement must be recorded with INPI to produce effects against third parties (art. 62). In the absence of agreement on remuneration, the parties may request INPI to arbitrate (art. 65).

**Trademarks**
- **Assignment and liens** — INPI records the assignment, limitations, and liens on the application or registration (art. 136), with effect against third parties from publication (art. 137).
- **License of use** — the owner may license the trademark while maintaining control over specifications and quality (art. 139), with mandatory recording for effects against third parties (art. 140).

**Technology transfer**
- Contracts involving technology transfer, franchising, and similar must be registered with INPI to produce effects against third parties, with a decision within 30 days (art. 211).

The practical point: the [LPI](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm) does not expressly regulate the pledge of IP rights, but arts. 59, II, and 136, II, provide for the annotation of "any limitation or lien" — the legal gateway for recording with INPI guarantees constituted over the asset. In market practice, the most commonly used structure is the **fiduciary assignment of royalty receivables**: the recorded license agreement generates a predictable payment flow, and that flow is what the bank is able to evaluate and pledge.

Contract registrations and recordings are done through INPI's **e-Contratos** system, which underwent a new version of its forms in August 2026 — worth checking the [official service page](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/contratos-de-tecnologia-e-de-franquia) before filing.

## What INPI plans: Strategic Objective 6

The [2027-2036 Strategic Plan]({{< relref "posts/inpi-plano-estrategico-2027-2036-guia-completo/" >}}) dedicates Strategic Objective 6 to monetization: "encouraging the monetization of Intellectual Property rights for the strategic use of intangible assets". Five strategies deserve attention from companies and financial institutions:

1. **Technical advisory in technology transfer** (6.1) — modernization of recording and registration processes, with new services to support the economic use of IP and improvement of the interactive contract data panel.
2. **IP asset valuation service** (6.2) — INPI wants to become a market benchmark in valuation, with guidelines, governance model, and practical tools such as valuation calculators.
3. **Studies, observatories, and indicators** (6.3) — economic analyses to support public policies and decisions by economic agents.
4. **Monetization ecosystem** (6.4) — valuation methodologies and tools, training and certification of appraisers, and market awareness.
5. **IP as bank collateral** (6.5) — coordination with financial regulators, banks, and the credit system to create regulatory mechanisms and incentives that enable the recognition of IP assets as collateral in banking operations.

If fulfilled, these strategies target exactly the four barriers in the table above: standardized valuation, risk indicators, market education, and regulatory alignment with the Central Bank and the CMN. Coordination with the financial system, however, depends on long-term stamina — the plan has a ten-year term and the implementation horizon is years, not months.

## GIPI and the IP and Financing Working Group

Strategy 6.5 does not operate in a vacuum: the main interministerial IP policy forum has already begun to execute the credit access agenda. The [Interministerial Group on Intellectual Property (GIPI)](https://www.gov.br/mdic/pt-br/assuntos/propriedade-intelectual), chaired by MDIC through the Secretariat of Competitiveness and Regulatory Policy and composed of 13 ministries, INPI, and representatives of civil society, is the body responsible for promoting and implementing the [National Intellectual Property Strategy (ENPI)](https://www.gov.br/mdic/pt-br/assuntos/propriedade-intelectual), in force until 2030.

In April 2026, GIPI approved the creation of the **Working Group on Intellectual Property and Financing**, with an explicit mandate to map, formulate, propose, and coordinate initiatives to structure and enable the use of intellectual property as an instrument of access to financing — the institutional translation, at interministerial scale, of strategy 6.5 of INPI's Strategic Plan, according to [coverage of the meeting](https://jornalismorondonia.com.br/acoes-para-promocao-da-propriedade-intelectual-sao-destaque-em-reuniao-do-gipi/) (the original source is MDIC). At the same meeting, the balance of the first year of GIPI's 2025-2027 Action Plan listed deliverables directly related to monetization: specialized mentoring in the management and commercialization of IP assets, valuation initiatives and economic use of IP assets — including access to credit — and strengthening technology transfer between ICTs and the productive sector. The secretariat's guidance was to complete the most advanced deliverables still in the first half of 2026.

Two developments on the same front are worth noting for those following the topic:

- **Reinventa.BR** — open innovation acceleration program that brings mid-sized and large industries closer to ICTs, integrated with ENPI, with nationwide expansion planned for the 2027-2028 period.
- **Registration and measurement of ICT assets** — MDIC and the Office of the Comptroller General (CGU) began working together to strengthen the registration and measurement of IP assets produced by Scientific, Technological, and Innovation Institutions, with a joint event held in May 2026. It is an accounting prerequisite for public intangibles to enter the balance sheet and, in the future, back operations.

## Practical step-by-step guide to monetizing IP in Brazil

While the ecosystem matures, a company that wants to use IP as a financial asset can start today:

**1. Regularize the asset.** The starting point is having the application/registration in good standing with INPI, with annuities and fees up to date — an extinguished right is not worth anything as an asset. Track the status through the [guide to checking case status at INPI]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}}).

**2. Value the asset.** Hire a valuation using a recognized method: replacement cost, market comparison, or discounted cash flow (income). For trademarks, the income method considers the royalty a third party would pay to use the trademark; for patents, one projects the royalty stream or the cost savings generated by exclusivity.

**3. Formalize agreements and record them with INPI.** License of use, assignment, or technology transfer must be registered in e-Contratos to produce effects against third parties. It is the recorded agreement that turns the asset into a verifiable revenue stream — and it is what the bank can analyze.

**4. Use the receivables as backing.** With the recorded license agreement generating predictable royalties, it is possible to structure a fiduciary assignment of receivables and present the flow as ancillary collateral in credit operations, complementing traditional guarantees.

**5. Monitor regulatory milestones.** Track the implementation of INPI's valuation service, the deliverables of GIPI's Working Group on Intellectual Property and Financing (created in April 2026 to structure the use of IP as an instrument of access to credit), and any bills on intangible collateral — the discussions on [improving the LPI]({{< relref "posts/lpi-30-anos-propostas-aperfeicoamento-inpi/" >}}) may bring express provisions for these instruments.

## What to watch for

IP monetization in Brazil has four signs of maturation to follow: (1) the effective implementation of INPI's valuation service and the promised calculators; (2) the first deliverables of GIPI's Working Group on Intellectual Property and Financing — what type of instrument will be proposed and which financial institutions will adopt it; (3) coordination with the Central Bank, CMN, and banks to recognize IP assets as collateral — strategy 6.5 of the plan; and (4) the movement of funds and development banks toward credit lines backed by intangibles.

For the average Brazilian company, the realistic path is not "giving the patent to the bank," but turning the patent into contractual revenue and using that revenue as collateral. The 2027-2036 Strategic Plan signals that INPI wants to build the infrastructure — valuation, certification, regulation — so that this path ceases to be the exception. Companies that already have recorded contracts and documented valuation will be ahead when IP-backed credit becomes a reality.
