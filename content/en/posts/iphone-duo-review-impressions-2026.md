The iPhone Duo, Apple's first foldable, launches on October 23, starting at $1,999. The first full reviews landed on 10/21, and the consensus is clear: it impresses as a foldable — but most buyers should still wait.

## What the iPhone Duo does well

The inner display is the main selling point. The matte finish reduces reflections and masks the crease, which is still visible at certain angles and lighting — it is not "crease-free." It is a large screen that fits in a pocket, and for people who actually use that space every day, it changes real tasks: reading, working across two apps, spreadsheets and video.

The hinge earns nearly universal praise (MKBHD, Dave2D and CNET all highlight it). If you want iOS in a foldable form factor, this is the most complete pitch to date.

## Where it falls short

- **Apps are the biggest uncertainty.** The experience depends on each app's adaptation; many still show small interfaces and wasted space instead of using the inner display.
- **It's the most expensive iPhone without the best camera kit.** It lacks the telephoto lens and Pro-series features, though the form factor enables interesting shooting angles.
- **Battery and ergonomics split opinions.** Reports range from satisfactory to problematic — autonomy isn't a safe bet.
- **Long-term durability is still open.** The 32,000+ folding cycles reported in testing don't reproduce years of daily use.

## Verdict

The enthusiastic reviews (CNET, Tom's Guide) acknowledge the compromises; the more critical ones (The Verge, WSJ) prefer to wait a generation or two of refinement.

The most defensible recommendation: **buy it if you plan to use the inner display daily and can justify the $1,999.** If cameras, comfort, or just messaging and social media are your priority, waiting is the safer call — this first generation is for enthusiasts willing to take a launch-year risk.
