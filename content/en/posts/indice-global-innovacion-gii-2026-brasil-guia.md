Todos os anos, a Organização Mundial da Propriedade Intelectual (OMPI) publica o Índice Global de Inovação (GII). Este índice é a referência que governos, bancos de desenvolvimento e empresas usam para medir o desempenho inovador de quase 140 economias. Embora muitas vezes seja lido como um simples ranking de países, o GII é um instrumento muito mais completo. Ele combina mais de 80 indicadores, desde o investimento em P&D até o comércio de alta tecnologia e o licenciamento de propriedade intelectual, para explicar o que impulsiona a inovação em cada país.

Para quem trabalha com propriedade industrial, patentes e inovação, entender como o índice é construído importa em dobro. A propriedade intelectual não é um dado isolado: ela é, ao mesmo tempo, uma das entradas que o GII mede e um dos resultados que ele reflete. Este guia explica o que é o GII, como ele é calculado e o que a edição 2026 diz sobre o Brasil.

## What is the Global Innovation Index

Launched in 2007, the GII has become the benchmark for comparing innovation ecosystems. It is not limited to patents or scientific publications: it assesses how well a country converts knowledge into economic and social value.

The index covers approximately 140 economies and is built from indicators grouped into two blocks:

- **Inputs (inputs)** — the resources and conditions that enable innovation: institutions, infrastructure, human capital, and market and business sophistication.
- **Outputs (outputs)** — what is effectively delivered: knowledge and technology output (where patents, scientific articles, and licenses reside) and creative output.

The relationship between the two produces the **innovation efficiency** indicator, which compares whether a country yields more than its level of development and inputs would predict. Economies that consistently surpass this threshold are called *overperformers* (outperformers).

## How it is calculated and why IP matters

Among the output indicators, intellectual property is central. The GII uses variables such as:

- patent applications per capita,
- number of registered trademarks,
- indexed scientific articles,
- IP licenses and royalties,
- value of high and medium-high technology production.

Thus, the index does not treat IP as an isolated legal formality: it treats it as a **thermometer of real innovative activity**. A country that patents little or licenses little IP tends to score lower in knowledge production, even with good infrastructure. For IP lawyers and consultants, this is a natural entry point: the index figures directly reflect the volume and quality of registered intangible assets.

No new economy entered the top 15 in 2026; the United Arab Emirates (25th) climbed five positions. Among those that have risen the most in the last decade are China, India, Vietnam, Türkiye, the Philippines, Morocco, and Indonesia, with **Brazil, Mauritius, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar on upward trajectories**.

**Report macro figures**

- Global R&D spending grew by over 3.3% in real terms in 2025 and is projected to reach **US$ 3.4 trillion** in 2026, increasingly concentrated in middle-income economies.
- Corporate R&D grew by 5.8% in real terms in 2025, to a record **US$ 1.5 trillion**, driven by software, AI, and defense.
- Venture capital grew by 28% to **US$ 510 billion** in 2025, the largest annual advance since 2021. But investment is increasingly concentrated: **AI accounted for 53% of the value of VC operations in 2025 and 77% in the first half of 2026**.
- Deep science startups are collectively worth **US$ 7.6 trillion** (over 30,000 companies mapped since 2000), 23% more than in 2024.

## Brazil in GII 2026: the only overperformer in the region

For Brazil, the 2026 edition offers two readings:

1. **Confirmed upward trajectory.** The communiqué places Brazil, along with Mauritius, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar, among the economies with sustained improvements in innovative performance.
2. **Only overperformer in Latin America.** Brazil remains the only economy in the region that performs above what its level of development would predict. This does not mean leading the absolute ranking; it means converting inputs into outputs more efficiently than the average for its income group.

In deep science, the report highlights that growth since 2020 has been faster in Central and Southern Asia (118%), Latin America and the Caribbean (71%), and Sub-Saharan Africa (65%), with **Türkiye, India, Malaysia, Brazil, and South Africa** leading the middle-income group.

## Why this matters for IP practice

- **IP is an innovation indicator.** The index's patent and licensing metrics confirm that protecting and managing intangible assets drives measured competitiveness. It's not just legal compliance; it's industrial policy.
- **Brazil monetizes what it already has.** Its overperformer position suggests that the problem is not a scarcity of resources, but the underutilization of existing ones. Improving the ability to patent, license, and negotiate IP — from research institutions, universities, and SMEs — is an efficiency lever.
- **AI is redesigning the patent map.** With 77% of first-half venture capital concentrated in AI and generative AI patenting nearly tripling in two years, according to WIPO data, those who build the capacity to register and protect AI assets today will be better positioned when the field matures.

## Conclusion

The Global Innovation Index is neither an award nor fleeting news: it is an annual — and cumulative — snapshot of how economies convert knowledge into value. For Brazil, the 2026 figure is encouraging but demanding: it maintains the country as a regional overperformer and an ascending economy, in a context where AI concentrates global investment and deep science creates new industries at high speed.

For the IP professional, the operational conclusion is immediate: intellectual property ceases to be an isolated legal topic and becomes a central indicator of competitiveness. Strengthening the management of patents, trademarks, and licenses is not bureaucracy — it is the most direct way to improve the country's (and its clients') position in next year's index.
