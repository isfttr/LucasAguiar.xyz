A new soybean variety, a coffee cultivar more resistant to drought, or a forage grass with higher nutritional value: in Brazil, the legal protection of these plants **does not go through the INPI** and **is not a patent**. It is a system of its own, plant variety protection, regulated by Law No. 9,456/1997 (Plant Variety Protection Act — PVPA) and administered by the National Plant Variety Protection Service (SNPC), linked to the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock (MAPA).

The confusion between cultivar and patent is one of the most frequent in the daily life of those who work with innovation in agribusiness — and it is costly: an application filed at the wrong office, an incomplete protection strategy, and, in the worst case, loss of the right due to disclosure before filing. This guide covers the legal basis, the requirements, the step-by-step registration process, and the rights (and limits) of those who protect a cultivar in Brazil.

## Cultivar is not a patent: the Brazilian sui generis system

The starting point is the Industrial Property Law (LPI). Article 18, III, of the LPI (Law 9,279/96) excludes from patentability "all or part of living beings," with a single exception: transgenic microorganisms that meet the requirements of novelty, inventive step, and industrial application — and, even in these cases, the sole paragraph of the provision expressly excludes "all or part of plants or animals."

In other words: **a plant variety as such cannot be patented in Brazil**. Those who obtain a new cultivar protect themselves through the plant variety protection certificate, which the PVPA (art. 2) defines as the "only form of protection of cultivars and of right that may prevent the free use of plants or of their reproductive or vegetative multiplication parts in the country." The certificate is considered movable property for all legal purposes.

| Aspect | Patent (LPI) | Plant variety protection (PVPA) |
|---------|---------------|----------------------------|
| Subject matter | Invention or utility model | Plant variety (cultivar) |
| Legal basis | Law 9,279/96 | Law 9,456/97 |
| Authority | INPI | SNPC/MAPA |
| Requirements | Novelty, inventive step, industrial application | Novelty, distinctness, uniformity, stability (DUS) |
| Term | 20 years (invention) / 15 years (utility model) | 15 years, or 18 for vines, fruit trees, forest trees, and ornamentals |
| Examination | Technical at INPI | DUS descriptors analyzed by SNPC |

Note that the examination of patents for biotechnological processes — for example, a genetic transformation method — remains with the INPI, but the **product** (the transgenic plant itself) is protected as a cultivar, and the **genetic construct** may be the subject of a transgenic microorganism patent within the limits of art. 18, III, of the LPI. In practice, large agricultural biotechnology companies combine both systems. The [Budapest Treaty]({{< relref "posts/brasil-tratado-budapeste-patentes-biotecnologia/" >}}) enters this equation when there is a deposit of biological material for patent purposes.

## The legal basis: Law 9,456/97 and UPOV

The PVPA implements in Brazil the International Convention for the Protection of New Varieties of Plants (UPOV), in the 1978 version, promulgated by [Decree No. 3,109/1999](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/decreto/D3109.htm). Brazil has been a member of [UPOV](https://www.upov.int/members/en/) since 1999, which guarantees Brazilian breeders reciprocity of treatment in other signatory countries.

The law defines cultivar (art. 3, IV) as the variety of any genus or higher plant species that is:
- clearly distinguishable from other known cultivars by a minimum set of descriptors;
- homogeneous and stable with respect to the descriptors through successive generations;
- of a species capable of use by the agroforestry complex;
- described in a specialized publication available and accessible to the public.

Also protectable are **hybrid component lines**. And there is the concept of **essentially derived cultivar** — one derived from another protected cultivar without essential differences in the characteristics resulting from the genotype —, which cannot be exploited without authorization from the holder of the initial cultivar, even when the derivation involves further breeding.

## Protection requirements: novelty and DUS

Two sets of requirements must be met:

1. **Commercial novelty (art. 3, V):** the cultivar cannot have been offered for sale in Brazil for more than 12 months prior to the filing date (with specific rules for commercialization abroad).
2. **DUS:** distinctness, uniformity, and stability — assessed by morphological, physiological, biochemical, or molecular descriptors that are genetically inherited (art. 3, II).

The analysis of descriptors is the heart of the examination at the SNPC: it is what defines whether the cultivar is truly "new" in relation to the bank of known cultivars and whether the characteristics remain stable over generations.

## Step-by-step: registration with SNPC/MAPA

Cultivar registration is not done at the INPI, but rather at the SNPC, linked to MAPA. The basic flow, provided for in arts. 13 to 22 of the PVPA:

1. **Application (arts. 13-14):** request signed by the breeder (or by an attorney domiciled in Brazil, in the case of holders abroad), referring to **a single cultivar**, containing botanical species, cultivar name, genetic origin, and descriptive report.
2. **Publication (art. 16):** the application is published as an extract within up to 60 calendar days.
3. **Opposition (art. 16, sole paragraph):** third parties have 90 days to oppose.
4. **Examination of descriptors (arts. 17-22):** the SNPC verifies formal compliance and analyzes the DUS descriptors; they cannot be modified after publication, except to correct errors or to meet requirements.
5. **Provisional Certificate of Protection:** granted at the initial stage, ensures protection from the publication of the application (the term of validity counts from the grant of the provisional certificate — art. 11).
6. **Final Certificate of Protection:** after the examination is approved, consolidates the right.

An important practical detail: when the development results from an employment contract or service provision, the application **must include the names of all breeders** who participated (art. 5, § 3) — the same care with ownership that applies to employee inventions under the LPI. And, if the development was cooperative, protection may be requested jointly or individually, with the qualification of each co-breeder (art. 5, § 2).

## What the certificate grants — and what it does not cover

The certificate holder has the exclusive right to commercially reproduce, offer for sale, import, export, and store propagating material in Brazil. But the PVPA preserves important balances, which confuse those coming from the patent regime:

- **Reservation for own use (art. 10, I):** farmers may reserve and plant seeds for their own use, without paying royalties.
- **Use as food/raw material (art. 10, II):** the product of the planting may be used or sold as food, provided it is not for reproductive purposes.
- **Research and breeding (art. 10, III):** the cultivar may be used as a source of genetic variation.
- **Small farmers and family farming (art. 10, IV and V):** multiplication of seeds for donation or exchange among small farmers and, within the scope of art. 19 of Law 10,696/2003, by family farmers covered by Law 11,326/2006.

The term of protection is **15 years** from the grant of the Provisional Certificate, and **18 years** for vines, fruit trees, forest trees, and ornamentals, including rootstocks (art. 11). After the term expires, the cultivar falls into the **public domain** and no other right may prevent its free use (art. 12).

The certificate is also a tradable asset: ownership may be transferred by inter vivos act or succession, and licenses, amendments, and other acts must be recorded on the certificate (arts. 23-24) — a process analogous to the registration of technology transfer contracts at the INPI, but with the recordal made before the SNPC.

## Enforcement: what happens to those who use without authorization

Article 37 of the PVPA is the backbone of enforcement: anyone who sells, offers for sale, reproduces, imports, exports, packages, or stores propagating material of a protected cultivar without authorization from the holder is obliged to **pay damages**, in addition to having the material **seized** and paying a **fine of 20% of the commercial value** of the seized material — without prejudice to the characterization of the crime of infringement of breeder's rights. In case of repeat offense, the fine percentage is doubled.

In practice, the protection strategy in agribusiness combines: (i) a cultivar certificate at the SNPC for the variety, (ii) a patent for the process/technology when applicable, (iii) well-recorded licensing contracts, and (iv) monitoring of the seed market — a problem that also affects the fight against trademark counterfeiting in retail and digital platforms.

## What to note

- **Register with the right office:** cultivar is SNPC/MAPA; patent is INPI. Choosing the wrong counter delays months.
- **Do not disclose before filing:** the commercial novelty window is 12 months of offering for sale in Brazil; plan scientific publication or commercial launch after filing.
- **Name all breeders** in the application (art. 5, § 3) to avoid ownership disputes later.
- **Record licenses and transfers** on the certificate to give publicity and opposability to third parties.
- **Essentially derived cultivars** still depend on authorization from the holder of the initial cultivar.

The plant variety protection system is the main instrument for appropriating innovation in Brazilian agribusiness — one of the sectors in which the country invests the most in genetic improvement. Understanding the difference between this regime and the patent regime is the first step toward a complete IP strategy, which can be combined with other forms of protection such as [geographical indications]({{< relref "posts/guia-indicacoes-geograficas-inpi-brasil/" >}}) for agri-food products.
