A new soybean variety, a coffee cultivar more resistant to drought, or a forage grass with higher nutritional value: in Brazil, the legal protection of these plants **does not go through INPI** and **is not a patent**. It is a sui generis system—plant variety protection—regulated by Law No. 9.456/1997 (Plant Variety Protection Law — LPC) and administered by the National Plant Variety Protection Service (SNPC), linked to the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock (MAPA).

The confusion between cultivar and patent is one of the most frequent in the daily life of those who work with innovation in agribusiness—and it costs dearly: an application filed at the wrong office, an incomplete protection strategy, and, in the worst scenario, loss of the right due to disclosure before filing. This guide covers the legal basis, the requirements, the step-by-step registration process, and the rights (and limits) of those who protect a cultivar in Brazil.

## Cultivar is not a patent: the Brazilian sui generis system

The starting point is the Industrial Property Law. Article 18, III, of the LPI (Law 9.279/96) excludes from patentability "all or part of living beings," with a single exception: transgenic microorganisms that meet the requirements of novelty, inventive step, and industrial application—and even in those cases, the sole paragraph of the provision expressly excludes "all or part of plants or animals."

In other words: **a plant variety as such cannot be patented in Brazil**. Those who obtain a new cultivar protect themselves through the plant variety protection certificate, which the LPC (Article 2) defines as the "only form of protection of cultivars and the right that may prevent the free use of plants or their parts of reproduction or vegetative multiplication in the Country." The certificate is considered movable property for all legal purposes.

| Aspect | Patent (LPI) | Plant variety protection (LPC) |
|---------|---------------|----------------------------|
| Subject matter | Invention or utility model | Plant variety (cultivar) |
| Legal basis | Law 9.279/96 | Law 9.456/97 |
| Office | INPI | SNPC/MAPA |
| Requirements | Novelty, inventive step, industrial application | Novelty, distinctness, uniformity, stability (DUS) |
| Term | 20 years (invention) / 15 years (utility model) | 15 years, or 18 for grapevines, fruit trees, forest trees, and ornamentals |
| Examination | Technical at INPI | DUS descriptors analyzed by SNPC |

Note that patent examination of biotechnological processes—for example, a genetic transformation method—remains at INPI, but the **product** (the transgenic plant itself) is protected as a cultivar, and the **genetic construct** can be the subject of a transgenic microorganism patent within the limits of Article 18, III, of the LPI. In practice, large agricultural biotechnology companies combine the two systems. The [Budapest Treaty]({{< relref "posts/brasil-tratado-budapeste-patentes-biotecnologia/" >}}) comes into the equation when there is a deposit of biological material for patent purposes.

## The legal basis: Law 9.456/97 and UPOV

The LPC implements in Brazil the International Convention for the Protection of New Varieties of Plants (UPOV), in its 1978 version, promulgated by [Decree No. 3.109/1999](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/decreto/D3109.htm). Brazil has been a member of [UPOV](https://www.upov.int/members/en/) since 1999, which guarantees Brazilian breeders reciprocal treatment in other signatory countries.

The law defines cultivar (Article 3, IV) as the variety of any genus or higher plant species that is:
- clearly distinguishable from other known cultivars by a minimum margin of descriptors;
- uniform and stable with respect to the descriptors through successive generations;
- of a species capable of use by the agricultural-forestry complex;
- described in a specialized publication available and accessible to the public.

Also protectable are **component lines of hybrids**. And there is the concept of the **essentially derived cultivar**—one derived from another protected cultivar without essential differences in the characteristics resulting from the genotype—which cannot be exploited without authorization from the holder of the initial cultivar, even when the derivation involves breeding.

## Protection requirements: novelty and DUS

Two blocks of requirements must be met:

1. **Commercial novelty (Article 3, V):** the cultivar cannot have been offered for sale in Brazil for more than 12 months prior to the filing date (with specific rules for commercialization abroad).
2. **DUS:** distinctness, uniformity, and stability—assessed by morphological, physiological, biochemical, or molecular descriptors that are genetically inherited (Article 3, II).

The analysis of descriptors is the heart of the examination at SNPC: it defines whether the cultivar is truly "new" compared to the bank of known cultivars and whether the characteristics are maintained over generations.

## Step-by-step registration at SNPC/MAPA

Cultivar registration is not done at INPI, but at SNPC, linked to MAPA. The basic flow, provided for in Articles 13 to 22 of the LPC:

1. **Application (Articles 13-14):** a request signed by the breeder (or an attorney domiciled in Brazil, in the case of holders abroad), referring to a **single cultivar**, containing the botanical species, cultivar name, genetic origin, and a descriptive report.
2. **Publication (Article 16):** the application is published in extract form within 60 calendar days.
3. **Opposition (Article 16, sole paragraph):** third parties have 90 days to file an opposition.
4. **Examination of descriptors (Articles 17-22):** SNPC verifies formal compliance and analyzes the DUS descriptors; they cannot be modified after publication, except to correct errors or to meet requirements.
5. **Provisional Protection Certificate:** granted at the initial stage, it ensures protection from the publication of the application (the term of validity runs from the grant of the provisional certificate—Article 11).
6. **Final Protection Certificate:** after the examination is approved, it consolidates the right.

An important practical detail: when the cultivar is obtained through an employment contract or service provision, the application **must include the names of all breeders** who participated (Article 5, §3)—the same care with ownership that applies to employee inventions under the LPI. And, if the development was carried out jointly, protection may be requested jointly or individually, with the identification of each co-breeder (Article 5, §2).

## What the certificate grants—and what it does not cover

The certificate holder has the exclusive right to commercial reproduction, offering for sale, importation, exportation, and storage of propagation material in Brazil. But the LPC preserves important balances, which confuse those coming from the patent regime:

- **Reservation for own use (Article 10, I):** farmers may reserve and plant seeds for their own use, without paying royalties.
- **Use as food/raw material (Article 10, II):** the product of the planting may be used or sold as food, provided it is not for reproductive purposes.
- **Research and breeding (Article 10, III):** the cultivar may be used as a source of genetic variation.
- **Small producers and family farming (Article 10, IV and V):** multiplication of seeds for donation or exchange among small producers and, under Article 19 of Law 10.696/2003, by family farmers classified under Law 11.326/2006.

The term of protection is **15 years** from the grant of the Provisional Certificate, and **18 years** for grapevines, fruit trees, forest trees, and ornamentals, including rootstocks (Article 11). Once the term has expired, the cultivar falls into the **public domain** and no other right may prevent its free use (Article 12).

The certificate is also a tradable asset: ownership may be transferred by inter vivos act or succession, and licenses, amendments, and other acts must be recorded in the certificate (Articles 23-24)—a process analogous to the registration of technology transfer agreements at INPI, but with the recordal made before SNPC.

## Enforcement: what happens to those who use without authorization

Article 37 of the LPC is the backbone of enforcement: whoever sells, offers for sale, reproduces, imports, exports, packages, or stores propagation material of a protected cultivar without the holder's authorization is obliged to **pay damages**, in addition to having the material **seized** and paying a **fine of 20% of the commercial value** of the seized material—without prejudice to the characterization of the crime of infringement of the breeder's rights. In the case of repeat offenses, the fine percentage is doubled.

In practice, the protection strategy in agribusiness combines: (i) a cultivar certificate at SNPC for the variety, (ii) a patent for the process/technology when applicable, (iii) well-recorded licensing agreements, and (iv) monitoring of the seed market—a problem that also affects the fight against trademark counterfeiting in retail and on digital platforms.

## What to note

- **Registration at the right office:** cultivar is SNPC/MAPA; patent is INPI. Filing at the wrong counter delays months.
- **Do not disclose before filing:** the commercial novelty window is 12 months of offering for sale in Brazil; plan scientific publication or commercial launch after filing.
- **Name all breeders** in the application (Article 5, §3) to avoid ownership disputes later.
- **Record licenses and transfers** in the certificate to provide publicity and opposability to third parties.
- **Essentially derived cultivars** remain dependent on authorization from the holder of the initial cultivar.

The plant variety protection system is the main instrument for appropriating innovation in Brazilian agribusiness—one of the sectors in which the country invests the most in genetic improvement. Understanding the difference between this regime and the patent regime is the first step toward a complete IP strategy, which can be combined with other forms of protection such as [geographical indications]({{< relref "posts/guia-indicacoes-geograficas-inpi-brasil/" >}}) for agri-food products.
