Associations, cooperatives, and unions can register a trademark — but not just any trademark. The Industrial Property Law reserves two specific types for these cases: the **collective trademark** and the **certification trademark**. Each has its own standing and mandatory documents that an ordinary trademark does not require; confusing the two is the most frequent cause of rejection.

This guide gathers what the LPI (Law No. 9,279/1996), the [INPI Trademark Manual](https://manualdemarcas.inpi.gov.br/), and the current fee schedule say about who can register, what must be filed, within what deadline, how much it costs, and which situations lead to termination of the registration.

## What Is a Collective Trademark and What Is a Certification Trademark

The legal definition is short and resolves most doubts (art. 123 of the LPI):

| Type | Legal definition | What the sign communicates |
|------|------------------|----------------------------|
| **Product or service trademark** | Distinguishes a product or service from an identical, similar, or related one from a different origin (item I) | "This product comes from a specific company" |
| **Certification trademark** | Used to **attest conformity** of a product or service with technical standards or specifications, as to quality, nature, material used, and methodology employed (item II) | "This product meets a verifiable technical standard" |
| **Collective trademark** | Used to **identify products or services originating from members** of a given entity (item III) | "This product comes from one of the members of this entity" |

The difference in function is structural. A collective trademark is an umbrella: each member uses the same sign, and the entity administers that use. A certification trademark is a technical seal: its holder does not produce or sell the certified product — it verifies whether third parties comply with the standard.

Neither replaces the individual trademark: a producer may have its own trademark and use the association's collective trademark, or receive the seal of an independent certification body.

## Collective Trademark, Certification, and Geographical Indication

The three legal instruments often appear together in value-adding projects, and the wrong choice is common. A [geographical indication]({{< relref "posts/guia-indicacoes-geograficas-inpi-brasil/" >}}) protects geographical origin and depends on reputation and a link to the territory; the collective trademark and the certification trademark do not require a territory, only the correct legal structure.

| Characteristic | Geographical Indication | Collective Trademark | Certification Trademark |
|----------------|-------------------------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| Protected subject matter | Geographical origin linked to reputation or quality | Identification of products/services of the members of an entity | Conformity with technical standards or specifications |
| Holder | Entity representing the producers of the area | Legal entity representing the collective | Person without direct commercial or industrial interest in the product or service |
| Requires regulations? | Yes (with the specifications book) | Yes — regulations of use | No; requires technical documentation |
| Term | Indefinite | 10 years, renewable | 10 years, renewable |
| Legal basis | Arts. 176 to 182 of the LPI | Art. 123, III, and arts. 147 to 154 | Art. 123, II, and arts. 148 to 154 |

It is worth remembering that a GI is an obstacle to trademark registration (art. 124, IX).

## Who Can Apply for Registration

Here is the point that defeats the most applications — and the rule differs for each type.

**Collective trademark (art. 128, § 2).** Only a **legal entity representing a collective** may apply — the Trademark Manual cites associations, cooperatives, unions, federations, confederations, and consortia. The entity may carry out an activity different from that of its members, provided it is compatible: INPI examines the corporate purpose or articles of association to verify whether the applicant in fact brings together associates, and elements such as membership dues, affiliation conditions, and periodic meetings reinforce standing.

**Certification trademark (art. 128, § 3).** Only a **natural or legal person without direct commercial or industrial interest** in the attested product or service may apply. The examination verifies whether the applicant's activity characterizes it as an economic agent involved in the production, commercialization, or supply of what the sign intends to certify. If direct interest is characterized, the application is rejected.

The [AGU/PGF/PFE/INPI/COOPI Opinion No. 33/2016](https://manualdemarcas.inpi.gov.br/projects/manual/wiki/5%C2%B705_An%C3%A1lise_da_legitimidade_do_requerente), incorporated into the Manual, goes further: **associations composed of economic agents involved in the production, supply, or commercialization of the products to be certified do not have standing to apply for a certification trademark**, nor do unions and organizations that represent the interests of those agents.

In practical terms: a producers' cooperative cannot create a "quality seal" to certify its own product. If the goal is to identify the associates' production, the path is the collective trademark. If the goal is to attest technical conformity, an independent certification entity must be formed or hired.

## Mandatory Documents: What Changes Compared with an Ordinary Trademark

### Collective Trademark: Regulations of Use

The collective trademark application must be accompanied by the **regulations of use**, which set out the conditions and prohibitions for use of the trademark (art. 147 of the LPI). If it does not accompany the filing, the regulations must be filed within **60 days** counted from the filing — under penalty of **definitive shelving** of the application.

Under Chapter XIV of INPI/PR Ordinance No. 8/2022, the regulations must contain, at minimum:

1. **Description of the applicant entity:** qualification, purpose, address, and the persons authorized to represent it;
2. **Conditions for withdrawal or renunciation** (partial or total), when not provided for in the articles of association or bylaws — a requirement linked to art. 152 of the LPI;
3. **Who may use the sign:** persons, natural or legal, authorized to use the trademark, and the requirements or conditions for doing so;
4. **Conditions of use:** how the sign is applied and other aspects of the marked product or service;
5. **Sanctions for inappropriate use.**

If the regulations refer to another document, that document must be attached. Subsequent amendments **must be communicated to INPI by petition**, describing the amended conditions, under penalty of not being considered (art. 149) — and an unmet requirement regarding the adequacy of the regulations leads to shelving of the application or of the amendment petition itself.

### Certification Trademark: Technical Documentation

A certification trademark does not have regulations of use; it has **technical documentation** (art. 148 of the LPI), detailed in Chapter XV of INPI/PR Ordinance No. 8/2022, with two blocks:

- **Subject matter of the certification:** characteristics of the product or service — quality, nature, material used, dimensions, components, technical conditions, manner of development or provision of the service, and other pertinent data;
- **Means to attest conformity and ensure control:** conformity assessment methodology and the sanctions applicable in case of noncompliance with the technical requirements.

In cases of a product or service subject to **compulsory certification**, a declaration of the reference documents in force (ordinances, resolutions, standards, regulations) pertinent to the subject matter of the certification is also required. The same deadline rule applies: the documentation accompanies the filing or is filed within 60 days, under penalty of shelving. Amendments may be communicated at any time, but **expansion of the scope** originally requested is not permitted.

In addition, the documents common to any trademark application apply: a power of attorney, when there is an attorney, and proof of the declared condition of lawful activity.

## Step by Step for Filing

1. **Define the nature of the sign.** Collective or certification — the choice defines standing, documents, and examination.
2. **Conduct a prior-art search** in the INPI database, following the [trademark search guide]({{< relref "posts/busca-anterioridade-marcas-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}). Note a detail unique to this type of sign: collective and certification trademarks already used and terminated **cannot be registered by third parties for 5 years** counted from termination (art. 154), and reproduction or imitation of a third party's collective or certification trademark is unregistrable (art. 124, XII).
3. **Issue and pay the GRU** in advance. Filing depends on the paid form number — the amounts are in the [trademark registration cost guide]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}).
4. **File through the e-Marcas system**, attaching the trademark image, regulations of use (collective) or technical documentation (certification), when already available.
5. **File the specific documents within 60 days** of the filing, if you did not do so at the time.
6. **Monitor the RPI.** The registrability examination is the same as for an ordinary trademark: distinctiveness, lawfulness, truthfulness, and availability. It is possible to receive office actions, oppositions, and appeals — the [step by step for monitoring the process]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}}) avoids missing deadlines, and the [total examination time]({{< relref "posts/quanto-tempo-demora-para-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}) follows the trademark queue.
7. **After approval**, grant and certificate issuance are automatic and without additional fee. The term is 10 years **counted from grant**, renewable — [trademark renewal]({{< relref "posts/prorrogacao-registro-marca-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}) follows the same windows as for an ordinary trademark.

## Costs

The official fee schedule (GM/MDIC Ordinance No. 110/2025 and INPI/PR Ordinance No. 10/2025) does not create a separate code for collective or certification trademarks: the same codes as for an ordinary trademark application apply, per class.

| Service | Code | Amount | With discount |
|---------|------|--------|----------------|
| Registration application — pre-approved specification | 389 | R$ 880.00 | R$ 440.00 |
| Registration application — free-form specification | 394 | R$ 1,720.00 | R$ 860.00 |
| First ten-year term + certificate | 372/373 | R$ 0.00 | R$ 0.00 |
| Renewal (ordinary term) | 374 | R$ 1,000.00 | R$ 500.00 |
| Appointment of attorney | 385 | R$ 90.00 | R$ 90.00 |

Under the current schedule, the 50% discount applies to natural persons (under conditions), microenterprises, MEIs, small businesses, simple innovation companies, ICTs, nonprofit entities, and public bodies — in joint ownership, only if all holders are entitled to the benefit. Associations classified as nonprofit entities are, as a rule, on this list; verification must be done case by case before issuing the GRU.

## Use, Control, and Cases of Termination

The use regime is more flexible than intuition suggests. Under art. 150 of the LPI, **use of the trademark does not depend on a license**: the authorization provided for in the regulations suffices. It is not necessary to record a license agreement with INPI for members to use the collective trademark.

The counterpart is that the registration depends on active governance. In addition to the common causes of termination, the collective trademark and the certification trademark are terminated when:

- the **entity ceases to exist** (art. 151, I); or
- the trademark is **used under conditions different from those provided for in the regulations** (art. 151, II).

**Renunciation** of a collective trademark registration is permitted only under the terms of the entity's articles of association or bylaws, or according to the regulations themselves (art. 152). And there is a specific rule that often goes unnoticed:

> **Forfeiture of the collective trademark (art. 153):** the registration will be declared lapsed if the trademark is **not used by more than one authorized person**.

In other words: it is not enough for one associate to use the trademark. If only one member uses it, the registration may be declared lapsed for lack of use — which requires systematic documentation of use by at least two authorized persons. Renewal, in turn, will not be granted if the holder ceases to meet the standing requirements of art. 128 (art. 133, § 3): an association that has lost its status as representative cannot renew the collective trademark.

After termination, the sign is blocked for third parties for 5 years (art. 154), to prevent reputation built collectively from being appropriated by a single agent.

## Mistakes That Lead to Rejection or Shelving

1. **A producers' association applying for a certification trademark** for its own associates' product — lack of standing (art. 128, § 3, and AGU Opinion No. 33/2016).
2. **Generic regulations**, without a list of authorized persons, conditions of use, and sanctions — generates an office action and, if not remedied, shelving.
3. **Missing the 60-day deadline** to file the regulations or technical documentation — definitive shelving.
4. **Amending the regulations without petitioning INPI** — the amendment is not considered (art. 149).
5. **Use concentrated in a single member** — risk of forfeiture (art. 153).
6. **Confusing a collective trademark with a geographical indication** — distinct legal instruments, holders, and requirements.
7. **Expanding the scope of the technical documentation** for a certification trademark after filing — not permitted.

## When to Choose Each Instrument

- **A group of producers, cooperative, association, union, or consortium** that wants to identify members' production: **collective trademark**.
- **An independent entity** that wants to attest technical conformity of third parties' products or services, without direct interest in the chain: **certification trademark**.
- **Producers of a region** whose reputation derives from the territory: **geographical indication**, possibly combined with a collective trademark.
- **A sole proprietorship** with its own product or service: **ordinary trademark**.

In all cases, the chosen sign must be distinctive — and may be blocked by similar third-party trademarks, including collective or certification trademarks registered or terminated less than 5 years ago.
