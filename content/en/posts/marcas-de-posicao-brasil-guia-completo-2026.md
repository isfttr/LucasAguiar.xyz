Position marks protect the **specific location** of an element applied to a product when that position, by itself, confers distinctiveness to the sign. In Brazil, this topic is at a turning point: after years of being decided case by case, the INPI opened a public consultation in 2026 to create its own regulatory framework — and the final text is expected to arrive within this semester. This guide explains what they are, how to register, and what to expect from the new rules.

> **Update (Aug/2026):** INPI Public Consultation No. 01/2026 on position marks closed on **July 24, 2026**. The INPI is consolidating contributions and will hold a **meeting with stakeholders on August 19, 2026** (2 PM to 5 PM, INPI auditorium, Rua Mayrink Veiga 9, Centro/RJ, with live broadcast via Zoom) to present the assessment of the new criteria for registrability and acquired distinctiveness. Responses to the submissions will be published together with the final texts of the normative act and the examination guidelines in the [Trademark Manual](http://manualdemarcas.inpi.gov.br/).

## What is a position mark?

A position mark is a trademark sign consisting of the **way an element is positioned** on a product — for example, the exact place where a stripe, an emblem, or a seam appears. What is protected is not the element itself, nor the design, nor the product's shape: it is the **position** occupied by that element.

Classic international examples:

- The **three Adidas stripes** on the side of footwear and apparel
- **Christian Louboutin's red sole**
- **Levi's back pocket stitching**

In Brazil, the recognition of position marks as an autonomous category of trademark sign has been debated for years. The INPI already examined applications of this nature based on administrative precedents, but without a clear regulatory framework — the gap that the current regulation seeks to fill.

## Position mark vs. 3D mark vs. figurative mark

The distinction is the point that raises the most doubts in practice — and it defines which protection strategy to adopt:

| Type of mark | What it protects | Example |
|---------------|---------------|---------|
| **Position mark** | The location of an element on the product | Stripes on the side of a sneaker |
| **3D mark** | The product's shape itself | Shape of the Coca-Cola bottle |
| **Figurative mark** | The design/logo, regardless of where it is applied | The Nike symbol applied anywhere |

The position mark is the most subtle of the three: it requires demonstrating that the **position itself** has acquired distinctive capacity — that is, that consumers identify the product's origin simply by seeing the element in that specific place.

## Legal basis: what the LPI says

The Industrial Property Law (Law No. 9,279/96) does not expressly list "position marks." Registration falls under **Art. 122**, which admits as a mark "visually perceptible distinctive signs, not comprised in the legal prohibitions."

Registrability challenges appear in the impediments of **Art. 124**:

- **Item III** — signs that constitute a necessary, common, or purely functional shape (the position cannot derive from a mandatory technical characteristic of the product)
- **Item VI** — a sign of a generic, necessary, or merely descriptive nature (the position must be distinctive, not obvious)

Therefore, the current regulatory discussion revolves around two concepts: **intrinsic distinctiveness** (the position identifies the origin by itself) and **acquired distinctiveness** (secondary meaning — the position has come to identify the origin through continuous use).

## What the INPI regulation proposes

INPI Public Consultation No. 01/2026, published on June 9, 2026 (DOU issue 105, section 1, p. 127, and RPI No. 2892), put two documents up for discussion:

| Document | Subject matter |
|-----------|--------|
| **Draft normative act** | Criteria for registrability, formal requirements, and grounds for unregistrability |
| **Draft examination guidelines** | Guidance for examiners, with examples and technical parameters, to be incorporated into the Trademark Manual |

Among the central points of the drafts:

- **Requirement of intrinsic distinctiveness** — the position must identify the origin of the product, and must not be merely functional or ornamental
- **Proof through use** — the possibility of proving acquired distinctiveness when the position is not intrinsically distinctive
- **Limitation to non-functional elements** — the position cannot result from a technical characteristic necessary for the product
- **Graphic representation** — requirement of visual elements that demonstrate the claimed position, such as dashed lines indicating the product's outline

The topic is not new at the INPI: there was already a specific public consultation on position marks in **2021** (April 13 to June 12, 2021), whose contributions were responded to by the Permanent Committee for the Improvement of Procedures and Examination Guidelines (CPAPD). The difference now is the design of a **formal normative act**, with binding guidelines for examination.

## Step-by-step guide to registering a position mark

### 1. Check feasibility

Before filing, assess whether the position you want to protect is:

- **Distinctive** — it is not the only possible way to apply the element
- **Non-functional** — it does not arise from a technical or ergonomic requirement
- **Graphically representable** — it is possible to clearly demonstrate the position (the INPI expects the use of dashed lines for the product's outline and continuous lines for the claimed element)

### 2. Conduct a prior art search

Search the INPI trademark database to check whether the position has already been registered or if there are conflicts. The search reduces the risk of office actions or rejections — and it is also a recommended step for conventional marks.

### 3. File via e-Marcas

The application is filed through the [e-Marcas](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas) system with payment of the fee (GRU). The graphic representation must make the claimed position unequivocal.

### 4. Follow the examination

The INPI publishes office actions in the RPI (Industrial Property Journal). In the event of an office action, there is a deadline to respond. Track the status through the system — see our guide on [how to check the status of a process at the INPI]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}}).

### 5. Costs involved

Fees follow the official INPI table for trademark registration, with discounts for MEI, ME, EPP, and educational institutions. See our guide with [all trademark registration costs at the INPI in 2026]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}) for the full fee table.

## Why this matters for businesses

The regulation of position marks has a direct impact on sectors that build visual identity applied to products:

- **Fashion and apparel** — placement of logos, seams, labels, stripes
- **Electronics** — placement of buttons, cameras, connectors
- **Automotive** — placement of emblems, grilles, headlights
- **Cosmetics and packaging** — placement of stripes, embossing, textures

Today, protecting these elements depends on case-by-case decisions and on proving acquired distinctiveness — a heavy burden of proof. With clear criteria, companies gain **predictability** to build defensive and offensive trademark portfolios, including through the **Madrid Protocol** (Brazil has been a signatory since 2019), which allows the international registration of position marks.

## What to watch for in the coming months

The regulatory calendar for the second half of 2026:

1. **August 19, 2026** — meeting with DIRMA stakeholders at the INPI (in-person + Zoom), with discussion of new criteria for registrability and acquired distinctiveness
2. **Publication of the normative act** — after consolidating the contributions, the INPI will publish the final text with responses to the submissions
3. **Update of the Trademark Manual** — the examination guidelines will be incorporated into the manual, providing binding guidance to examiners

For those working with trademark strategy, the time is to study the drafts and prepare the documentation for acquired distinctiveness (evidence of use, perception surveys, market share) — especially for positions already established in the market that currently rely on protection through other legal instruments.
