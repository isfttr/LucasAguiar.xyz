Position marks protect the **specific location** of an element applied to a product when that position, in itself, confers distinctiveness to the sign. In Brazil, the topic is at a turning point: after years of being decided case by case, the INPI opened a public consultation in 2026 to create its own regulatory framework — and the final text should arrive within this semester. This guide explains what they are, how to register them, and what to expect from the new rules.

> **Update (Aug/2026):** INPI Public Consultation No. 01/2026 on position marks ended on **07/24/2026**. The INPI is consolidating the contributions and will hold a **meeting with stakeholders on 08/19/2026** (2 PM to 5 PM, INPI auditorium, Rua Mayrink Veiga 9, Centro/RJ, with live broadcast via Zoom) to present the evaluation of the new criteria for registrability and acquired distinctiveness. The responses to the submissions will be published together with the final texts of the normative act and the examination guidelines in the [Trademark Manual](http://manualdemarcas.inpi.gov.br/).

## What is a position mark?

A position mark is a trademark sign constituted by the **way an element is positioned** on a product — for example, the exact location where a stripe, an emblem, or a seam appears. What is protected is not the element itself, nor the design, nor the shape of the product: it is the **position** occupied by that element.

Classic international examples:

- The **three stripes of Adidas** on the side of footwear and clothing
- **Christian Louboutin's red sole**
- **Levi's back pocket stitching**

In Brazil, the recognition of position marks as an autonomous category of trademark sign has been debated for years. The INPI already examined applications of this nature based on administrative precedents, but without a clear regulatory framework — the gap that the ongoing regulation seeks to fill.

## Position mark vs. three-dimensional mark vs. figurative mark

The distinction is the point that generates the most doubts in practice — and it defines which protection strategy to adopt:

| Type of mark | What it protects | Example |
|---------------|---------------|---------|
| **Position mark** | The location of an element on the product | Stripes on the side of the sneaker |
| **Three-dimensional mark** | The shape of the product itself | Shape of the Coca-Cola bottle |
| **Figurative mark** | The design/logo, regardless of where applied | The Nike symbol applied anywhere |

The position mark is the most subtle of the three: it requires demonstrating that the **position itself** acquired distinctive capacity — that is, that the consumer identifies the origin of the product just by seeing the element in that specific place.

## Legal basis: what the LPI says

The Industrial Property Law (Law No. 9,279/96) does not expressly list "position marks." Registration falls under **Art. 122**, which admits as a trademark "visually perceptible distinctive signs, not comprised in the legal prohibitions."

The registrability challenges appear in the impediments of **Art. 124**:

- **Item III** — signs that constitute a necessary, common, or purely functional shape (the position cannot derive from a mandatory technical characteristic of the product)
- **Item VI** — signs of a generic, necessary, or merely descriptive nature (the position must have distinctiveness, not be obvious)

Therefore, the current regulatory discussion revolves around two concepts: **intrinsic distinctiveness** (the position identifies the origin by itself) and **acquired distinctiveness** (secondary meaning — the position came to identify the origin through continuous use).

## What the INPI regulation proposes

INPI Public Consultation No. 01/2026, published on June 9, 2026 (DOU edition 105, section 1, p. 127, and RPI No. 2892), put two documents up for discussion:

| Document | Subject |
|-----------|--------|
| **Draft normative act** | Registrability criteria, formal requirements, and grounds for unregistrability |
| **Draft examination guidelines** | Guidance for examiners, with examples and technical parameters, to be incorporated into the Trademark Manual |

Among the central points of the drafts:

- **Intrinsic distinctiveness requirement** — the position must identify the origin of the product, not being merely functional or ornamental
- **Proof through use** — possibility of proving acquired distinctiveness when the position is not intrinsically distinctive
- **Limitation to non-functional elements** — the position cannot result from a technical characteristic necessary to the product
- **Graphic representation** — requirement of visual elements demonstrating the claimed position, such as dashed lines indicating the contour of the product

The topic is not new at the INPI: there was already a specific public consultation on position marks in **2021** (04/13 to 06/12/2021), whose contributions were answered by the Permanent Committee for the Improvement of Examination Procedures and Guidelines (CPAPD). The difference now is the design of a **formal normative act**, with binding guidelines for examination.

## Step by step to register a position mark

### 1. Check viability

Before filing, assess whether the position you want to protect is:

- **Distinctive** — it is not the only possible way to apply the element
- **Non-functional** — it does not result from a technical or ergonomic requirement
- **Graphically representable** — it is possible to demonstrate the position clearly (the INPI expects the use of dashed lines for the contour of the product and continuous lines for the claimed element)

### 2. Conduct a prior art search

Consult the INPI trademark database to check whether the position has already been registered or whether there are conflicts. The search reduces the risk of office actions or refusal — and it is also a recommended step for conventional trademarks.

### 3. File through e-Marcas

The application is filed through the [e-Marcas](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas) system with the payment of the fee (GRU). The graphic representation must make the claimed position unequivocal.

### 4. Follow the examination

The INPI publishes decisions in the RPI (Industrial Property Review). In the event of an office action, there is a deadline for response. Follow the status through the system — see our guide on [how to check the status of a process at the INPI]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}}).

### 5. Costs involved

Fees follow the official INPI table for trademark registration, with discounts for MEI, ME, EPP, and educational institutions. See our guide with [all trademark registration costs at the INPI in 2026]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}) for the complete table of fees.

## Why this matters for companies

The regulation of position marks has a direct impact on sectors that build visual identity applied to products:

- **Fashion and apparel** — position of logos, seams, labels, stripes
- **Electronics** — position of buttons, cameras, connectors
- **Automotive** — position of emblems, grilles, headlights
- **Cosmetics and packaging** — position of stripes, reliefs, textures

Today, protecting these elements depends on case-by-case decisions and proof of acquired distinctiveness — a heavy burden of proof. With clear criteria, companies gain **predictability** to build defensive and offensive trademark portfolios, including through the **Madrid Protocol** (Brazil has been a signatory since 2019), which admits the international registration of position marks.

## What to watch in the coming months

The regulatory calendar for the second half of 2026:

1. **08/19/2026** — meeting with stakeholders from DIRMA at the INPI (in-person + Zoom), with discussion of new criteria for registrability and acquired distinctiveness
2. **Publication of the normative act** — after consolidating the contributions, the INPI will publish the final text with responses to the submissions
3. **Update of the Trademark Manual** — the examination guidelines will be incorporated into the manual, guiding examiners in a binding manner

For those working with trademark strategy, the moment calls for studying the drafts and anticipating the documentation of acquired distinctiveness (evidence of use, perception surveys, market share) — especially for positions already consolidated in the market that currently depend on protection through other institutes.
