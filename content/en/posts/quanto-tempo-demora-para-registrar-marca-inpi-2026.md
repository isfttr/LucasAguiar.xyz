There is no single answer to “how long does it take to register a trademark with INPI”: the total time depends on how many stages your application goes through — and on two factors you do not control (third-party oppositions and the Institute’s own queue). What can be answered precisely is the process design: it consists of well-defined stages, with fixed legal deadlines for each act. This guide walks through the complete flow, from the prior art search to the certificate, and explains the reform INPI presented in August 2026 to change that design.

> **Context note (Sep/2026):** on August 19, 2026, INPI presented to stakeholders a **proposed change to the trademark examination flow** (new stage before opposition). It is still a proposal — the official schedule provides for RIA and public consultation in 2027. The flow described below is the one **currently in force**; the final section details what may change.

## Process Overview

Trademark registration in Brazil is governed by the [Industrial Property Law (LPI, Law 9.279/96)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm), especially arts. 122 to 128 (what is registrable and who may file), 133 (10-year term), and 155 to 162 (procedure). The current flow is as follows:

| # | Stage | What happens | Deadline | Legal basis |
|---|-------|----------------|-------|------------|
| 1 | **Prior art search** | Checking for conflicts before spending on filing | — (recommended) | — |
| 2 | **Filing** | Application with the mark and the list of goods/services, on e-Marcas | — | art. 155 |
| 3 | **Formal examination** | Document verification and docketing | — | arts. 156-157 |
| 4 | **Publication in the RPI** | The application is published for third parties’ awareness | — | art. 158 |
| 5 | **Opposition** | Third parties contest the registration | 60 days from publication | art. 158 |
| 6 | **Response** | Applicant replies to the opposition | 60 days from notification | art. 158, §1 |
| 7 | **Substantive examination** | Registrability analysis; requirements may be raised | 60 days to respond to a requirement | art. 159 |
| 8 | **Decision** | Grant or refusal of the application | — | art. 160 |
| 9 | **Payment and certificate** | Fee for first ten-year term and issuance | 60 days from grant (+30 with extra fee) | arts. 161-162 |
| 10 | **Appeal** (only if refused) | Reexamination by INPI’s appellate body | 60 days; counterarguments in 60 days | arts. 212-215 |

The granted registration is valid for **10 years** from the grant date, renewable for equal and successive periods (art. 133 of the LPI) — renewal has its own cost and deadline.

## Step by Step, in Practice

### 1. Prior Art Search (Before Filing)

The most expensive mistake in the process is filing a mark that conflicts with an earlier registration. The search is not mandatory, but it is what separates an application with a real chance from a foregone refusal. See the [complete trademark prior art search guide]({{< relref "posts/busca-anterioridade-marcas-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}) to run this check before filing.

### 2. Filing on e-Marcas

The application is filed through the [e-Marcas](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/e-marcas) system, with a gov.br login. The LPI requires that the application refer to a single sign and contain a request, the representation of the mark, and proof of payment (art. 155). In practice, two points decide cost and risk:

- **Pre-approved specification vs. free-form specification**: choosing the goods/services list from INPI’s standardized wording is cheaper and reduces requirements; a self-written list costs more and increases the chance of an office action.
- **Correct class**: the application is per class under the Nice Classification. A wrong class is a frequent cause of rework.

Updated fees and the 50% discounts for MEI, ME, and EPP are in the [guide to trademark registration costs at INPI]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}).

### 3. Formal Examination

Once the application is submitted, INPI performs the preliminary formal examination and, if it is in order, dockets the application — the filing date is the date of presentation (art. 156). Applications with insufficient data may be handed in with a dated receipt, subject to requirements to be met within 5 days, under penalty of the application being considered nonexistent (art. 157).

### 4. Publication and Opposition (the 60-Day Window)

Once docketed, the application is published in the Industrial Property Journal (RPI) **for opposition to be filed within 60 days** (art. 158). This is the window in which third parties — owners of earlier marks, competitors, or any person with legitimate interest — may contest the registration, usually based on the impediments of art. 124 of the LPI.

If someone opposes, the applicant is notified and may respond within **60 days** (art. 158, §1). The opposition is not decided in isolation: it becomes part of the record and is considered in the substantive examination.

> ⚠️ An untimely opposition will not be considered, and those who do not monitor the RPI can silently miss the window. The [guide to checking procedural status]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}}) shows how to monitor case publications.

### 5. Substantive Examination

Once the opposition period has passed (or the response period, if there was an opposition), INPI examines the application (art. 159). In this phase, the examiner analyzes registrability — distinctiveness, lawfulness, truthfulness, and conflict with earlier marks. It is also when INPI may impose **requirements**, which must be responded to within **60 days**; silence leads to the **final dismissal** of the application (art. 159, §1). Once the requirement is answered — even if not complied with — or its issuance is contested, the examination proceeds (art. 159, §2).

Marks that depend on proof of use — such as signs without intrinsic distinctiveness, including [position marks]({{< relref "posts/consulta-publica-marcas-posicao-inpi-2026/" >}}) — may need to present documentation of acquired distinctiveness (secondary meaning) at this stage. INPI has also updated the [guidelines on high-renown marks and the Madrid Protocol]({{< relref "posts/inpi-manual-marcas-alto-renome-protocolo-madri/" >}}), which serve as a reference for examination.

### 6. Decision, Payment, and Certificate

Once the examination is concluded, INPI decides, granting or refusing the application (art. 160). If granted, the certificate is issued after payment of the fees is proven (art. 161). The law allows **60 days from the grant** to pay for the issuance of the certificate and the first ten-year term, with an additional grace period of 30 days upon payment of a specific fee, under penalty of final dismissal (art. 162). Since the 2025 fee schedule, these fees are **free of charge and issuance is automatic** — there is no longer a first-ten-year payment slip to pay.

### 7. Appeal Against Refusal

If the application is refused, an appeal may be filed within **60 days** (art. 212 of the LPI), which is received with suspensive and full devolutive effect; interested parties have 60 days to file counterarguments (art. 213), and the decision on the appeal is final in the administrative sphere (art. 215). In practice, trademark appeals are judged by INPI’s appellate instance (Third Vice-Presidency).

Two important consequences of the legal design:

- **No appeal lies from the decision granting the registration** (art. 212, §2): the third party that lost the opposition cannot appeal administratively — the remedy is judicial (nullity action).
- **No appeal lies from the final dismissal**: that is why the deadline to respond to a requirement (60 days) is, in practice, the most dangerous one in the process.

## And How Long Does It Take, After All?

The stages have fixed legal deadlines for **the parties**; INPI has no legal deadline to examine. The total time, therefore, is the sum of three components:

1. **Party deadlines**: if there is an opposition, add its 60 days + up to 60 days for the response; if there is a requirement, another 60 days. These deadlines are the largest predictable determinants of the schedule.
2. **INPI’s examination queue**: it varies with the volume of applications and changes over time. INPI publishes the official time indicators on the [INPI Data](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/inpi-data) portal — it is the right source for the current average time, rather than relying on third-party estimates.
3. **Application complexity**: marks with oppositions, requirements, or distinctiveness issues take longer than simple, uncontested applications.

The strategic takeaway is this: what is under your control is the **quality of the filing** (a well-done search, correct class, adequate specification) and **timeliness in responses** (opposition, requirement, appeal). Well-instructed applications with no opposition are those that move through the flow fastest.

## What May Change: the Proposed Reform of the Examination Flow

On August 19, 2026, the Trademark Directorate (DIRMA) presented to stakeholders a proposal to amend INPI/PR Ordinance No. 08/2022 that **creates an examination stage before opposition** — the biggest procedural change in trademarks in recent years ([official news](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias/encontro-promove-dialogo-e-apresenta-novidades-no-exame-de-marcas), [event presentation](https://inpidrive.inpi.gov.br/index.php/s/BQKlKMwGBepQjv6)).

The stated logic is efficiency: today **all** published applications go through the opposition window, even those with obvious obstacles. Under the proposal, INPI would first carry out a **Preliminary Technical Examination (PTE)** checking lawfulness, mandatory documents, distinctiveness/truthfulness, and availability:

| Current flow (in force) | Proposed flow (under review) |
|---|---|
| Filing → formal examination → publication for opposition | Filing → formal examination → **preliminary technical examination** |
| All published applications open opposition | Only applications **approved in the PTE** are published for opposition |
| Substantive examination assesses all registrability issues | Substantive examination focused on the allegations in oppositions; PTE matters are not reexamined |
| — | Applications rejected in the PTE: refusal published, 60 days to appeal, no opposition |
| — | Multi-class applications: approved classes wait until the appeal deadline for refused classes expires, for joint publication |
| — | Prior art searches limited to the classes indicated in the opposition |

Other points in the proposal: the deadline to request **acquired distinctiveness** would start counting from publication of the PTE result (or from notification of the oppositions); **prioritized processing** would receive priority at two moments (after formal examination and after the end of the opposition/response period). At the same event, INPI presented the status of the **guidelines for opinion surveys on acquired distinctiveness** (GERDIA working group) and of the revisions to the examination of three-dimensional and position marks.

**Official reform schedule:** Public Input Call (TPS) from October to December 2026 → Regulatory Impact Analysis (RIA) by December 2026, with report by January 15, 2027 → public consultation between January and March 2027 → possible regulatory amendment in June 2027. In other words: **nothing changes in the current flow before mid-2027**, and it still depends on a decision after the contributions.

If approved, the reform changes the game for both sides: for **applicants**, the early registrability analysis means the quality of the filing decides the outcome even earlier; for **third parties**, opposition ceases to be a mass screening and becomes a surgical dispute over applications that INPI has already considered registrable — requiring more precise grounds (and, in multi-class applications, extra attention to the effect of refused classes on approved ones).

## Checklist for Those About to File

1. Do the [prior art search]({{< relref "posts/busca-anterioridade-marcas-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}) before paying the GRU.
2. Check the class (Nice Classification) and the specification — prefer the pre-approved one when possible.
3. File on e-Marcas and keep the receipt; check the [costs and discounts]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}).
4. Monitor the RPI: the 60 days for opposition and response do not notify you by email.
5. Respond to **any requirement within 60 days** — silence leads to final dismissal.
6. If refused, consider an appeal within 60 days — and remember that the third party who lost the opposition has no administrative appeal against the grant.
