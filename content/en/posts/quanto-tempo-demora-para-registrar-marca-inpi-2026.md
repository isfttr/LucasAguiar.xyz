There is no single answer to "how long does it take to register a trademark with INPI": the total time depends on how many stages your application has to go through — and on two factors you do not control (oppositions from third parties and the Institute's own queue). What can be answered with precision is the process design: the stages are well defined, with fixed legal deadlines for each act. This guide walks through the complete flow, from the prior art search to the certificate, and explains the reform that INPI presented in August 2026 to change this design.

> **Context note (Sep 2026):** on August 19, 2026, INPI presented to interested parties a **proposed change to the trademark examination flow** (a new stage before opposition). It is still a proposal — the official schedule provides for a Regulatory Impact Analysis (AIR) and public consultation in 2027. The flow described below is the one **currently in force**; the final section details what may change.

## Process overview

Trademark registration in Brazil is regulated by the [Industrial Property Law (LPI, Law 9.279/96)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm), especially arts. 122 to 128 (what is registrable and who may apply), 133 (10-year term), and 155 to 162 (procedure). The current flow looks like this:

| # | Stage | What happens | Deadline | Legal basis |
|---|-------|----------------|-------|------------|
| 1 | **Prior art search** | Checking for conflicts before spending on the filing | — (recommended) | — |
| 2 | **Filing** | Application containing the sign and the list of products/services, filed in e-Marcas | — | art. 155 |
| 3 | **Formal examination** | Document check and protocoling | — | arts. 156-157 |
| 4 | **Publication in the RPI** | The application is published to give third parties notice | — | art. 158 |
| 5 | **Opposition** | Third parties contest the registration | 60 days from publication | art. 158 |
| 6 | **Reply** | Applicant responds to the opposition | 60 days from notice | art. 158, §1 |
| 7 | **Substantive examination** | Registrability analysis; office actions may be issued | 60 days to respond to office action | art. 159 |
| 8 | **Decision** | Grant or refusal of the application | — | art. 160 |
| 9 | **Payment and certificate** | Fee for the first 10-year term and issuance | 60 days from the grant (+30 with additional fee) | arts. 161-162 |
| 10 | **Appeal** (only if refused) | Reexamination by INPI's appellate instance | 60 days; counter-arguments within 60 days | arts. 212-215 |

The granted registration is valid for **10 years** counted from the grant, renewable for equal and successive periods (art. 133 of the LPI) — renewal has its own cost and deadline.

## Step by step, in practice

### 1. Prior art search (before filing)

The most expensive mistake in the process is filing a trademark that conflicts with an earlier registration. The search is not mandatory, but it is what separates an application with a real chance from a refusal announced in advance. See the [complete trademark prior art search guide at INPI]({{< relref "posts/busca-anterioridade-marcas-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}) to do this check before filing.

### 2. Filing in e-Marcas

The application is filed through the [e-Marcas](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/e-marcas) system, with a gov.br login. The LPI requires that the application refer to a single sign and contain a formal request, the representation of the sign, and proof of payment (art. 155). In practice, two points decide cost and risk:

- **Pre-approved specification vs. free-form list**: choosing the list of products/services from INPI's standardization is cheaper and reduces office actions; writing your own list costs more and increases the chance of a notification.
- **Correct class**: the application is filed per class of the Nice Classification. A wrong class is a frequent cause of rework.

Updated fees and the 50% discounts for MEI, ME and EPP are in the [guide to trademark registration costs at INPI]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}).

### 3. Formal examination

Once the application has been submitted, INPI carries out the preliminary formal examination and, if it is in order, accepts the filing — the filing date is the date of submission (art. 156). Applications with insufficient data may be returned with a dated receipt, subject to requirements to be met within 5 days, on penalty of the application being considered nonexistent (art. 157).

### 4. Publication and opposition (the 60-day window)

Once accepted, the application is published in the Industrial Property Journal (RPI) **for oppositions to be filed within 60 days** (art. 158). This is the window in which third parties — owners of earlier trademarks, competitors, or any person with a legitimate interest — may contest the registration, usually based on the impediments in art. 124 of the LPI.

If someone opposes, the applicant is notified and may respond within **60 days** (art. 158, §1). The opposition is not decided in isolation: it becomes part of the record and is considered in the substantive examination.

> ⚠️ An untimely opposition is not considered, and anyone who does not monitor the RPI may silently lose the window. The [guide to checking procedural status]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}}) shows how to monitor the publications in the process.

### 5. Substantive examination

Once the opposition period (or the reply period, if there was an opposition) has elapsed, INPI examines the application (art. 159). At this stage, the examiner analyzes registrability — distinctiveness, lawfulness, truthfulness, and conflict with earlier trademarks. This is also when INPI may issue **office actions**, which must be answered within **60 days**; silence leads to **definitive shelving** of the application (art. 159, §1). Once the office action has been answered — even if not complied with — or its issuance has been contested, the examination proceeds (art. 159, §2).

Trademarks that depend on proof of use — such as signs without intrinsic distinctiveness, including [position marks]({{< relref "posts/consulta-publica-marcas-posicao-inpi-2026/" >}}) — may need to submit documentation of acquired distinctiveness (secondary meaning) at this stage. INPI has also updated the [guidelines on highly renowned marks and the Madrid Protocol]({{< relref "posts/inpi-manual-marcas-alto-renome-protocolo-madri/" >}}), which serve as a reference for examination.

### 6. Decision, payment, and certificate

Once the examination is complete, INPI decides by granting or refusing the application (art. 160). If granted, the certificate is issued after payment of the fees is confirmed (art. 161). The law grants **60 days from the grant** to pay for the issuance of the certificate and the first 10-year term, with an additional 30-day grace period upon payment of a specific fee, under penalty of definitive shelving (art. 162). Since the 2025 fee schedule, these fees are **free of charge and issuance is automatic** — there is no longer a payment slip for the first 10-year term to pay.

### 7. Appeal against refusal

If the application is refused, an appeal may be filed within **60 days** (art. 212 of the LPI), admitted with suspensive effect and full devolutive effect; interested parties have 60 days to file counter-arguments (art. 213), and the decision on the appeal is final in the administrative sphere (art. 215). In practice, trademark appeals are decided by INPI's appellate instance (3rd Vice-Presidency).

Two important consequences of the legal design:

- **No appeal lies from the decision granting the registration** (art. 212, §2): the third party that lost the opposition cannot file an administrative appeal — the path is judicial (nullity action).
- **No appeal lies from definitive shelving**: that is why the office action deadline (60 days) is, in practice, the most dangerous one in the process.

## And how long does it actually take?

The stages have fixed legal deadlines for **the parties**; INPI has no legal deadline to examine. The total time, therefore, is the sum of three components:

1. **Deadlines of the parties**: if there is an opposition, add the 60 days for it + up to 60 days for the reply; if there is an office action, add another 60 days. These deadlines are the largest predictable determinants of the timeline.
2. **INPI's examination queue**: it varies with the volume of applications and changes over time. INPI publishes the official time indicators on the [INPI Data](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/inpi-data) portal — that is the right source for the current average time, rather than relying on third-party estimates.
3. **Application complexity**: trademarks with oppositions, office actions, or distinctiveness issues take longer than simple, uncontested applications.

The strategic takeaway is this: what is under your control is the **quality of the filing** (a well-conducted search, the correct class, an adequate specification) and **timeliness in responses** (opposition, office action, appeal). Well-prepared applications without opposition are the ones that move through the flow fastest.

## What may change: the proposed examination flow reform

On August 19, 2026, the Trademarks Directorate (DIRMA) presented to interested parties a proposal to amend INPI/PR Ordinance No. 08/2022 that **creates an examination stage before opposition** — the biggest procedural change for trademarks in recent years ([official news](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias/encontro-promove-dialogo-e-apresenta-novidades-no-exame-de-marcas), [event presentation](https://inpidrive.inpi.gov.br/index.php/s/BQKlKMwGBepQjv6)).

The stated logic is efficiency: today **all** published applications go through the opposition window, even those with obvious impediments. In the proposal, INPI would first conduct a **Preliminary Technical Examination (ETP)** checking lawfulness, mandatory documents, distinctiveness/truthfulness, and availability:

| Current flow (in force) | Proposed flow (under review) |
|---|---|
| Filing → formal examination → publication for opposition | Filing → formal examination → **preliminary technical examination** |
| All published applications are open to opposition | Only applications **approved in the ETP** are published for opposition |
| Substantive examination assesses all registrability aspects | Substantive examination focused on the allegations raised in the oppositions; ETP matters are not reexamined |
| — | Applications refused in the ETP: refusal published, 60 days for appeal, no opposition |
| — | Multi-class applications: approved classes wait for the end of the appeal period of the refused ones to be published jointly |
| — | Prior art searches limited to the classes indicated in the opposition |

Other points in the proposal: the period for requesting **acquired distinctiveness** would start running from the publication of the ETP result (or from the notice of the oppositions); **prioritized processing** would gain priority at two points (after formal examination and after the end of the opposition/reply period). At the same meeting, INPI presented the progress of the **guidelines for opinion surveys on acquired distinctiveness** (GERDIA group) and of the reviews of the examination of three-dimensional and position marks.

**Official reform schedule:** Public Call for Input (TPS) from October to December 2026 → Regulatory Impact Analysis (AIR) by December 2026, with a report by January 15, 2027 → public consultation between January and March 2027 → possible regulatory change in June 2027. In other words: **nothing changes in the current flow before mid-2027**, and it still depends on a decision after the contributions.

If approved, the reform changes the game for both sides: for **applicants**, the earlier registrability analysis means that the quality of the filing decides the outcome even sooner; for **third parties**, opposition is no longer mass screening and becomes a surgical dispute over applications that INPI has already considered registrable — which will require more precise reasoning (and, in multi-class applications, extra attention to the effect of refused classes on approved ones).

## Checklist for anyone planning to file

1. Run the [prior art search]({{< relref "posts/busca-anterioridade-marcas-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}) before paying the GRU.
2. Check the class (Nice Classification) and the specification — prefer the pre-approved one when possible.
3. File in e-Marcas and keep the receipt; check the [costs and discounts]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}).
4. Monitor the RPI: the 60-day periods for opposition and reply are not announced by email.
5. Respond to **any office action within 60 days** — silence leads to definitive shelving.
6. If refused, consider an appeal within 60 days — and remember that a third party that lost the opposition has no administrative appeal against the grant.
