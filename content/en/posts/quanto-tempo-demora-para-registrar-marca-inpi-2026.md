There is no single answer to "how long does it take to register a trademark at INPI": the total time depends on how many stages your application will go through—and on two factors you don't control (third-party oppositions and the Institute's own backlog). What can be answered with precision is the design of the process: it consists of well-defined stages, with fixed legal deadlines for each act. This guide walks through the complete flow, from the prior art search to the certificate, and explains the reform INPI presented in August 2026 to change this design.

> **Context notice (Sep/2026):** on 19 August 2026, INPI presented to interested parties a **proposed change to the trademark examination flow** (a new stage before opposition). It is still a proposal—the official schedule provides for a Regulatory Impact Analysis (AIR) and public consultation in 2027. The flow described below is what is **in force today**; the final section details what may change.

## Process overview

Trademark registration in Brazil is regulated by the [Industrial Property Law (LPI, Law 9.279/96)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm), especially by arts. 122 to 128 (what is registrable and who may apply), 133 (10-year validity), and 155 to 162 (procedure). The current flow looks like this:

| # | Stage | What happens | Deadline | Legal basis |
|---|-------|----------------|-------|------------|
| 1 | **Prior art search** | Checking for conflicts before spending on filing | — (recommended) | — |
| 2 | **Filing** | Application containing the sign and the list of products/services, via e-Marcas | — | art. 155 |
| 3 | **Formal examination** | Document review and protocoling | — | arts. 156-157 |
| 4 | **Publication in the RPI** | The application is published for third parties' awareness | — | art. 158 |
| 5 | **Opposition** | Third parties challenge the registration | 60 days from publication | art. 158 |
| 6 | **Response** | Applicant responds to the opposition | 60 days from notification | art. 158, §1 |
| 7 | **Substantive examination** | Registrability analysis; requirements may be issued | 60 days to respond to a requirement | art. 159 |
| 8 | **Decision** | Grant or refusal of the application | — | art. 160 |
| 9 | **Payment and certificate** | Fee for the first 10-year term and issuance | 60 days from grant (+30 with extra fee) | arts. 161-162 |
| 10 | **Appeal** (only if refused) | Reexamination by INPI's appellate instance | 60 days; counter-arguments within 60 days | arts. 212-215 |

A granted registration is valid for **10 years** from the grant, renewable for equal and successive periods (art. 133 of the LPI)—renewal has its own cost and deadline.

## Stage by stage, in practice

### 1. Prior art search (before filing)

The most expensive mistake in the process is filing a trademark that conflicts with an earlier registration. The search is not mandatory, but it is what separates an application with a real chance from a refusal that is announced in advance. See the [complete trademark prior art search guide]({{< relref "posts/busca-anterioridade-marcas-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}) to carry out this check before filing.

### 2. Filing via e-Marcas

The application is filed through the [e-Marcas](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/e-marcas) system, using a gov.br login. The LPI requires that the application refer to a single sign and contain the request, the representation of the sign, and proof of payment (art. 155). In practice, two points decide cost and risk:

- **Pre-approved specification vs. free text**: choosing the product/service list from INPI's standardization is cheaper and reduces requirements; a self-written list costs more and increases the chance of a notification.
- **Correct class**: the application is filed per class of the Nice Classification. A wrong class is a frequent cause of rework.

Updated fees and the 50% discounts for MEI, ME, and EPP are in the [INPI trademark registration cost guide]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}).

### 3. Formal examination

Once the application is presented, INPI conducts the preliminary formal examination and, if it is in order, protocols the application—the filing date is the presentation date (art. 156). Applications with insufficient data may be handed back with a dated receipt, with requirements to be fulfilled within 5 days, under penalty of the application being deemed nonexistent (art. 157).

### 4. Publication and opposition (the 60-day window)

Once protocoled, the application is published in the Industrial Property Magazine (RPI) **for opposition within 60 days** (art. 158). This is the window in which third parties—owners of earlier trademarks, competitors, or any person with a legitimate interest—may challenge the registration, usually based on the grounds of art. 124 of the LPI.

If someone opposes, the applicant is notified and may respond within **60 days** (art. 158, §1). The opposition is not decided in isolation: it becomes part of the case file and is considered in the substantive examination.

> ⚠️ An untimely opposition is not considered, and those who do not follow the RPI may silently miss the window. The [procedural status consultation guide]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}}) shows how to monitor the case publications.

### 5. Substantive examination

Once the opposition period (or the response period, if there was an opposition) has elapsed, INPI examines the application (art. 159). At this stage, the examiner analyzes registrability—distinctiveness, lawfulness, truthfulness, and conflict with earlier trademarks. It is also when INPI may issue **requirements**, which must be answered within **60 days**; silence leads to the **definitive dismissal** of the application (art. 159, §1). Once a requirement is answered—even if not complied with—or its formulation is challenged, the examination proceeds (art. 159, §2).

Trademarks that depend on proof of use—such as signs without inherent distinctiveness, including [position marks]({{< relref "posts/consulta-publica-marcas-posicao-inpi-2026/" >}})—may need to present documentation of acquired distinctiveness (secondary meaning) at this stage. INPI has also updated the [guidelines on well-known marks and the Madrid Protocol]({{< relref "posts/inpi-manual-marcas-alto-renome-protocolo-madri/" >}}), which serve as a reference for the examination.

### 6. Decision, payment, and certificate

Once the examination is concluded, INPI decides to grant or refuse the application (art. 160). If granted, the certificate is issued after proof of payment of the fees (art. 161). The law gives **60 days from the grant** to pay for the certificate issuance and the first 10-year term, with a 30-day grace period upon payment of a specific fee, under penalty of definitive dismissal (art. 162). Since the 2025 fee schedule, these fees are **free of charge and issuance is automatic**—there is no longer a first-10-year-term payment slip to pay.

### 7. Appeal against refusal

If the application is refused, an appeal is available within **60 days** (art. 212 of the LPI), received with suspensive effect and full devolutive effect; interested parties have 60 days to file counter-arguments (art. 213), and the decision on the appeal is final in the administrative sphere (art. 215). In practice, a trademark appeal is examined by INPI's appellate instance (3rd Vice-Presidency).

Two important consequences of the legal design:

- **No appeal is available from the decision that grants the registration** (art. 212, §2): the third party that lost the opposition cannot appeal administratively—the route is judicial (nullity action).
- **No appeal is available from definitive dismissal**: this is why the requirement deadline (60 days) is, in practice, the most dangerous part of the process.

## How long does it actually take?

The stages have fixed legal deadlines for **the parties**; INPI has no legal deadline for examination. The total time, therefore, is the sum of three components:

1. **Party deadlines**: if there is an opposition, add its 60 days + up to 60 days for the response; if there is a requirement, another 60 days. These deadlines are the largest predictable determinants of the schedule.
2. **INPI examination backlog**: it varies with the volume of applications and changes over time. INPI publishes the official time indicators on the [INPI Data](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/inpi-data) portal—that is the correct source for finding out the current average time, rather than relying on third-party estimates.
3. **Application complexity**: trademarks with opposition, requirements, or distinctiveness issues take longer than simple, uncontested applications.

The strategic reading is this: what is under your control is the **quality of the filing** (a well-done search, correct class, adequate specification) and **timeliness in responses** (opposition, requirement, appeal). Well-prepared applications with no opposition are those that move through the flow fastest.

## What may change: the proposed examination flow reform

On 19 August 2026, the Trademark Directorate (DIRMA) presented to interested parties a proposal to amend INPI/PR Ordinance No. 08/2022 that **creates an examination stage before opposition**—the biggest procedural change in trademarks in recent years ([official news](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias/encontro-promove-dialogo-e-apresenta-novidades-no-exame-de-marcas), [event presentation](https://inpidrive.inpi.gov.br/index.php/s/BQKlKMwGBepQjv6)).

The stated logic is efficiency: today **all** published applications go through the opposition window, even those with obvious impediments. Under the proposal, INPI would first carry out a **Preliminary Technical Examination (ETP)** checking lawfulness, mandatory documents, distinctiveness/truthfulness, and availability:

| Current flow (in force) | Proposed flow (under review) |
|---|---|
| Filing → formal examination → publication for opposition | Filing → formal examination → **preliminary technical examination** |
| All published applications open opposition | Only applications **approved in the ETP** are published for opposition |
| Substantive examination assesses full registrability | Substantive examination focused on opposition arguments; ETP matters are not reexamined |
| — | Applications rejected in the ETP: published refusal, 60 days for appeal, no opposition |
| — | Multi-class applications: approved classes wait for the end of the appeal period for refused classes before joint publication |
| — | Prior art searches limited to the classes indicated in the opposition |

Other points in the proposal: the deadline to request **acquired distinctiveness** would start counting from the publication of the ETP result (or from the notification of oppositions); **prioritized processing** would gain prioritization at two moments (after formal examination and after the end of the opposition/response period). At the same meeting, INPI presented the status of the **guidelines for opinion surveys on acquired distinctiveness** (GERDIA group) and of the reviews of the examination of three-dimensional and position marks.

**Official reform schedule:** Public Call for Contributions (TPS) from October to December 2026 → Regulatory Impact Analysis (AIR) until December 2026, with a report by 15 January 2027 → public consultation between January and March 2027 → possible regulatory change in June 2027. In other words: **nothing changes in the current flow before mid-2027**, and it still depends on a decision after the contributions.

If approved, the reform changes the game for both sides: for **applicants**, the early registrability analysis means the quality of the filing decides the application's fate even sooner; for **third parties**, opposition ceases to be a mass screening and becomes a surgical dispute over applications that INPI has already considered registrable—which will require more precise reasoning (and, in multi-class applications, extra attention to the effect of refused classes on approved ones).

## Checklist for those filing

1. Do the [prior art search]({{< relref "posts/busca-anterioridade-marcas-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}) before paying the GRU.
2. Check the class (Nice Classification) and the specification—prefer the pre-approved one when possible.
3. File at e-Marcas and keep the receipt; check the [costs and discounts]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}).
4. Follow the RPI: the 60-day opposition and response deadlines are not notified by email.
5. Respond to **any requirement within 60 days**—silence leads to definitive dismissal.
6. If refused, consider the appeal within 60 days—and remember that the third party who lost the opposition has no administrative appeal against the grant.
