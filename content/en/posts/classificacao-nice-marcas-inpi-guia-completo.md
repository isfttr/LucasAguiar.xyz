Choosing the wrong class (or forgetting a class) is one of the most expensive mistakes in trademark registration at INPI: protection only covers the products and services listed in the application, and each class has its own filing fee. This guide explains how the International Nice Classification works, how to identify the right class for your case, and what tools INPI offers to reduce the risk of error in the specification.

## What is the Nice Classification

The International Classification of Products and Services — known as the Nice Classification or NCL — is the system created by the [Nice Agreement of 1957](https://www.wipo.int/classifications/nice/en/), administered by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO). It organizes all products and services that can be subject to trademark registration into **45 classes**:

- **Classes 1 to 34**: products (physical goods)
- **Classes 35 to 45**: services

INPI fully adopts the Nice Classification for trademark applications, as explained on the [official product and service classification page](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/classificacao-marcas). Because the system is an international agreement, it cannot be changed by each country — which ensures that a trademark registered in class 25 (clothing) in Brazil has the same classification in Europe, the US, or China.

Since 01/01/2026, the **13th edition** of the Nice Classification is in force, with periodic updates to items and explanatory notes. Each edition has a validity period of 3 years (decision of the 31st Meeting of Experts of the Nice Union, in 2022).

## Why the right class matters

Trademark registration protects the sign **only for the products or services listed in the specification**. This means:

- Registering only in class 25 (clothing) does not protect the same trademark for accessories in class 18 (bags) or for retail services in class 35.
- Another company can register the same name in a class you did not cover — and your later use in that area may constitute infringement.
- Each class has its **own cost**: the registration application (code 389, pre-approved specification) costs R$ 880.00 per class (R$ 440.00 for ME/MEI with a 50% discount). A trademark in 3 classes costs 3× the amount. The free specification (code 394) costs R$ 1,720.00 per class (R$ 860.00 with the discount).

The decision of how many and which classes to register is therefore a business strategy decision — not a bureaucratic detail. Before filing, it is worth doing a [prior trademark search at INPI]({{< relref "posts/busca-anterioridade-marcas-inpi-guia-completo/" >}}) in all intended classes.

## The most common pitfalls in choosing a class

**1. Product and retail sale are different classes.** Those who manufacture cosmetics register in class 3 (product). But those who have a store — physical or e-commerce — selling third-party cosmetics need class 35, which covers retail services. INPI even exhausted the priority processing quotas for trademark applications in e-commerce precisely because of the volume of applications in class 35 — see the [impact of this for those registering e-commerce trademarks]({{< relref "posts/inpi-esgotamento-cotas-tramite-prioritario-marcas-ecommerce/" >}}).

**2. Software and technology services are not "automatic class 9."** Software as a product (media, downloadable app) goes to class 9. But custom software development, system maintenance, and hosting are class 42 services. Technology companies often need both classes — and sometimes class 35, 38, or 41 as well.

**3. Restaurant and delivery are the same class (43), but ready-made packaged food can go to 29/30.** The food service belongs in class 43; the industrialized food product follows the food classes. If the business does both, evaluate both fronts.

**4. Events and sports products are not the same thing.** Organizing championships and courses is class 41 (services); manufacturing balls and equipment is class 28 (products).

The practical rule: list **all** products and services the business offers today — and those it intends to offer in the next 3 to 5 years. From that list, identify each corresponding class. Registering later is possible, but the subsequent application will have a new filing date, which may cause you to lose priority over a competitor.

## Auxiliary lists, pre-approved specification, and free specification

INPI does not work only with the international list: it maintains **Auxiliary Lists** of products and services (updated in 2026), which supplement Nice with items frequently used in Brazil. There are three ways to define the application specification:

| Path | How it works | Cost (per class) |
|---------|---------------|--------------------|
| **Pre-approved specification (code 389)** | Choose items from the e-Marcas system list (Nice + auxiliary lists) | R$ 880.00 (R$ 440.00 ME/MEI) |
| **Free specification (code 394)** | You write the products/services yourself | R$ 1,720.00 (R$ 860.00 ME/MEI) |
| **Consultation with the classification committee (code 357)** | Formal inquiry to INPI about the classification of items | R$ 170.00 for up to 5 items + R$ 20.00 per additional item |

The pre-approved specification is recommended for most cases: it is cheaper and reduces the risk of technical office actions. The free specification only pays off when the business is very specific and no pre-approved item describes the product or service well. Throughout 2025, INPI removed some items from the pre-approved list (announced in RPIs 2816, 2817, and 2849) — so it is worth checking the [current list in e-Marcas](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/classificacao-marcas) before filing.

If you have questions about the classification of an item, the consultation with the classification committee (code 357 of the Fee Table) is the official way to resolve the question before filing. Important: the consultation is **not** equivalent to a registration application or a search certificate — it is only a classification clarification.

## Errors that delay (or sink) the application

- **Vague or generic specification** — terms such as "miscellaneous products" or "general services" are not accepted; each item must be identifiable.
- **Item in the wrong class** — placing clothing in class 35 or software in class 42 without the proper correspondence triggers a reclassification requirement.
- **Copying another application's specification** — other people's specifications often contain items that do not match your business, creating unnecessary conflict with third-party trademarks.
- **Ignoring class 35 in retail businesses** — the most common error in online store applications.

After filing, the application status can be tracked through the [guide to consulting INPI processes]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}}). And if INPI considers the specification generic or imprecise, the requirement arrives through an official office action — responding within the deadline is essential to avoid losing the application.

## What to keep in mind

Choosing the class is the most strategic decision in trademark registration and should not be treated as a formal detail: it defines the real scope of protection and the cost of the application. Before filing, (1) list all current and future products and services of the business, (2) map the corresponding classes using the table above, (3) conduct a prior search in each intended class, and (4) prefer the pre-approved specification unless there is a real need for a free specification. With the 13th edition of Nice in force since January 2026, the available items have changed — always check the current list on INPI's website before filing.
