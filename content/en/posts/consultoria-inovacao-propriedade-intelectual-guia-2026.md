Innovation and intellectual property consulting transforms intangible assets — trademarks, patents, industrial designs, software, and know-how — into measurable competitive advantage. It also prevents the opposite scenario: losing a right due to a missed deadline, poorly drafted application, or lack of portfolio strategy. This guide explains what this type of consulting delivers, when to hire, how much it costs, and how to choose the right professional.

## What is innovation and intellectual property consulting

It is the specialized service of identifying, protecting, managing, and monetizing intangible assets — and connecting that protection to the company's innovation strategy. While a traditional lawyer acts in the defense of specific cases, the consultant acts beforehand: in designing the protection strategy, analyzing feasibility, managing the portfolio, and economically exploiting rights.

The practice combines three fronts:

- **Technical**: prior art search, drafting and monitoring of patent applications, registration of trademarks, industrial designs, and software.
- **Strategic**: aligning the IP portfolio with the business plan, market, and competition.
- **Economic**: valuation of intangibles, licensing, technology transfer agreements, and use of IP as credit collateral.

## What a consultant delivers

The scope varies according to the company's maturity, but the most common services are:

**1. Portfolio diagnosis and strategy**
A survey of what the company has already protected, what it should protect, and what is exposed. The result is a priority plan with costs and deadlines.

**2. Prior art and feasibility search**
Before filing a patent or requesting trademark registration, you need to know whether the application has a chance. The prior art search prevents spending on applications that will be rejected — and it is mandatory for those who want to qualify as a searcher at INPI.

**3. Registration and monitoring at INPI**
Trademarks, patents (invention and utility model), industrial design, computer program, and geographical indications. Includes monitoring the process until grant, with management of deadlines and fees.

**4. IP due diligence**
In mergers, acquisitions, investment rounds, and tenders, the buyer or investor needs to know whether the intangible assets exist, are valid, and actually belong to the company. Due diligence maps this with a technical report.

**5. Licensing and technology transfer agreements**
Structuring agreements that allow monetizing IP without giving up control — and registration of these agreements with INPI, mandatory to produce effects against third parties.

**6. Industrial policy, incentives, and innovation**
Support in the use of public policy instruments: special regimes, innovation incentives, and support programs that require intellectual protection as a counterpart.

## When to hire

Some signs indicate that the company needs IP consulting:

- You discovered that a competitor registered a trademark identical or similar to yours.
- The company innovates but has never performed a prior art search before launching products.
- An investor, client, or bidder asked for proof that the intellectual property belongs to the company.
- You want to know whether a technology or trademark has economic value — to sell, license, or use as collateral.
- Registration deadlines are passing without anyone taking responsibility.

The cost of hiring after the problem has occurred (opposition, nullity, lawsuit) is always higher than the cost of preventing it.

## How much it costs

There is no single price list — the price depends on the service and complexity:

- **Prior art search**: from R$ 500 to R$ 3,000 per search, depending on depth and scope (national or international).
- **Portfolio diagnosis**: from R$ 3,000 to R$ 15,000, depending on the number of assets.
- **Trademark registration**: professional fees from ~R$ 1,000 to R$ 2,500 per class (INPI fees are paid separately and vary by company size).
- **Patent drafting and filing**: from R$ 5,000 to R$ 20,000, depending on technical complexity.
- **IP due diligence**: usually charged per project, from R$ 8,000 to R$ 40,000.
- **Recurring consulting (retainer)**: R$ 2,000 to R$ 10,000/month for ongoing portfolio management.

## How to choose a consultant

Check four things: technical or legal background in the field, proven experience with the type of asset you need to protect, transparency about costs and deadlines, and — for patents — technical qualification compatible with the invention's segment. Ask for reports of previous work and avoid anyone who promises guaranteed grant: no registration at INPI is guaranteed.
