The race for patenting generative AI inventions has reached a new milestone: more patent families were published in 2024 and 2025 than in the entire previous decade combined. The figures came from WIPO's (World Intellectual Property Organization) **Technology SPARK** report, released in July 2026, and redefine the global innovation map.

If you work with intellectual property, patents, or innovation, this overview brings together the essential data from the report in one place — a reference guide on where generative AI is being invented, who is filing patents, and at what pace.

## The Explosive Growth of Generative AI Patents

The standard metric for measuring new inventions is the **published patent family**. According to WIPO, the number jumped from approximately **14,000 families in 2023 to over 37,800 in 2025** — practically tripling in two years.

The movement becomes even more impressive when accumulated over the period:

| Period | GenAI patent families published |
|---------|--------------------------------------|
| 2014 to 2023 (entire decade) | < 56,000 cumulative |
| 2024 and 2025 (just two years) | > 56,000 |
| 2023 | ~14,000 |
| 2025 | > 37,800 |

In other words, **over 56,000 generative AI patent families were published in 2024 and 2025 alone** — surpassing the cumulative production from 2014 to 2023. Generative AI already accounts for **8.7% of all published AI-related patent families**, up from 6.1% in 2023 and just 4.2% in 2017.

## Who Leads in GenAI Patent Filings

The report reveals that the protection of generative inventions is no longer an exclusive matter for technology companies. The ranking is led by large multinationals from diverse sectors:

1. **SoftBank (Japan)** — largest global filer, with almost **3,000 families** published in 2024-2025
2. **Tencent Holdings (China)**
3. **Ping An Insurance Group (China)**
4. **Baidu (China)**

Completing the top 10 are the American companies **Alphabet, Microsoft, and IBM**, as well as research institutions and infrastructure companies. Six of the world's top ten filers are Chinese.

## China Dominates; Japan Grows Fastest

China remains the **world's largest source** of GenAI patent publications, with over **43,000 families** from Chinese inventors published in 2024-2025 — a substantial margin over other countries. The Chinese compound annual growth rate (CAGR) between 2023 and 2025 was **64%**.

The relative highlight, however, is **Japan**, which recorded the fastest growth rate among the leading countries: a CAGR of **210%**, rising from fourth to third global position. The United States recorded a **92%** CAGR in the same period, as the commercial development of GenAI by American companies translated into an acceleration of patenting.

| Country | GenAI families CAGR (2023-2025) | Position |
|------|------------------------------------|---------|
| Japan | 210% | 3rd |
| Switzerland | 124% | top 10 |
| Canada | 109% | top 10 |
| USA | 92% | 2nd |
| Germany | 85% | European leader |
| China | 64% | 1st |

**Germany surpassed the United Kingdom** as Europe's leading inventing locality, with an 85% CAGR. Seven of the world's top ten inventing localities published more GenAI patent families in the last two years than in the entire previous decade.

## What This Data Means

For those dealing with IP, three insights stand out:

**1. Generative AI has become a cross-cutting patent theme.** It's not just tech companies filing — banks (Ping An), telecommunications, and infrastructure are at the forefront. This broadens the scope of prior art that must be considered in any patent search involving AI.

**2. The window for novelty is narrow.** With 56,000 families published in two years, the chance of a GenAI invention encountering prior art has grown dramatically. Careful prior art searches have never been more critical before filing.

**3. The next frontier is innovation.** As WIPO Director-General Daren Tang said, "while the initial impact of GenAI was on content, the next frontier will be its impact on how we innovate".
