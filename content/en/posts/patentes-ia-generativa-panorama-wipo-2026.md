The race for patenting generative AI inventions has reached a new milestone: more patent families were published in 2024 and 2025 than in the entire previous decade combined. The figures came from WIPO's (World Intellectual Property Organization) **Technology SPARK** report, released in July 2026, and redefine the global innovation map.

If you work with intellectual property, patents, or innovation, this overview brings together the essential data from the report in one place — a reference guide on where generative AI is being invented, who is filing patents, and at what pace.

## The Explosive Growth of Generative AI Patents

The standard metric for measuring new inventions is the **published patent family**. According to WIPO, the number jumped from approximately **14,000 families in 2023 to over 37,800 in 2025** — practically tripling in two years.

The trend becomes even more impressive when considering the period:

| Period | GenAI patent families published |
|---------|--------------------------------------|
| 2014 to 2023 (entire decade) | < 56,000 cumulative |
| 2024 and 2025 (just two years) | > 56,000 |
| 2023 | ~14,000 |
| 2025 | > 37,800 |

In other words, **over 56,000 generative AI patent families were published in 2024 and 2025 alone** — surpassing the cumulative output from 2014 to 2023. Generative AI now accounts for **8.7% of all AI-related patent family publications**, up from 6.1% in 2023 and just 4.2% in 2017.

## Who Leads GenAI Patent Filings

The report reveals that the protection of generative inventions is no longer solely the domain of technology companies. The ranking is led by large multinationals from various sectors:

1. **SoftBank (Japan)** — largest global filer, with almost **3,000 families** published in 2024-2025
2. **Tencent Holdings (China)**
3. **Ping An Insurance Group (China)**
4. **Baidu (China)**

The top 10 is completed by American companies **Alphabet, Microsoft, and IBM**, as well as research institutions and infrastructure companies. Six of the world's ten largest filers are Chinese.

## China Dominates; Japan Grows Fastest

China remains the **world's largest source** of GenAI patent publications, with over **43,000 families** from Chinese inventors published in 2024-2025 — a substantial lead over other countries. The Chinese compound annual growth rate (CAGR) between 2023 and 2025 was **64%**.

The relative highlight, however, is **Japan**, which recorded the highest growth rate among major countries: a CAGR of **210%**, climbing from fourth to third global position. The United States noted a **92%** CAGR in the same period, as the commercial development of GenAI by American companies translated into accelerated patenting.

| Country | GenAI family CAGR (2023-2025) | Position |
|------|------------------------------------|---------|
| Japan | 210% | 3rd |
| Switzerland | 124% | top 10 |
| Canada | 109% | top 10 |
| USA | 92% | 2nd |
| Germany | 85% | European leader |
| China | 64% | 1st |

**Germany surpassed the United Kingdom** as Europe's leading inventive locality, with an 85% CAGR. Seven of the ten largest inventive localities in the world published more GenAI patent families in the last two years than in the entire previous decade.

## What This Data Means

For those dealing with IP, three insights stand out:

**1. Generative AI has become a transversal patent topic.** It's not just tech companies filing — banks (Ping An), telecommunications, and infrastructure are on the front lines. This broadens the scope of prior art that must be considered in any patent search involving AI.

**2. The window of novelty is narrow.** With 56,000 families published in two years, the chance of a GenAI invention encountering prior art has grown dramatically. Careful prior art searches have never been more critical before filing.

**3. The next frontier is innovation.** As WIPO Director-General Daren Tang stated, "while GenAI's initial impact was on content, the next frontier will be its impact on how we innovate."
