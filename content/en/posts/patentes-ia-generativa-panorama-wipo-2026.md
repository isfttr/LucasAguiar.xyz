The race to protect generative AI inventions has reached a new milestone: more patent families were published in 2024 and 2025 than in the entire preceding decade combined. The numbers come from WIPO's **Technology SPARK** report, released in July 2026, and they redraw the global innovation map.

If you work with intellectual property, patents or innovation, this landscape brings the essential data from the report into one place — a reference guide on where generative AI is being invented, who is filing patents and at what pace.

## The explosive growth of GenAI patents

The standard metric for measuring new inventions is the **published patent family**. According to WIPO, the count jumped from roughly **14,000 families in 2023 to more than 37,800 in 2025** — nearly tripling in two years.

The movement is even more striking when summed over time:

| Period | GenAI patent families published |
|--------|--------------------------------|
| 2014–2023 (entire decade) | < 56,000 cumulative |
| 2024 and 2025 (only two years) | > 56,000 |
| 2023 | ~14,000 |
| 2025 | > 37,800 |

More than **56,000 generative AI patent families were published in 2024 and 2025 alone**, surpassing the total output of 2014 through 2023. GenAI now accounts for **8.7% of all AI-related patent family publications**, up from 6.1% in 2023 and just 4.2% in 2017.

## Who is leading the GenAI patent race

The report shows that protecting generative inventions is no longer exclusive to tech companies. The ranking is led by large multinationals across diverse sectors:

1. **SoftBank (Japan)** — the world's leading applicant, with nearly **3,000 families** published in 2024-2025
2. **Tencent Holdings (China)**
3. **Ping An Insurance Group (China)**
4. **Baidu (China)**

The US giants **Alphabet, Microsoft and IBM** round out the top 10, along with research institutions and infrastructure companies. Six of the world's top ten applicants are Chinese.

## China dominates; Japan grows the fastest

China remains the **largest single source** of GenAI patent publications worldwide, with more than **43,000 families** from Chinese-based inventors published in 2024-2025 — a substantial margin over all other countries. China's compound annual growth rate (CAGR) between 2023 and 2025 was **64%**.

The relative highlight, however, is **Japan**, which posted the highest growth rate among leading countries: a **210% CAGR**, rising from fourth to third place globally. The United States recorded a **92% CAGR** over the same period, as commercial GenAI development by American firms translated into accelerating patent activity.

| Country | GenAI family CAGR (2023-2025) | Rank |
|---------|-------------------------------|------|
| Japan | 210% | 3rd |
| Switzerland | 124% | top 10 |
| Canada | 109% | top 10 |
| USA | 92% | 2nd |
| Germany | 85% | European leader |
| China | 64% | 1st |

**Germany overtook the United Kingdom** as Europe's leading inventor location, with an 85% CAGR. Seven of the world's top ten inventor locations published more GenAI patent families in the past two years than in the entire preceding decade.

## What these numbers mean

For IP professionals, three takeaways stand out:

**1. GenAI has become a cross-cutting patent topic.** It is not just tech companies filing — banks (Ping An), telecom and infrastructure are at the forefront. This broadens the prior-art scope that must be considered in any AI-related patent search.

**2. The novelty window is narrow.** With 56,000 families published in two years, the chance of a GenAI invention hitting prior art has grown dramatically. Careful prior-art searches have never been more critical before filing.

**3. The next frontier is innovation itself.** As WIPO Director General Daren Tang put it, "while the initial impact of GenAI has been on content, the next frontier will be in its impact on the way we innovate."
