Any contract involving the licensing of industrial property rights, technology supply, technical assistance services, or franchising **must be registered with Brazil's INPI** (National Institute of Industrial Property) to be enforceable against third parties. Without registration, royalties are not tax-deductible, franchise agreements lack _erga omnes_ validity, and the company is exposed in legal disputes.

This guide covers the complete step-by-step process for registering technology transfer contracts at the INPI in 2026: contract types, required documents, costs, timelines, and the new rules effective since December 2025.

## What are technology transfer contracts

Brazil's Industrial Property Law (Law 9.279/96), Articles 211 through 216, establishes that licensing, technology supply, and franchise agreements **must be registered with the INPI** to produce effects against third parties. In practice, this covers:

- **Patent licensing** (Art. 61) — authorization to exploit an invention patent (PI) or utility model (MU)
- **Industrial design licensing** (Art. 121) — use of an industrial design registration
- **Trademark licensing** (Art. 139) — exploitation of a registered trademark
- **Technology supply** — transfer of non-patented technical knowledge (know-how)
- **Technical assistance services** — specialized technical service agreements
- **Business franchising** (Law 13.966/2019) — franchise system using trademarks and know-how

Registration at the INPI is a condition for the contract to produce effects against third parties (Art. 212 of the IPL) — without it, the agreement is valid only between the signing parties but cannot be asserted against creditors, tax authorities, or competitors.

## Why register the contract at the INPI?

| Consequence | Registered | Not registered |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| Effect against third parties | Yes (Art. 212 IPL) | No |
| Royalty tax deductibility | Yes | No |
| Proof of trademark use (cancellation defense) | Licensed use counts | Does not count |
| Cross-border royalty remittance | Authorized by Central Bank | Blocked |
| Evidentiary weight in court | Enforceable against third parties | Valid between parties only |
| Tax authority compliance | Compliant | Risk of penalty |

For companies paying royalties abroad, INPI registration is a prerequisite for Central Bank authorization of cross-border remittances. The Brazilian Federal Revenue Service requires registration to accept royalty deductibility as an operating expense.

## Contract types accepted by the INPI

The INPI classifies technology contracts into five main modalities, each with its own service code and fee:

### 1. IP rights licensing

Agreements authorizing third parties to exploit:

- **Patents** (PI and MU) — voluntary or compulsory licensing
- **Trademarks** — licensing with or without assignment
- **Industrial designs** — registration licensing
- **Integrated circuit topographies**

### 2. Technology supply

Transfer of technical knowledge, know-how, manufacturing processes, formulas, or non-patentable specifications. This is the modality used when the contract's subject matter does not fit into registered IP licensing.

### 3. Technical assistance services

Contracts for specialized technical services: engineering projects, consulting, training, assembly supervision, maintenance. The INPI requires the contract to clearly specify the scope, duration, and technical qualifications of the provider.

### 4. Business franchising

Governed by Law 13.966/2019 (Franchise Law), franchise agreements involve trademark licensing + know-how + business model transfer. The INPI registers franchise agreements that have been properly structured under the Franchise Law.

### 5. Assignment

Definitive transfer of IP rights ownership. Unlike licensing, the assignor transfers all rights, not merely authorizes use.

## New rules (effective December 2025)

New regulations on technology contract registration came into effect on December 12, 2025. Key changes include:

- **Fully digital process** — e-Contratos replaced physical filing
- **Reduced deadlines** for office actions (from 60 to 30 days)
- **Simplified documentation** for trademark licensing with pre-registered parties
- **New service codes** in the Fee Table
- **Partial registration allowed** — contracts can be registered even with minor pending issues

Contracts registered under the old rules should be reviewed for any required complementation.

## Step-by-step: how to register a technology transfer contract

### Step 1: Prepare the documentation

Before starting, gather:

- **Signed contract** by all parties (digital or physical)
- **Power of attorney** from the legal representative, if applicable
- **GRU payment receipt** (to be issued in step 2)
- **Party documents** (company registration or individual ID)
- **IP registration certificate** (trademark, patent, or industrial design), if applicable

The INPI provides a [Contract Manual](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/contratos-de-tecnologia-e-de-franquia) with detailed checklists for each modality.

### Step 2: Issue and pay the GRU (Federal Payment Guide)

1. Access [e-INPI](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/e-inpi) and log in with your gov.br account
2. Navigate to "GRU Issuance"
3. Select the corresponding service code (see table above)
4. Enter the applicant's CNPJ/CPF
5. Generate the GRU and pay at any bank (barcode payment)

GRU payment is a prerequisite for filing the request. Keep the GRU number — it will be required in the e-Contratos form.

### Step 3: Access e-Contratos and fill out the form

1. Access the [e-Contratos](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/contratos-de-tecnologia-e-de-franquia) system
2. Log in as "Attorney or Individual with Power of Attorney"
3. Select "New Request"
4. Choose the contract modality (licensing, technology supply, franchising, etc.)
5. Enter:
   - Contracting party information
   - Contract subject matter (clear description of what is being licensed)
   - Financial clauses (royalties, payment terms)
   - Term/validity period
   - Paid GRU number
6. Upload scanned documents (contract, power of attorney, certificates)
7. Submit the request

The generated protocol number is the reference for tracking.

### Step 4: Monitor the process

The INPI publishes the Industrial Property Gazette (RPI) weekly on Tuesdays with decisions on contract processes. You can monitor in two ways:

1. **Process search:** use the [public search tool](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/contratos-de-tecnologia-e-de-franquia/busca) by application number, protocol, party name, or CPF/CNPJ
2. **My Requests:** in the e-Contratos platform, add the process to "My Requests" to receive email notifications for each update

The INPI may request additional documents — it is essential to monitor the RPI weekly and respond to office actions within the deadline (30 days under the new rules).

### Step 5: Receive the registration certificate

Upon approval, the INPI issues the **Registration Certificate**, which should be kept with the original contract. This certificate proves enforceability against third parties and enables tax deductibility of royalties.

## Average processing times

The INPI does not publish a specific target time for technology contracts, but the average observed in 2025-2026 is:

| Modality | Average time |
|----------|-------------|
| Trademark licensing | 30-60 days |
| Patent licensing | 60-120 days |
| Technology supply | 60-90 days |
| Technical assistance | 30-60 days |
| Franchising | 45-90 days |
| Assignment | 30-60 days |

Contracts with complete documentation and well-filled forms tend to be processed faster. Unanswered office actions are the main cause of delays.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to register the contract before starting to pay royalties?**

Yes. INPI registration is a prerequisite for royalty tax deductibility and cross-border remittance. Ideally, file before payments begin.

**My contract is in a foreign language. Do I need a translation?**

Yes. Contracts in foreign languages must be accompanied by a sworn translation into Portuguese.

**What happens if the contract is not registered?**

The contract remains valid between the parties, but does not produce effects against third parties. Royalties paid without registration may be disallowed by tax authorities, and the company lacks protection in IP disputes.

**Does a trademark licensing registration prevent cancellation for non-use?**

Yes. A registered trademark licensing agreement counts as effective use of the mark, serving as a defense in cancellation proceedings. See the [complete guide to trademark cancellation in Brazil]({{< relref "posts/trademark-cancellation-non-use-brazil-inpi-guide/" >}}).

## Conclusion

Technology transfer contract registration at the INPI is a mandatory procedure that many companies neglect — whether due to lack of knowledge or perceived bureaucracy. The consequences of not registering range from tax disallowance to loss of IP rights in legal disputes.

With the digitization of e-Contratos and the 70% discount for SMEs, the registration cost has dropped to around BRL 104 for most modalities. The process, which once required physical presence at the INPI's headquarters, is now 100% digital.

The key recommendation: **register the contract before the first royalty payment.** The cost is low, the process is digital, and the risk of not registering far outweighs the administrative effort.
