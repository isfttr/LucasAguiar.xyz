The prior art search is step zero of any trademark strategy in Brazil — and the most neglected one. Filing a trademark application with INPI without first checking whether the mark already exists is betting against the odds: applications that collide with earlier marks are denied based on Art. 124, item XIX, of the Industrial Property Law (Law No. 9,279/96), which prohibits the registration of a sign that reproduces or imitates a third party's mark for identical, similar, or related products or services, causing confusion.

The cost of the error is not only the lost fee: it is the rework of a new filing, the delay in launching a product, and, in the worst case, infringement of a third party's right of exclusive use (Art. 129 of the IPL), with risk of lawsuit.

The good news: the search can be done for free, without registration, in INPI's official database. This guide shows the complete path — from accessing the system to interpreting the results.

## When to Do the Prior Art Search

Consulting the trademark database is not only used to check whether the name you chose is already registered. It is useful at several moments:

| Scenario | Search type | Objective |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| Before filing the application | Prior art search | Check whether the mark has already been applied for/registered by a third party |
| Before launching a product or service | Collision search | Avoid infringing a third party's trademark in the market |
| Upon receiving an opposition notice | Confrontation search | Assess the strength of the right invoked by the opponent |
| In licensing or M&A negotiations | IP due diligence | Confirm ownership and validity of registrations |
| In portfolio monitoring | Search for identical/similar marks | Detect third-party applications that collide with your mark |

## Where to Do the Search: The pePI System

The official INPI trademark database is in **pePI (Industrial Property Search)**, accessible at [busca.inpi.gov.br](https://busca.inpi.gov.br/pePI/jsp/marcas/Pesquisa_num_processo.jsp) (also known as Busca Web). Access is free and does not require registration: just click **Continue** on the login screen to search anonymously. Creating an account (or logging in with gov.br) provides access to additional services, such as making documents available.

Two important observations about the database:

- The collection covers **documents published from 2000 onwards** (and, for technology contracts, from October 2009).
- For legal purposes, the official publication channel is the **Electronic Industrial Property Journal (RPI)** — the search database is a consultation tool, not a substitute for the RPI for counting deadlines.

## Step-by-Step Basic Search

With the system open, access **Basic Search** (the "Marca" link leads to the same name query). The main fields are:

1. **Search Type: Exact or Radical** — the most important search choice (detailed below).
2. **Mark** — the name you want to check.
3. **Nice Classification (NCL)** — optional, but highly recommended to filter results by product or service class.
4. **Number of Processes per Page** — 20 to 100 results per screen.

### Exact vs. Radical: Which to Use?

- **Exact** returns only records with the word identical to the one searched. Useful when you want to know if that specific mark exists.
- **Radical** returns all records containing the searched term in any position — including combinations and variations. It is the recommended search for prior art, because it captures similar marks, not just identical ones.

To understand the difference in practice: a **radical** search for "natura" returned, in August 2026, **19,907 records** — from "VIVA NATURAL" to "NATURANA" and "NATURAL LOOK". An exact search for "natura" returns only the processes with the mark exactly "NATURA". For prior art, the radical search is almost always the right choice: the collision that INPI examines includes similarity, not just identity.

### Filtering by Nice Class

Marks are registered by product and service classes of the [Nice Classification](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/classificacao-marcas) (NCL). Two identical marks can coexist if they are in different classes and there is no affinity between the products. Therefore, searching without filtering by class generates noise: thousands of processes in classes irrelevant to your case.

The basic search allows you to fill in the class directly. The result shows the class in which each process was filed, in the format `NCL(8) 31`, `NCL(12) 32`, etc. — the number in parentheses indicates the edition of the classification used at filing (older records appear in legacy format, such as `03 : 20`).

### Other Search Options

In addition to searching by name, pePI allows you to query by:

- **Owner** — locate all processes of a company or person.
- **Fig. Code** — search for figurative elements of mixed and figurative marks (relevant for marks with logos).
- **Process No., GRU, Protocol, or International Registration** — locate a specific process when you already have the number.

## How to Interpret the Results

Each result row contains: **Number** (of the process), **Priority**, the **type** of mark (word mark, mixed or figurative), the **name** of the mark, the **status** with details, the **owner**, and the **class**. The status is the most important field:

| Displayed status | What it means |
|------------------|-----------------|
| Registered Mark — Mark registration in force | Registration granted and in force: blocks collision |
| Registered Mark — Extinct registration | Was registered, but the registration lapsed or was extinguished: may clear the way |
| Archived Mark — Application definitively archived | Application was archived: generates no right |
| Archived Mark — Extinct | Process closed without registration |
| Under examination / Published | Pending application: may still become a registration |

A detail that escapes first-time searchers: **an extinct registration does not block forever** — extinction due to lapse, surrender, or non-payment may open space for a new registration. Reading the status correctly avoids both false positives (thinking everything is blocked) and false negatives (thinking the path is clear when there is an earlier pending application under examination).

## Search Limitations and Best Practices

The pePI search is indispensable, but it has limits that professional analysis covers:

1. **Collision is not just identity.** INPI examines phonetic, visual, and conceptual similarity, as well as affinity between products. Search variations: plurals, translations, phonetic versions ("Pharma" and "Farma", "K" and "C").
2. **Non-obvious classes.** The risk of collision exists in related classes, not only in the exact class of your product. An affinity analysis between classes is part of the examination.
3. **Well-known and highly renowned marks.** [Highly renowned](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/alto-renome/) and well-known marks (Arts. 125 and 126 of the IPL) have expanded protection, outside class logic — an apparently "free" registration may run into them.
4. **The database does not replace the RPI** for legal purposes, and a search result is not a guarantee of registrability: it is an input for decision-making.

The free search handles initial screening. When the result indicates potential conflict — or when the mark has strategic value — a detailed analysis by an IP specialist is worthwhile.

## After the Search: Next Steps

If the search does not point to relevant collision, the filing is done at [e-Marcas](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/e-marcas), with updated costs in the [official fee table](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/custos) (MEI and ME get 50% discount). After filing, monitoring the process — including third-party oppositions — is part of the cycle. ## Conclusion

The prior art search is the cheapest investment in the entire trademark registration process: free, takes minutes, and avoids refusal, rework, and the risk of infringing third-party rights. Mastering pePI — the difference between exact and radical search, the class filter, and reading the status — puts any company in a position to make an informed decision before spending on the filing.
