The clearance search is step zero of any trademark strategy in Brazil — and the most neglected one. Filing a registration application with INPI without first checking whether the trademark already exists is betting against the odds: applications that conflict with prior trademarks are rejected based on Article 124, item XIX, of the Industrial Property Law (Law No. 9,279/1996), which prohibits the registration of a sign that reproduces or imitates a third party's trademark for identical, similar, or related products or services, causing confusion.

The cost of the mistake is not only the lost fee: it is the rework of a new filing, the delay in launching a product and, in the worst case, the violation of a third party's exclusive right of use (Article 129 of the LPI), with risk of a lawsuit.

The good news: the search can be done for free, without registration, in the official INPI database. This guide shows the complete path — from accessing the system to interpreting the results.

## When to Perform the Clearance Search

Searching the trademark database is not only used to check whether the name you chose is already registered. It is useful at several moments:

| Scenario | Search type | Objective |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Before filing the application | Clearance search | Check whether the trademark has already been applied for/registered by a third party |
| Before launching a product or service | Conflict search | Avoid infringing a third party's trademark in the market |
| Upon receiving an opposition notice | Confrontation search | Assess the strength of the right invoked by the opponent |
| In licensing or M&A negotiations | IP due diligence | Confirm ownership and validity of registrations |
| When monitoring the portfolio | Identical/similar trademark search | Detect third-party applications that conflict with your trademark |

## Where to Do the Search: the pePI System

The official trademark database of INPI is in **pePI (Industrial Property Search)**, accessible at [busca.inpi.gov.br](https://busca.inpi.gov.br/pePI/jsp/marcas/Pesquisa_num_processo.jsp). Access is free and does not require registration: just click **Continue** on the login screen to search anonymously. Creating an account (or logging in with gov.br) provides access to additional services, such as the provision of documents.

Two important observations about the database:

- The collection covers **documents published from the year 2000** (and, for technology contracts, from October 2009).
- For legal purposes, the official publication channel is the **Industrial Property Electronic Journal (RPI)** — the search database is a consultation tool, not a substitute for the RPI for counting deadlines.

## Step-by-Step Basic Search

With the system open, access **Basic Search** (the "Trademark" link leads to the same query by name). The main fields are:

1. **Search Type: Exact or Radical** — the most important choice of the search (detailed below).
2. **Trademark** — the name you want to check.
3. **Nice Classification (NCL)** — optional, but highly recommended for filtering results by product or service class.
4. **Number of Processes per Page** — 20 to 100 results per screen.

### Exact vs. Radical: Which One to Use?

- **Exact** returns only records with the word identical to the one searched. Useful when you want to know whether that specific trademark exists.
- **Radical** returns all records that contain the searched term in any position — including combinations and variations. It is the recommended search for prior rights, because it captures similar trademarks, not just identical ones.

To understand the difference in practice: a **radical** search for "natura" returned, in August 2026, **19,907 processes** — from "VIVA NATURAL" to "NATURANA" and "NATURAL LOOK". An exact search for "natura" returns only the processes with the trademark exactly "NATURA". In a clearance search, the radical option is almost always the right choice: the conflict INPI examines includes similarity, not only identity.

### Filtering by Nice Class

Trademarks are registered by product and service classes of the [Nice Classification](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/classificacao-marcas) (NCL). Two identical trademarks can coexist if they are in different classes and there is no relatedness between the products. Therefore, searching without filtering by class creates noise: thousands of processes in classes irrelevant to your case.

The basic search allows you to fill in the class directly. The result shows the class in which each process was filed, in the format `NCL(8) 31`, `NCL(12) 32`, etc. — the number in parentheses indicates the edition of the classification used in the filing (old records appear in legacy format, such as `03 : 20`).

### Other Query Options

In addition to searching by name, pePI allows queries by:

- **Owner** — locate all processes of a company or individual.
- **Figure Code** — search for figurative elements of mixed and figurative trademarks (relevant for trademarks with logos).
- **Process No., GRU, Protocol, or International Registration** — locate a specific process when you already have the number.

## How to Interpret the Results

Each result line contains: **Number** (of the process), **Priority**, the **type** of trademark (word, mixed, or figurative), the **name** of the trademark, the **status** with details, the **owner**, and the **class**. Status is the most important field:

| Displayed status | What it means |
|------------------|---------------|
| Registered Trademark — Trademark registration in force | Registration granted and in force: blocks conflict |
| Registered Trademark — Registration extinguished | It was already registered, but the registration lapsed or was extinguished: may clear the way |
| Archived Trademark — Application definitively archived | Application has been archived: does not generate rights |
| Archived Trademark — Extinguished | Process ended without registration |
| Under Examination / Published | Application pending: may still become a registration |

A detail that escapes first-time searchers: **an extinguished registration does not block forever** — extinction due to lapse, surrender, or non-payment can open the way for a new registration. Correctly reading the status avoids both false positives (thinking everything is blocked) and false negatives (thinking the path is clear when there is an earlier application under examination).

## Search Limitations and Best Practices

The search in pePI is indispensable, but it has limitations that professional analysis covers:

1. **Conflict is not just identity.** INPI examines phonetic, visual, and conceptual similarity, as well as the relatedness between products. Search for variations: plurals, translations, phonetic versions ("Pharma" and "Farma", "K" and "C").
2. **Non-obvious classes.** The risk of conflict exists in related classes, not only in the exact class of your product. An analysis of class relatedness is part of the examination.
3. **Well-known and high-renown marks.** Marks of [high renown](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/alto-renome/) and well-known marks (Articles 125 and 126 of the LPI) have extended protection, outside the logic of class — a seemingly "free" registration can run into them.
4. **The database does not replace the RPI** for legal purposes, and the result of a search is not a guarantee of registrability: it is an input for decision-making.

The free search performs the initial screening. When the result indicates a potential conflict — or when the trademark has strategic value — a detailed analysis by an IP specialist is worthwhile.

## After the Search: Next Steps

If the search does not indicate a relevant conflict, the filing is done at [e-Marcas](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/e-marcas), with updated costs in the [official fee table](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/custos) (MEI and ME get a 50% discount). After filing, monitoring the process — including third-party oppositions — is part of the cycle. ## Conclusion

The clearance search is the cheapest investment in the entire trademark registration process: it is free, takes minutes, and avoids rejection, rework, and the risk of infringing third-party rights. Mastering pePI — the difference between exact and radical search, the class filter, and reading the status — puts any company in a position to make an informed decision before spending on the filing.
