You built a product — or an improvement to something that already exists — and now you have to decide: file as a **patent of invention (PI)** or as a **utility model (MU)**? Choosing wrong can cost you 5 years of protection, unnecessary fees, or a rejected application that demanded a level of inventiveness your object simply does not have.

The good news: Brazil's Industrial Property Law (Law No. 9.279/96, the "LPI") defines both institutes clearly, and the decision comes down to a few objective questions. This guide compares the two patent types based on the law, on the INPI fee schedule in force in 2026, and on practical examples — so you file the right type on the first attempt.

## What the law says: Article 8 vs Article 9 of the LPI

The starting point is the statute itself:

> **Art. 8.** A patent shall be granted for an invention that meets the requirements of novelty, inventive activity and industrial application.

> **Art. 9.** A utility model shall be patentable when the object of practical use, or part thereof, susceptible of industrial application, presents a new form or arrangement, involving inventive act, that results in functional improvement in its use or manufacture.

Note the core difference: an invention protects **products and processes** that are new, with **inventive activity** (non-obvious to a person skilled in the art). A utility model protects only the **form or arrangement** of an object of practical use — and requires only an **inventive act**, a lower inventive threshold, as long as it results in a **functional improvement** in use or manufacture.

In practice, the MU is the classic "improvement" protection: the object already exists, but you found a new form or arrangement that makes it more functional. For an overview of the other protection types (industrial design, trademark, software, plant variety), see our [guide to intellectual property protection types in Brazil]({{< relref "posts/ip-protection-types-brazil-complete-guide/" >}}).

## Comparison table: PI vs MU

| Criterion | Patent of Invention (PI) | Utility Model (MU) |
|-----------|--------------------------|--------------------|
| **Legal basis** | Art. 8 of the LPI | Art. 9 of the LPI |
| **Protected subject matter** | Product or process | Form or arrangement of an object of practical use (functional improvement) |
| **Inventive requirement** | Inventive activity (non-obvious to a skilled person) | Inventive act (lower threshold) |
| **Term** | 20 years from filing | 15 years from filing |
| **Renewable?** | No | No |
| **Filing fee (individual/ME/EPP/ICT)** | R$ 95 | R$ 75 |
| **Filing fee (other legal entities)** | R$ 285 | R$ 220 |

| **Substantive examination** | Required, generally more complex | Required, tends to be simpler |
| **Can it protect processes?** | Yes | No |

## How to decide: 4 objective questions

### 1. Is it a process, method or composition?

If your creation is a manufacturing process, a method, a chemical compound or a composition, the answer is direct: **utility models do not protect processes**. Article 9 requires an "object of practical use" — something physical, with form. Processes can only be protected by a patent of invention.

### 2. Is the object radically new?

If nothing similar exists — a new product category, an unprecedented mechanism, a technical solution with no precedent — the natural path is the **patent of invention**, with its full inventive-activity requirement and 20 years of protection.

### 3. Is it a functional improvement to a known object?

If the object already exists and your contribution is a **new form or arrangement** that improves use or manufacture — a more ergonomic handle, a more efficient seal, an arrangement of parts that simplifies assembly — the **utility model** is generally the right choice. You pay less, face a less complex examination, and the inventiveness requirement is lower.

### 4. How long do you need protection?

Term is the final trade-off: 20 years (PI) versus 15 years (MU), both counted from the filing date (Art. 40 of the LPI), with no renewal. For products with short life cycles — consumer goods, utensils, packaging — the MU's 15 years are usually enough. For long-cycle technologies (pharmaceuticals, industrial equipment, telecom), the PI pays off.

## Practical examples

| Example | Best type | Why |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| New tool handle with anatomical grip | MU | Form improvement on a known object |
| Bottle cap with a new sealing system | MU | New arrangement that improves functionality |
| Folding mechanism in a chair | MU | Arrangement of parts with functional improvement |
| New chemical compound or alloy | PI | New product with full inventive activity |
| Manufacturing method for a component | PI | Process — MU does not protect processes |
| Electronic circuit with novel architecture | PI | Radically new technical solution |

A single product can combine several protections: a bottle can have an **MU** (functional cap), an **industrial design** (ornamental shape) and a **trademark** (name). The most common confusion is between MU and industrial design: design protects **aesthetic appearance**, while the MU protects the **functionality** of the form. See our [industrial design registration guide at INPI]({{< relref "posts/industrial-design-registration-brazil-inpi-2026-guide/" >}}) for the full distinction.

## Costs in 2026: how much does each path cost?

The figures below follow the INPI Patent Services Fee Schedule (INPI/PR Ordinance No. 12 of 2025, in force since December 20, 2025). Individuals, MEIs, small companies (ME/EPP) pay 50% of the value on most services.

| Cost | PI | MU |
|------|----|----|
| Filing (individual/ME/EPP/ICT) | R$ 95 | R$ 75 |
| Filing (other legal entities) | R$ 285 | R$ 220 |
| Total annuities (individual/ME/EPP) | ~R$ 9,800 | ~R$ 4,900 |
| Total annuities (other legal entities) | ~R$ 19,600 | ~R$ 9,800 |

The annuity difference is the heaviest part of the decision: maintaining an MU costs **half** of maintaining a PI over its lifetime. Because annuities become due as early as the 3rd year from filing (Art. 84 of the LPI) — even while the application is still in the examination queue — it pays to project this cost from the start. Deadlines, GRU codes and restoration rules are detailed in our [complete patent annuity guide]({{< relref "posts/guia-anuidades-patentes-inpi-prazos-valores-2026/" >}}).

## The filing process is the same

The filing flow is identical for PI and MU: prior art search → application filing in the **e-Patentes** system → GRU payment → publication → substantive examination → grant. The difference lies in the application form (type of filing) and the technical content — MU claims must demonstrate the functional improvement of the form, while PI claims must demonstrate full inventive activity.

The complete step-by-step is in our [guide to filing a patent at INPI]({{< relref "posts/how-to-file-patent-inpi-2026-guide/" >}}). Before filing, run the prior art search — the MU also requires novelty, and lack of it is a frequent cause of rejection. See how to hire or run [patent prior art searches]({{< relref "posts/inpi-credenciamento-busca-anterioridade-patentes/" >}}) and how to use the [International Patent Classification (IPC)]({{< relref "posts/classificacao-internacional-patentes-ipc-guia-brasil/" >}}) in your application.

## Common mistakes when choosing

1. **Filing as PI something that is an MU** — the application faces the full inventive-activity requirement and may be rejected, on top of higher fees. If the contribution is a form improvement, the MU is the right route.
2. **Filing as MU something that is a PI** — protection is limited to the claimed form; a competitor can design around it with another form or a different process. Product/process inventions lose scope as an MU.
3. **Believing an MU protects processes** — it does not. Processes require a PI.
4. **Ignoring novelty for the MU** — the inventive act is lower, but novelty is required in full. Without a prior art search, the risk of rejection is high.
5. **Confusing MU with industrial design** — one protects functionality, the other appearance. For purely aesthetic creations, industrial design is the right route.

## Conclusion

Choosing between a patent of invention and a utility model comes down to three questions: **is it a process?** (PI), **is it a radically new object?** (PI), **is it a functional form improvement?** (MU). When in doubt, a credentialed industrial property agent can assess your specific case — that consulting cost is small next to the cost of filing the wrong type and losing 5 years of protection, or the application entirely.
