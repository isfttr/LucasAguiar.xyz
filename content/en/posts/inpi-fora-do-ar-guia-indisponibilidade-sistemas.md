On Monday, August 3, 2026, INPI’s electronic services were unavailable for most of the day due to a technical incident. Anyone who needed to file a patent, submit an appeal, or track a proceeding found systems offline — and the question that always arises at such times is: *what about my deadlines?*

This was not an isolated situation. In June 2026, the computer program filing system was unstable for more than a week (June 20–30). Outages, maintenance, and instabilities are recurring in INPI’s digital infrastructure — and knowing how to act before, during, and after them protects rights that can be lost by a few minutes of system downtime.

This guide consolidates what INPI officially does when systems go down, how deadlines are handled, and the step-by-step process to recover protocols and minimize risks.

## What INPI officially does when systems become unavailable

When a technical incident brings down electronic services, INPI follows a documented protocol:

1. **Publishes a notice on the Portal** — the unavailability news is published on INPI’s Content Center, informing the problem and the responsible team. In the case of August 3, the [notice](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias/servicos-do-inpi-estao-temporariamente-indisponiveis-em-3-8) was published at 2:51 PM, attributing the response to the General Coordination of Information Technology (CGTI).

2. **Restores and publishes the normalization** — as soon as services return, a new notice is released. [Electronic services were normalized](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias/servicos-eletronicos-do-inpi-ja-estao-normalizados) still on the same day, August 3, at 8:43 PM.

3. **Issues the Technical Unavailability Report** — for unavailability exceeding 60 minutes, CGTI publishes a formal report based on INPI/PR Ordinance No. 049, of December 3, 2021, and Decree No. 8,854, of September 22, 2016. The [report on the August 3, 2026 occurrence](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias/servicos-eletronicos-do-inpi-ja-estao-normalizados/Relatriodeindisponibilidadetcnica20260803.pdf) (PDF) officially documents that services were unavailable for more than 60 minutes.

This report is the **official documentary evidence** of the unavailability period. Keep the link and the date: it is the document you will attach in any petition, appeal, or extrajudicial notice if a deadline is reached during the unavailability window.

## What about deadlines? Does INPI automatically extend them?

**There is no automatic extension of deadlines due to technical unavailability.** This is the most important answer in this guide — and the one that causes the most harm when misunderstood.

What does exist is a specific mechanism: when INPI decides that an unavailability (or prolonged instability) justifies suspending or reopening deadlines, it publishes an **official notice in Section I (Notices) of the Industrial Property Magazine (RPI)**, which is the Institute’s official publication vehicle.

In practice, what is observed:

| Situation | What happens to deadlines |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| Short unavailability (restored the same day) | Deadlines follow the normal flow; there is no extension notice. This was the case on August 3, 2026: [RPI 2900, of August 4, 2026](https://revistas.inpi.gov.br/pdf/Comunicados2900.pdf) does not include a suspension notice related to the occurrence |
| Prolonged instability (days) | INPI publishes specific guidance — usually as a news item + notice in the RPI. This was the case with the computer program filing instability (June 20–30, 2026) |
| Deadline falling exactly on the day of unavailability | There is no automatic rule. Document the unavailability (report + screenshots) and file on the next business day, attaching the evidence |

**Golden rule:** never assume a deadline was extended. Check the RPI (published on Tuesdays) and the portal’s news. If the deadline expired during an unavailability and there was no notice, file immediately after normalization with as much documentation as possible that the system was offline.

### The June/2026 precedent: protocol recovery via GRU

The instability of the computer program filing system between June 20 and 30, 2026, set an important practical precedent. When the system was back up on July 1, [INPI instructed](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias/sistema-de-peticionamento-de-programa-de-computador-volta-a-funcionar) that users who filed during the instability and **did not receive the final protocol** should:

1. Access the filing area;
2. Enter the number of the **Union Collection Guide (GRU)** used in the request;
3. Download the protocol.

This reinforces the importance of **keeping the GRU number** even when the petition is not completed. The GRU is the official payment trail — without it, it is much harder to prove that the filing attempt existed.

## Contingency checklist: before, during, and after

### Before (prevention)

- [ ] **Don’t leave deadlines for the last day.** INPI has deadlines in calendar days, but the actual useful window ends when the system closes or crashes. For critical acts (filing, appeal, compliance with an office action), anticipate by 1–2 business days.
- [ ] **Always save the GRU number** and the payment receipt, even if the filing fails.
- [ ] **Keep screenshots of the screens** when the system displays an error while attempting to file.
- [ ] Know the [INPI service channels](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/canais-de-atendimento) before you need them.

### During

- [ ] Check whether the unavailability is general (portal, all systems) or specific (one module). The official news on the INPI Portal is the reliable source.
- [ ] Try filing at alternative times — partial instabilities are often intermittent.
- [ ] Document: save the official notice, the time, and screenshots of the errors.
- [ ] Follow the official channels (INPI news portal and social media) to know when services return.

### After

- [ ] Check the latest RPI (Section I) to see whether there was any notice about deadlines.
- [ ] If the protocol was not issued but the GRU was paid: access the filing area, enter the GRU number, and download the protocol (procedure from the June/2026 precedent).
- [ ] If a deadline was missed during the unavailability and there was no notice: file immediately with the Technical Unavailability Report attached and a justification.
- [ ] Record in meeting minutes or an internal spreadsheet the impact of the unavailability on your docket.

## Where to monitor system status

| Source | What to monitor | Frequency |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| [INPI News Portal](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias) | Unavailability and normalization notices | In real time during the occurrence |
| [RPI — Section I (Notices)](https://revistas.inpi.gov.br/) | Notices of suspension/reopening of deadlines | Every Tuesday (weekly publication) |
| [INPI Service Channels](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/canais-de-atendimento) | Confirmation of procedures if in doubt | On demand |
| Technical Unavailability Report | Official document of the occurrence (INPI/PR Ordinance 049/2021) | Published after normalization, for unavailability > 60 min |

## Conclusion

Technical unavailability is part of INPI’s operational reality — and the right response is never panic, it’s procedure. The Institute officially documents every relevant occurrence (notice on the portal + Technical Unavailability Report), but **does not extend deadlines automatically**: the extension, when it exists, comes through a notice in the RPI.

Risk management, therefore, rests with the holder and the IP professional: anticipate critical deadlines, preserve the GRU number, document filing attempts, and monitor the RPI after each occurrence. These are simple practices that prevent the loss of rights — and that pay off the first time an unavailability hits an important deadline.
