A patent is, in practice, a right to prevent: without enforcement mechanisms, the INPI certificate is worth only as a decorative piece. When a third party manufactures, sells, or imports a protected product without authorization, the holder needs to know the judicial path — the patent infringement action — and the accused needs to know the available defenses.

This topic is no longer rare in Brazil. The Brazilian chapter of the Patent Litigation Review 2027, by IAM (Intellectual Asset Management), points to an increase in patent litigation cases in the country in sectors such as telecommunications, medtech, and artificial intelligence. This guide consolidates the legal basis of the Industrial Property Law (Law No. 9.279/96), the applicable civil procedure, and the practical strategies of those who want to enforce — or defend against — a patent in Brazil.

## What the patent grants to the holder

The starting point is [art. 42 of the LPI](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm): the patent grants the holder the right to prevent third parties, without consent, from **producing, using, offering for sale, selling, or importing**:

- a product that is the object of a patent; or
- a process, or a product obtained directly by a patented process.

Two details of the same article often go unnoticed:

1. **Contributory infringement (§ 1):** the holder can also prevent those who *contribute* to others performing the acts — anyone who supplies an essential component of a patented product or equipment to carry out a patented process may be liable.
2. **Reversal of the burden of proof in process patents (§ 2):** if the patent protects a manufacturing process, the possessor of the product must prove, upon specific judicial determination, that the product was obtained by a process different from the protected one.

It is worth emphasizing: the right is to *prevent unauthorized use for commercial or industrial purposes*. The patent is not an exploitation license — it is a barrier against third parties. For those who do not yet have the patent, the path begins earlier, with the [filing at the INPI]({{< relref "posts/guia-deposito-patente-inpi-2026/" >}}).

## When there is no infringement: the limits of art. 43

Not every use of patented technology is infringement. Art. 43 of the LPI lists the cases in which the holder's right does not apply — the most relevant in practice:

| Item | Case | Practical example |
|--------|----------|-----------------|
| I | Acts of a private nature and **without commercial purpose**, without prejudice to the holder's economic interest | Domestic use of a patented mechanism |
| II | Acts with **experimental purpose**, related to scientific/technological studies or research | Laboratory test to develop one's own product |
| IV | **Exhaustion:** product placed on the domestic market by the holder or with their consent | Resale of a product originally sold by the holder |
| V and VI | Use of living matter without economic purpose | Research with patented cultivar or microorganism |

Item IV deserves special attention: it is the legal consecration of the *exhaustion* of the right — after the holder places the product on the domestic market (directly or through a licensee), the holder cannot prevent the resale of that specific unit. Infringement analysis, in any case, is always carried out **claim by claim**: the accused product or process is compared with each patent claim, on pain of the violation being partial or nonexistent.

## The judicial path: infringement action

### Jurisdiction

The patent infringement action is filed in **State Court** (venue of the defendant's domicile, the place of damage, or the establishment). The relevant exception: when the dispute involves the validity of the patent with INPI as a party — the nullity action — jurisdiction lies with **Federal Court**, because INPI is a federal autarchy.

### Before filing

- **Extrajudicial notice (cease & desist):** many disputes end before litigation, especially when the infringer is a reseller unaware of the patent.
- **Verification expert report:** produced in advance (early production of evidence, arts. 381 and following of the CPC), documents the violation before the defendant destroys evidence.
- **Validity analysis:** enforcing a weak patent may lead to damages for bad-faith litigation; it is worth auditing the claims beforehand.

### Preliminary relief: the decisive moment

The [CPC/2015](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2015/lei/l13105.htm) gives the holder two fast tools:

- **Urgent relief (art. 300):** requires probability of the right + danger of damage. In patents, the "probability" is reinforced when the holder presents a prior technical report.
- **Evidentiary relief (art. 311):** dispenses with the danger of damage when the right is documented and there is abuse of the right of defense.

The LPI adds to the CPC in art. 209, § 1: the judge may, **before service of process on the defendant**, grant a preliminary injunction **to halt the infringement**, requiring a cash deposit or surety guarantee if deemed necessary. Typical precautionary measures: search and seizure of products, production of accounting documents, and attachment.

### Technical expert examination: the heart of the case

In patent disputes, evidence is essentially technical. The expert compares the accused product/process with the claims, applies the doctrine of equivalents, and answers whether there was infringement. The choice of a qualified technical assistant and the formulation of the expert questions define, in practice, the outcome — more than legal argumentation.

## The defendant's defense

Anyone who receives an infringement action has a specific arsenal:

1. **Nullity as a defense (art. 56, § 1, LPI):** the nullity of the patent may be raised at any time, including incidentally in the infringement action — and the judge may suspend the effects of the patent preventively or incidentally (§ 2). It is the strongest defense: once the patent is annulled, the infringement action falls.
2. **Autonomous nullity action (art. 56):** any person with a legitimate interest, or INPI itself, may request nullity at any time during the term. It proceeds in Federal Court, with INPI as defendant.
3. **Art. 43 exceptions:** exhaustion, experimental use, private use without commercial purpose (see table above).
4. **Limitation (art. 225):** the action for damages to industrial property rights is time-barred after **5 (five) years**. If the holder delayed, the preliminary defense defeats the damages claim.
5. **Absence of proof of infringement:** the burden is on the holder (except for reversal in process patents).

## Damages: how they are calculated

Art. 210 of the LPI adopts the criterion **most favorable to the injured party** among three alternatives:

| Criterion | Description |
|----------|-----------|
| I | Benefits the injured party **would have earned** if the infringement had not occurred |
| II | Benefits **earned by the infringer** |
| III | Remuneration the infringer **would have paid for a license** (reasonable royalty) |

The holder may also seek damages for exploitation occurring **between the publication of the application and the grant of the patent** (art. 44) — limited to the content of the application as published (art. 41). In practice, criterion III (royalties) is the most used when the holder does not directly exploit; criterion II requires an accounting expert report on the infringer's profit.

## The criminal sphere: crimes against patents

The LPI criminalizes crimes against invention patents and utility models:

- **Art. 183:** manufacturing a patented product or using a patented means/process without authorization — detention from 3 months to 1 year or a fine.
- **Art. 184:** exporting, selling, offering for sale, keeping in stock, concealing, or importing a product manufactured in violation — detention from 1 to 3 months or a fine.
- **Art. 185:** supplying a component of a patented product or material/equipment to carry out a patented process.
- **Art. 186:** the violation is characterized even if it does not reach all claims or uses equivalent means.

Strategic point: crimes against patents are subject to **private criminal prosecution (criminal complaint)** — art. 199 requires the initiative of the holder, except for the crime of art. 191. Many holders use the criminal route as a negotiation lever: the threat of a criminal complaint and criminal search and seizure often accelerate settlements that civil litigation would take years to produce.

## In practice: strategy and trends

- **The winning sequence:** verification (prior expert report) → notice → preliminary relief → court expert report → judgment. Each step has its own cost and risk; preliminary relief is the moment when the dispute usually gets resolved.
- **SEP/FRAND:** the wave of standard-essential patent litigation (telecommunications) has brought to Brazil discussions on FRAND licensing, injunctions, and *anti-suit injunctions* — the IAM chapter confirms that the country has entered this circuit.
- **Artificial intelligence:** with the accelerated growth of AI patent families (WIPO 2026 data), enforcement of these patents — often with method claims — is beginning to reach the courts, testing the limits of art. 43.
- **Alternatives to litigation:** cross-licensing agreements, mediation, and arbitration are gaining ground in disputes between companies that want to avoid years of litigation.

The final message is symmetrical: for the holder, the patent only has value if there is an enforcement plan; for those who produce with third-party technology, the freedom-to-operate audit is cheaper than any judicial defense. On both sides, knowledge of art. 42 to art. 225 of the LPI is the first asset.
