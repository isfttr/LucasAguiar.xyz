A patent is, in practice, a right to prevent: without enforcement mechanisms, the INPI certificate is worth only as a decorative piece. When a third party manufactures, sells or imports a protected product without authorization, the holder needs to know the judicial path — the patent infringement action — and the accused needs to know the available defenses.

The topic is no longer rare in Brazil. The Brazilian chapter of the 2027 Patent Litigation Review, by IAM (Intellectual Asset Management), points to an increase in patent litigation cases in the country in sectors such as telecommunications, medtech and artificial intelligence. This guide consolidates the legal basis of the Industrial Property Law (Law No. 9.279/96), the applicable civil procedure and the practical strategies of those who want to enforce — or defend against — a patent in Brazil.

## What the patent grants to the holder

The starting point is [art. 42 of the LPI](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm): the patent grants the holder the right to prevent third parties, without consent, from **producing, using, offering for sale, selling or importing**:

- product that is the subject of a patent; or
- process, or product obtained directly by a patented process.

Two details of the same article often go unnoticed:

1. **Contributory infringement (§ 1):** the holder may also prevent those who *contribute* to others practicing the acts — whoever supplies an essential component of a patented product or equipment to execute a patented process may be held liable.
2. **Reversal of the burden of proof in process patents (§ 2):** if the patent protects a manufacturing process, the product possessor must prove, upon specific court order, that the product was obtained by a process different from the protected one.

It is worth reinforcing: the right is to *prevent unauthorized use for commercial or industrial purposes*. The patent is not an exploitation license — it is a barrier against third parties. For those who do not yet have the patent, the path begins earlier, with the [filing with the INPI]({{< relref "posts/guia-deposito-patente-inpi-2026/" >}}).

## When there is no infringement: the limits of art. 43

Not every use of patented technology is infringement. Art. 43 of the LPI lists hypotheses in which the holder's right does not apply — the most relevant in practice:

| Item | Hypothesis | Practical example |
|------|------------|-------------------|
| I | Acts of a private nature and **without commercial purpose**, without prejudice to the holder's economic interest | Domestic use of patented mechanism |
| II | Acts with **experimental purpose**, related to scientific/technological studies or research | Laboratory test to develop one's own product |
| IV | **Exhaustion:** product placed on the domestic market by the holder or with their consent | Resale of product originally sold by the holder |
| V and VI | Use of living matter without economic purpose | Research with patented cultivar or microorganism |

Item IV deserves highlight: it is the legal consecration of *exhaustion* of the right — after the holder places the product on the domestic market (directly or through a licensee), it cannot prevent the resale of that specific copy. The infringement analysis, in any case, is always done **claim by claim**: the accused product or process is compared with each patent claim, under penalty of the violation being partial or nonexistent.

## The judicial path: the infringement action

### Jurisdiction

The patent infringement action is filed in the **State Court** (forum of the defendant's domicile, the place of damage, or the place of business). The relevant exception: when the discussion involves the validity of the patent with the INPI as a party — the nullity action — jurisdiction lies with the **Federal Court**, because the INPI is a federal agency.

### Before filing

- **Extrajudicial notice (cease & desist):** many disputes end before the lawsuit, especially when the infringer is a reseller who was unaware of the patent.
- **Verification expert report:** produced in advance (anticipated evidence production, arts. 381 and following of the CPC), documents the infringement before the defendant destroys evidence.
- **Validity analysis:** enforcing a weak patent can lead to compensation for bad-faith litigation; it is worth auditing the claims in advance.

### Provisional relief: the decisive moment

The [CPC/2015](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2015/lei/l13105.htm) gives the holder two quick tools:

- **Urgency relief (art. 300):** requires likelihood of right + danger of harm. In patents, the “likelihood” is reinforced when the holder presents a prior technical report.
- **Evidentiary relief (art. 311):** dispenses with the danger of harm when the right is documented and there is abuse of the right of defense.

The LPI adds to the CPC in art. 209, § 1: the judge may, **before the defendant is served**, order by injunction the **cessation of the infringement**, requiring a cash bond or surety guarantee if deemed necessary. Typical precautionary measures: search and seizure of products, production of accounting documents and attachment.

### The technical expert report: the heart of the case

In patent disputes, evidence is essentially technical. The expert compares the accused product/process with the claims, applies the doctrine of equivalents and answers whether infringement occurred. The choice of a qualified technical assistant and the formulation of the questions determine, in practice, the outcome — more than legal argumentation.

## The defense of the accused

Those who receive an infringement action have a specific arsenal:

1. **Nullity as a defense (art. 56, § 1, LPI):** patent nullity may be raised at any time, including incidentally in the infringement action — and the judge may suspend the effects of the patent preventively or incidentally (§ 2). It is the strongest defense: if the patent is annulled, the infringement action falls.
2. **Autonomous nullity action (art. 56):** any person with a legitimate interest, or the INPI itself, may request nullity at any time during its term. It proceeds in the Federal Court, with the INPI as the defendant.
3. **Exceptions of art. 43:** exhaustion, experimental use, private use without commercial purpose (see table above).
4. **Limitation (art. 225):** the action for damages for infringement of industrial property rights is time-barred in **5 (five) years**. If the holder delayed, the preliminary defense defeats the damages claim.
5. **Lack of proof of infringement:** the burden is on the holder (except reversal in process patents).

## Damages: how they are calculated

Art. 210 of the LPI adopts the criterion **most favorable to the injured party** among three alternatives:

| Criterion | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| I | Benefits that the injured party **would have earned** if the infringement had not occurred |
| II | Benefits **earned by the infringer** |
| III | Remuneration that the infringer **would have paid for a license** (reasonable royalty) |

The holder may also claim damages for exploitation that occurred **between the publication of the application and the grant of the patent** (art. 44) — limited to the content of the application as published (art. 41). In practice, criterion III (royalties) is the most used when the holder does not directly exploit; criterion II requires accounting expert analysis on the infringer's profit.

## The criminal sphere: crimes against patents

The LPI defines crimes against invention patents and utility models:

- **Art. 183:** manufacturing a patented product or using a patented means/process without authorization — detention from 3 months to 1 year, or fine.
- **Art. 184:** exporting, selling, offering for sale, keeping in stock, concealing or importing a product manufactured with infringement — detention from 1 to 3 months, or fine.
- **Art. 185:** supplying a component of a patented product or material/equipment to execute a patented process.
- **Art. 186:** infringement is characterized even if it does not reach all claims or uses equivalent means.

Strategic point: crimes against patents are **private criminal action (criminal complaint)** — art. 199 requires the holder's initiative, except for the crime of art. 191. Many holders use the criminal route as a negotiation lever: the threat of a criminal complaint and criminal search and seizure tend to accelerate agreements that civil litigation would take years to produce.

## In practice: strategy and trends

- **The winning sequence:** verification (preliminary expert report) → notice → provisional relief → judicial expert report → judgment. Each stage has its own cost and risk; provisional relief is the moment in which the dispute usually gets resolved.
- **SEP/FRAND:** the wave of litigation over standard-essential patents (telecommunications) brought to Brazil discussions about FRAND licensing, injunctions and *anti-suit injunctions* — the IAM chapter confirms that the country has entered this circuit.
- **Artificial intelligence:** with the accelerated growth of AI patent families (WIPO 2026 data), the enforcement of these patents — often with method claims — is beginning to reach the courts, testing the limits of art. 43.
- **Litigation alternatives:** cross-licensing agreements, mediation and arbitration are gaining space in disputes between companies that want to avoid years of litigation.

The final message is symmetrical: for the holder, a patent only has value if there is an enforcement plan; for those who produce with third-party technology, the freedom-to-operate audit (*freedom to operate*) is cheaper than any judicial defense. On both sides, knowledge of art. 42 to art. 225 of the LPI is the first asset.
