The illegal market cost Brazil more than **R$ 473 billion in 2025** — smuggling, counterfeiting, and piracy combined, according to the National Forum Against Piracy and Illegality (FNCP). Of that total, R$ 326.3 billion were direct industry losses and R$ 146.8 billion in tax evasion, in 15 sectors. In 2020, the loss was R$ 288 billion. For a trademark owner, this scenario is not a statistic: it is an unfair competitor selling a fake product, an illegal ad on a marketplace, and cargo held — or not — at customs.

This guide brings together the legal arsenal available in Brazil to combat trademark counterfeiting: administrative measures (Federal Revenue Service, councils, and police), civil measures (emergency relief, search and seizure, damages), and criminal measures (criminal complaint), with the legal basis for each and practical step-by-step guidance.

> In July 2026, the [ABPI joined the National Council to Combat Piracy and Crimes Against Intellectual Property (CNCP)](https://abpi.org.br/noticias/abpi-integra-o-conselho-nacional-de-combate-a-pirataria/), strengthening the coordination between the private sector and public authorities in tackling piracy and smuggling. The CNCP is the advisory body of the Ministry of Justice and Public Security created by [Decree No. 9.875/2019](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2019-2022/2019/decreto/D9875.htm) to formulate the national plan to combat piracy, smuggling, resulting tax evasion, and crimes against intellectual property.

## Counterfeiting, Piracy, and Smuggling: Everything in Its Place

Before choosing a measure, it is necessary to know what the law calls each thing — the terminology confuses even experienced professionals:

| Term | What it means | Legal basis |
|-------|-----------------|------------|
| **Trademark counterfeiting** | Reproducing, imitating, or altering a registered trademark without authorization, or selling/storing a product with a counterfeit trademark | Law 9.279/96 (LPI), arts. 189 and 190 |
| **Piracy** (legal sense) | Copyright infringement — software, music, movies, books | Laws 9.609/98 and 9.610/98 |
| **Smuggling / customs evasion** | Importing or exporting prohibited goods (smuggling) or evading tax payment (customs evasion) | Criminal Code, arts. 334-A and 334 |
| **False indication of origin** | Using a geographic name that leads the consumer to believe the product comes from another region | LPI, arts. 178 and 198 |

The distinction matters in practice: the CNCP decree, for example, defines "piracy" only as copyright infringement, while trademark counterfeiting is treated as counterfeiting. In common parlance, however, "piracy" has become synonymous with everything — which hinders communication with authorities and the correct classification of the measure.

## Why trademark registration is the starting point

Without registration with the INPI, there is no counterfeiting — at most, there is unfair competition. The LPI assures the owner of a registered trademark the right to safeguard its material integrity or reputation (art. 130, III), and the crimes of arts. 189 and 190 presuppose a **registered** trademark. The first step in any enforcement strategy is therefore to have the registration granted and up to date. See the [complete guide to trademark registration costs at the INPI]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}) and the recent discussions on [position marks and acquired distinctiveness]({{< relref "posts/consulta-publica-marcas-posicao-inpi-2026/" >}}).

## The legal basis for combating counterfeiting

The table below summarizes the main instruments and where to find them in the law:

| Measure | Legal basis | What it allows |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Customs detention of counterfeit products | LPI, art. 198; Customs Regulation (Decree 6.759/2009), arts. 605-606; TRIPS Agreement, arts. 51-60 | Customs authority detains products with counterfeit, altered, or imitated marks or with false indication of origin, on its own initiative or upon request |
| Search and seizure | LPI, arts. 200-201; Code of Criminal Procedure | Seizure of counterfeit goods and materials, including in preliminary investigative proceedings prior to the action |
| Preliminary suspension of the violation | LPI, art. 209, § 1 | Emergency relief to prevent the continuation of the violation |
| Compensation for losses and damages | LPI, arts. 209-210 | Indemnification with lost profits calculated by the criterion most favorable to the injured party |
| Crime of reproduction/imitation of a trademark | LPI, art. 189 | Penalty: detention from 3 months to 1 year, or fine |
| Crime of selling/stocking counterfeit products | LPI, art. 190 | Penalty: detention from 1 to 3 months, or fine |
| Crime of unfair competition | LPI, art. 195 | Various conduct, including customer poaching and improper use of another's sign |

## Administrative measures: Federal Revenue Service and the councils

The fastest and cheapest route begins at customs. Under art. 198 of the LPI, customs authorities may **seize, on their own initiative or at the request of the interested party**, at the time of inspection, products bearing counterfeit, altered, or imitated marks, or with false indication of origin. The Customs Regulation details the procedure:

- **Art. 605**: the customs authority may detain the products, on its own initiative or at the request of the interested party, during customs inspection.
- **Art. 606**: after the detention, the trademark owner is notified that, within **10 business days** of being served, they must file the criminal complaint and request the **judicial seizure** of the goods. The deadline may be extended once, for an equal period, in justified cases (mirroring art. 55 of the TRIPS Agreement).

In practice, this means the owner needs an active channel with the Federal Revenue Service and a lawyer prepared to file the judicial measure within the deadline — detention alone does not destroy the goods; it only provides the window to take legal action.

At the institutional level, the **CNCP** (Decree 9.875/2019) is the forum that articulates public authorities and civil society: it prepares studies, maintains statistics integrated with the Unified Public Security System, proposes inspections at ports, airports, and borders, and coordinates educational campaigns. ABPI's entry into the Council (Jul/2026) expands the private sector's technical participation in these policies.

There are also specialized police stations — such as the Department for the Repression of Crimes against Intellectual Property (DRCPIM), of Deic, in São Paulo — and Federal Police operations, which act based on a complaint filed by the owner.

## Civil measures: speed is everything

When the violation is located (physical store, warehouse, marketplace), the owner may:

1. **Extrajudicial notice** — often resolves cases of small good-faith retailers, but rarely against organized counterfeiters.
2. **Emergency relief** — art. 209, § 1 of the LPI authorizes the judge to determine, preliminarily, the **suspension of the violation** to prevent irreparable or hard-to-repair damage. It is the most used instrument against marketplaces and websites that continue selling after notification.
3. **Search and seizure** — under arts. 200-201 of the LPI, regulated by the Code of Criminal Procedure, with the possibility of preliminary investigative measures to gather evidence even before the main action.
4. **Lawsuit for damages** — arts. 209 and 210 of the LPI guarantee losses and damages, with lost profits calculated under the **criterion most favorable to the injured party**, among: (i) what the owner would have earned without the violation; (ii) the benefit earned by the violator; or (iii) the remuneration of a hypothetical license.

The critical point of the civil route is **evidence preservation**: test purchases with invoices, date-stamped screenshots, photos of the counterfeit product next to the original, and sealed samples. Without this, the emergency relief loses support.

## Criminal measures: criminal complaint and Brazilian peculiarities

Trademark counterfeiting crimes have two peculiarities that surprise those coming from other countries:

- **Private criminal prosecution**: under the rule of art. 199 of the LPI, the crimes of the Title (including arts. 189 and 190) are prosecuted only **upon complaint** by the owner — that is, the injured party must file the criminal complaint. The exception is art. 191 (improper use of official arms, coats of arms, and insignia), which is a public criminal action.
- **Low penalties**: detention from 3 months to 1 year (art. 189) and from 1 to 3 months (art. 190). Because they are offenses of lesser offensive potential, Law 9.099/95 applies, with the possibility of **criminal transaction** — which, in practice, turns the criminal process into an instrument of negotiation and formal registration of the offense, rather than effective punishment.

Even so, the criminal route has strategic value: the criminal complaint allows preliminary search and seizure, pressures the infringer into an agreement, and creates a useful judicial record in repetitive civil actions. For foreign owners, the [international trademark protection through the Madrid Protocol]({{< relref "posts/inpi-manual-marcas-alto-renome-protocolo-madri/" >}}) ensures registration in Brazil based on the international filing — a prerequisite for any local enforcement measure.

## E-commerce and social media

Much of counterfeiting has migrated to the digital environment, and combating it requires its own strategy:

- **Brazilian Civil Rights Framework for the Internet** (Law 12.965/2014, art. 19): application providers are only liable for third-party content if, after a **specific court order**, they fail to take measures to remove it. The judicial route, therefore, is the standard path against recalcitrant marketplaces.
- **Voluntary platform programs**: Amazon Brand Registry, Mercado Livre's Brand Protection Program, and AliExpress's Intellectual Property Protection (IPP) allow faster reporting and removal of counterfeit listings, without relying on a court order. Each platform has its own registration and evidence rules — it is worth maintaining a standardized dossier.
- **Continuous monitoring**: automated searches for the brand + common spelling variations ("iphone", "i-phone", "iPh0ne") are the starting point for detecting fake listings before they grow.

## Step by step: the owner's checklist

1. **Registration up to date** — without a registered trademark with the INPI, there is no counterfeiting. Check renewal deadlines and [maintenance costs]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}).
2. **Monitor** — marketplaces, social media, trade fairs, ports, and borders. Establish contact with the Federal Revenue unit responsible for your region.
3. **Document and preserve evidence** — test purchases, invoices, date-stamped screenshots, samples.
4. **Notify** — send an extrajudicial notice to the infringer and to the platform (brand protection program).
5. **Litigate swiftly** — emergency relief or search and seizure; if applicable, customs detention in progress.
6. **Claim compensation** — lawsuit for losses and damages based on the criteria of art. 210 of the LPI.
7. **Consider the criminal route** — criminal complaint (art. 199) when the identity of the perpetrator is known and there is interest in formal registration of the offense.

## What to watch for

The fight against counterfeiting in Brazil is in motion. It is worth following: (i) the renewed work of the CNCP with ABPI's participation and the national plan to combat piracy; (ii) the case law on marketplace liability for counterfeit listings; (iii) the Federal Revenue Service and Federal Police operations at ports and borders; and (iv) the trajectory of the illegal market, which went from R$ 288 billion (2020) to R$ 473 billion (2025) — growth that makes enforcement an essential part of trademark strategy, not an ancillary cost.
