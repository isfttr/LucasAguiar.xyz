Trademark registration with INPI is not permanent. It is valid for **10 years** from grant and, to continue producing effects, must be **renewed by the holder** — every new ten-year period. Whoever lets the window pass loses the registration: the mark is extinguished and becomes available again for third-party applications.

The problem is that most holders discover the deadline too late, because the count does not start on the filing date (but on the grant date, which may come years later) and renewal does not happen automatically: it depends on payment, per class, within a specific window.

This guide brings together what the Industrial Property Law (Law No. 9.279/1996), the [INPI Trademark Manual](https://manualdemarcas.inpi.gov.br/projects/manual/wiki/06_Concess%C3%A3o_manuten%C3%A7%C3%A3o_e_extin%C3%A7%C3%A3o_do_registro), and the current fee schedule say to explain the deadlines, amounts, and step-by-step renewal process — in addition to the pitfalls that cause old and valuable marks to be extinguished due to an operational detail.

## Registration is valid for 10 years — and the count does not start on filing

[Art. 133 of the LPI](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm) provides that trademark registration shall be valid for 10 years, **counted from the grant date**, renewable for equal and successive periods. The Trademark Manual details: the term runs from the **publication of the grant in the RPI** — the moment when INPI publishes the final approval and issues the certificate.

This creates the first practical pitfall: the "anniversary" that matters is not that of the filing. A mark filed in 2016, granted in 2019, has its first ten-year term ending in **2029** — and the renewal window starts in 2028, not 2026.

The correct date is on the registration certificate (digital) and in the notices published in the RPI. If you do not have the certificate at hand, it is possible to reconstruct the timeline through [monitoring the case at INPI]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}}).

| Grant date | End of ten-year term | Ordinary renewal window |
|---|---|---|
| 15/03/2016 | 15/03/2026 | 16/03/2025 to 15/03/2026 |
| 10/09/2017 | 10/09/2027 | 11/09/2026 to 10/09/2027 |

The ten-year terms are **successive**: the expiration remains anchored to the original grant, not to the date on which the renewal payment was made. Paying on the first day of the window or on the last does not change the next critical date.

## The ordinary window: the last year of validity

Paragraph 1 of Art. 133 is clear: the renewal request must be filed **during the last year of validity of the registration**, accompanied by proof of payment of the corresponding fee.

In INPI's workflow, this is resolved in a single payment action — the service **does not require a form**. According to the Trademark Manual (§6.4), renewal requires:

- that the request be made **by the holder of the right**;
- payment of the fee in the exact amount of the current schedule — without the need to prove payment through a specific petition;
- the **declaration, at the time of issuance of the GRU**, that the holder's corporate activity continues to comprise the goods or services indicated in the registration.

If there is an attorney with specific powers to renew, the power of attorney (when submitted) must be filed through the petition **"Apresentação de documentos (em petição)" — Serviço 381**.

For **collective and certification marks**, renewal has an additional condition: the holder must continue to meet the requirements of paragraphs 2 and 3 of Art. 128 of the LPI, or renewal may not be granted.

## Extraordinary period: the 6-month "second chance"

If renewal is not requested by the end of the validity term, paragraph 2 of Art. 133 grants a grace period: the holder may do so **in the following 6 months**, upon payment of an **additional fee** — in practice, double the amount.

The Trademark Manual specifies the count: the 6 months run **from the day immediately following the end of the validity term, even if it is not a business day**. That is, the deadline does not shift to the next business day; it starts counting from the day after expiration, as it appears on the calendar.

Once this window has passed without payment, the registration is extinguished and there is no retroactive renewal.

## How much renewal costs

The current fee schedule uses **amounts per class**. The codes for renewal of a national trademark registration are:

| Service | Code | Full amount (per class) | With 50% discount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Renewal — ordinary period | 374 | R$ 1.000,00 | R$ 500,00 |
| Renewal — extraordinary period | 375 | R$ 2.000,00 | R$ 1.000,00 |

Two practical points:

**1. It is per class.** A mark registered in three Nice classes pays the renewal three times. Failing to pay for one class means partial extinction — the registration dies only in that class, but it dies.

**2. The 50% discount has its own rules.** According to the note in the official schedule itself, the following are entitled to the reduction: natural persons (provided they do not hold an interest in a company in the sector and the company itself is not entitled to the discount), microenterprises, MEI, and small businesses (Complementary Law No. 123/2006), simple innovation companies (Complementary Law No. 167/2019), ICTs (Law No. 10.973/2004), nonprofit entities, and public bodies. **In co-ownership, the discount applies only if all holders are entitled to it.**

It is worth remembering that, since the 2025 schedule, the **first ten-year term is free** — the first renewal expense occurs only at the transition from the first to the second ten-year term. The complete breakdown of trademark costs is in [our guide to INPI registration costs]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}). Amounts change by ordinance: always check the [current official schedule](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/custos) before issuing the GRU.

## Step by step for renewal

1. **Determine the grant date** for each registration (digital certificate, RPI, or consultation in e-INPI) and calculate the end of the ten-year term.
2. **Access e-Marcas** with the holder's account and issue the GRU choosing service **374** (ordinary period) or **375** (extraordinary period).
3. **Make the activity declaration** required when issuing the GRU: that the holder's corporate activity continues to comprise the goods or services of the registration.
4. **Pay the GRU per class** within the window. It is not necessary to file a specific petition to prove payment.
5. **Monitor the recordal** and download the certificate of the renewed mark, issued only in digital format.
6. **If there is a specific power of attorney**, file it through the Service 381 petition.

### Attention to classes "in transition"

The Trademark Manual includes an observation that has already tripped up many inattentive holders: if there is **partial forfeiture or partial ex officio cancellation** pending appeal, or **partial surrender** of entire classes not yet processed by INPI, renewal must be paid **including in relation to forfeited, cancelled, or surrendered classes** within the ordinary or extraordinary period. Anyone who tries to "save" on these classes may end up with the case under an office action and, at worst, miss the deadline.

## What happens if the deadline passes

After the last year of validity and the 6 months of extraordinary period pass without payment, the registration is **extinguished by expiration of the validity term** (Art. 142, I, of the LPI). The Trademark Manual confirms: the mark is extinguished when the holder does not file the renewal in either of the two windows.

The consequences are harsher than they seem:

- The LPI **does not provide for restoration** of a trademark registration extinguished due to expiration — unlike what exists for deadlines in some patent proceedings. In practice, the holder must **file a new application**, which will be examined as new, without priority, and may conflict with applications filed by third parties in the meantime.
- Since the mark is no longer protected, third parties may use the sign and even register it. Recovering exclusivity then depends on legal action and proof of consolidated use — a much more expensive and uncertain path than the renewal GRU.

**Renewing is not the same as proving use.** Renewal does not require proof of use — but the holder has a parallel duty to use the mark, under penalty of **forfeiture**, which is declared upon request by any person with a legitimate interest after 5 years from grant (Art. 143). These are two different clocks, and both need to be managed.

## Pitfalls that appear in practice

- **Confusing the filing date with the grant date.** This is the most common cause of missed deadlines in older portfolios.
- **Paying only one class** of a multi-class registration and assuming the registration is "renewed".
- **Holder domiciled abroad** without an attorney appointed and maintained in Brazil — a ground for extinction provided in Art. 142, IV, for failure to comply with Art. 217.
- **Unrecorded assignment:** renewal must be requested by the holder listed in INPI's records. Without recordal of the assignment (Art. 136), INPI does not see the new owner.
- **Collective or certification mark** that has lost legal requirements: renewal may simply not be granted.

## How not to miss the deadline

Portfolios with dozens of marks are not managed by memory. The operational minimum is:

- record the **grant date** of each registration (not the filing date) in a database with alerts at D-365, D-180, and D-30;
- periodically review the portfolio for registrations granted 9 years ago or more;
- keep the **electronic domicile and attorney up to date** at INPI, so as not to miss publications;
- monitor the RPI and official actions — classes in transition (partial forfeiture, ex officio cancellation, pending surrender) change what must be paid.

Renewing is one of the cheapest and least glamorous operations in IP management — and one of those that destroys the most value when it fails. A registration with 10 years of real use is worth far more than the fee that keeps it alive.
