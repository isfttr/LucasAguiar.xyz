A trademark registration with the INPI is not definitive. It is valid for **10 years** counted from grant and, to continue producing effects, must be **renewed by the holder** — every new ten-year term. Anyone who lets the window pass loses the registration: the trademark is extinguished and becomes available again for third-party applications.

The problem is that most holders discover the deadline too late, because the count does not start on the filing date (but rather on grant, which may come years later), and renewal does not happen automatically: it depends on payment, per class, within a specific window.

This guide brings together what the Industrial Property Law (Law No. 9,279/1996), the [INPI Trademark Manual](https://manualdemarcas.inpi.gov.br/projects/manual/wiki/06_Concess%C3%A3o_manuten%C3%A7%C3%A3o_e_extin%C3%A7%C3%A3o_do_registro), and the current fee schedule say to explain the deadlines, amounts, and step-by-step renewal process — in addition to the pitfalls that cause old and valuable trademarks to be extinguished due to an operational detail.

## The registration is valid for 10 years — and the count does not start at filing

[Art. 133 of the LPI](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm) provides that a trademark registration will be valid for 10 years, **counted from the grant date**, renewable for equal and successive periods. The Trademark Manual details: the term runs from the **publication of the grant in the RPI** — the moment when the INPI publishes the final approval and issues the certificate.

This creates the first practical trap: the "anniversary" that matters is not the filing one. A trademark filed in 2016 and granted in 2019 has its first ten-year term ending in **2029** — and the renewal window starts in 2028, not 2026.

The correct date is on the registration certificate (digital) and in the notices published in the RPI. If you do not have the certificate at hand, it is possible to reconstruct the timeline through [tracking the process at the INPI]({{< relref "posts/como-consultar-andamento-processo-inpi/" >}}).

| Grant date | End of ten-year term | Ordinary renewal window |
|---|---|---|
| 15/03/2016 | 15/03/2026 | 16/03/2025 to 15/03/2026 |
| 10/09/2017 | 10/09/2027 | 11/09/2026 to 10/09/2027 |

The ten-year terms are **successive**: the expiration remains anchored to the original grant, not to the date on which the renewal payment was made. Paying on the first day of the window or on the last does not change the next critical date.

## The ordinary window: the last year of validity

Paragraph 1 of Art. 133 is clear: the renewal request must be filed **during the last year of the registration's term**, accompanied by proof of payment of the corresponding fee.

In the INPI workflow, this is resolved in a single payment act — the service is **exempt from a form**. According to the Trademark Manual (§6.4), renewal requires:

- that the request be made **by the holder of the right**;
- payment of the fee in the exact amount of the current schedule — without the need to prove payment via a specific petition;
- the **declaration, at the time of issuing the GRU**, that the holder's corporate purpose continues to comprise the products or services indicated in the registration.

If there is an agent with specific powers to renew, the power of attorney (when submitted) must be filed via the petition **"Submission of documents (by petition)" — Service 381**.

For **collective and certification marks**, renewal has an additional condition: the holder must continue to comply with the requirements of §§2 and 3 of Art. 128 of the LPI, under penalty of the renewal not being granted.

## Extraordinary period: the 6-month "second chance"

If renewal is not requested by the end of the term, §2 of Art. 133 grants a grace period: the holder may do so **in the following 6 months**, upon payment of an **additional fee** — in practice, double the amount.

The Trademark Manual specifies the counting: the 6 months run **from the day immediately following the end of the term, even if it is not a business day**. That is, the deadline does not shift to the next business day; it begins counting from the day after expiration, as shown on the calendar.

Once this window expires without payment, the registration is extinguished and there is no retroactive renewal.

## How much renewal costs

The current fee schedule uses **amounts per class**. The codes for renewing a national trademark registration are:

| Service | Code | Full amount (per class) | With 50% discount |
|---|---|---|---|
| Renewal — ordinary period | 374 | R$ 1,000.00 | R$ 500.00 |
| Renewal — extraordinary period | 375 | R$ 2,000.00 | R$ 1,000.00 |

Two practical points:

**1. It is per class.** A trademark registered in three Nice classes pays the renewal three times. Failing to pay one class means partial extinction — the registration dies only in that class, but it dies.

**2. The 50% discount has its own rules.** According to the note in the official schedule itself, the following are entitled to the reduction: natural persons (provided they do not hold an interest in a company in the field and that the company itself is not entitled to the discount), microenterprises, MEI, and small businesses (Complementary Law No. 123/2006), simple innovation companies (Complementary Law No. 167/2019), ICTs (Law No. 10,973/2004), non-profit entities, and public bodies. **In co-ownership, the discount applies only if all holders are entitled to it.**

It is worth remembering that, since the 2025 schedule, the **first ten-year term is free** — the first disbursement for renewal occurs only at the transition from the first to the second ten-year term. The complete breakdown of trademark costs is in [our guide to registration costs at the INPI]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}). Amounts change by ordinance: always check the [current official schedule](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/custos) before issuing the GRU.

## Step by step for renewal

1. **Obtain the grant date** for each registration (digital certificate, RPI, or e-INPI query) and calculate the end of the ten-year term.
2. **Access e-Marcas** with the holder's account and issue the GRU by choosing service **374** (ordinary period) or **375** (extraordinary period).
3. **Make the activity declaration** required when issuing the GRU: that the holder's corporate purpose continues to comprise the products or services in the registration.
4. **Pay the GRU per class** within the window. There is no need to file a specific petition to prove payment.
5. **Monitor the recordal** and download the certificate of the renewed trademark, issued only in digital format.
6. **If there is a specific power of attorney**, file it via the Service 381 petition.

### Watch out for classes "in transition"

The Trademark Manual includes an observation that has already tripped up many inattentive holders: if there is a **partial forfeiture or partial ex officio cancellation** pending appeal, or a **partial surrender** of entire classes not yet processed by the INPI, renewal must be paid **including for the forfeited, cancelled, or surrendered classes** within the ordinary or extraordinary period. Anyone who tries to "save" on these classes may end up with the process under an office action and, at the limit, miss the deadline.

## What happens if the deadline passes

Once the last year of validity and the 6-month extraordinary period have passed without payment, the registration is **extinguished by expiration of the term of validity** (Art. 142, I, of the LPI). The Trademark Manual confirms: the trademark is extinguished when the holder does not file the renewal in either of the two windows.

The consequences are harsher than they seem:

- The LPI **does not provide for restoration** of a trademark registration extinguished due to expiry — unlike what exists for deadlines in some patent processes. In practice, the holder must **file a new application**, which will be examined as new, without priority, and may conflict with applications made by third parties in the interval.
- As the trademark ceases to be protected, third parties may use the sign and even register it. Recovering exclusivity then depends on legal action and proof of consolidated use — a much more expensive and uncertain path than the renewal GRU.

**Renewing is not the same as proving use.** Renewal does not require proof of use — but the holder has a parallel duty to use the trademark, under penalty of **forfeiture**, which is declared upon request by any person with a legitimate interest after 5 years from grant (Art. 143). These are two different clocks, and both need management.

## Pitfalls that appear in practice

- **Confusing the filing date with the grant date.** This is the most common cause of missing the deadline in older portfolios.
- **Paying only one class** of a multi-class registration and assuming the registration is "renewed".
- **Holder domiciled abroad** without an agent appointed and maintained in Brazil — a case of extinction provided for in Art. 142, IV, for non-compliance with Art. 217.
- **Unrecorded assignment:** renewal must be requested by the holder listed in the INPI records. Without the recordal of the assignment (Art. 136), the INPI does not see the new owner.
- **Collective or certification mark** that has lost legal requirements: renewal may simply not be granted.

## How not to miss the deadline

Portfolios with dozens of trademarks are not managed from memory. The operational minimum is:

- record the **grant date** of each registration (not the filing date) in a database with alerts at D-365, D-180, and D-30;
- periodically review the portfolio for registrations granted 9 years ago or more;
- keep the **electronic domicile and agent up to date** with the INPI, so as not to miss publications;
- monitor the RPI and office actions — classes in transition (partial forfeiture, ex officio cancellation, pending surrender) change what must be paid.

Renewal is one of the cheapest and least glamorous operations in IP management — and one of those that destroys the most value when it fails. A 10-year registration with real use is worth far more than the fee that keeps it alive.
