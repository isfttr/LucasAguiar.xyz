Any contract involving the licensing of industrial property rights, technology supply, technical assistance services, or franchising **must be registered or recorded with the INPI** to produce effects vis-à-vis third parties. Without registration, royalties are not deductible from Income Tax, franchise contracts have no _erga omnes_ validity, and the company is exposed in legal disputes.

This guide covers the complete step-by-step process for registering technology transfer contracts with the INPI in 2026: contract types, documents, costs, deadlines, and the new rules applicable since December 2025.

## What are technology transfer contracts

The Industrial Property Law (Law 9.279/96) defines, in articles 211 to 216, that IP licensing, technology supply, and franchise contracts **must be registered with the INPI** to be enforceable against third parties. In practice, this means:

- **Patent licensing** (Art. 61) — authorization to exploit an invention patent (IP) or utility model (MU)
- **Industrial design licensing** (Art. 121) — use of an ID registration
- **Trademark licensing** (Art. 139) — exploitation of a registered trademark
- **Technology supply** — transfer of non-patented technical knowledge (know-how)
- **Technical assistance services** — provision of specialized services
- **Business franchising** (Law 13.966/2019) — franchise system involving trademark and know-how

Registration with the INPI is a condition for the contract to produce effects vis-à-vis third parties (Art. 212 of the LPI) — without it, the contract is valid only between the contracting parties, but cannot be opposed to creditors, the Federal Revenue Service, or potential competitors.

## Why register the contract with the INPI?

| Consequence | With registration | Without registration |
|-------------|------------------|---------------------|
| Effect vis-à-vis third parties | Yes (Art. 212 LPI) | No |
| Deductibility of royalties in IR | Yes | No |
| Proof of trademark use (cancellation) | Licensed registration counts as use | Does not count |
| Remittance of royalties abroad | Authorized by the Central Bank | Blocked |
| Validity in legal action | Enforceable against third parties | Valid only between parties |
| Inspection by the Federal Revenue Service | Compliant | Risk of penalties |

For companies paying royalties abroad, registration with the INPI is a prerequisite for the Central Bank to authorize the remittance. The Federal Revenue Service, in turn, requires registration to accept the deductibility of royalties as an operating expense.

## Types of contracts accepted by the INPI

The INPI classifies technology contracts into five main types, each with its own service code and fee amount:

### 1. IP rights licensing

Contracts that authorize third parties to exploit:

- **Patents** (IP and MU) — voluntary or compulsory licensing
- **Trademarks** — use licensing, with or without assignment
- **Industrial designs** — registration licensing
- **Integrated circuit topographies**

### 2. Technology supply

Transfer of technical knowledge, know-how, manufacturing processes, formulas, or non-patentable specifications. This type is used when the contract's subject does not fall under registered industrial property licensing.

### 3. Technical assistance services

Contracts for the provision of specialized technical services: projects, consulting, training, assembly supervision, maintenance. The INPI requires the contract to clearly specify the scope, duration, and technical qualification of the service provider.

### 4. Business franchising

Governed by Law 13.966/2019 (Franchise Law), the franchise contract involves trademark licensing + know-how + business model. The INPI records the franchise contract that has already been registered as such under the Franchise Law.

### 5. Assignment

Definitive transfer of ownership of IP rights. It differs from licensing because the assignor transfers all rights, not just authorizes use.

## New rules (as of December 2025)

On December 12, 2025, new regulations on the recording and registration of technology contracts with the INPI came into force. The main changes include:

- **Complete digitization of the process** — e-Contratos replaced physical filing
- **Reduced deadlines** for requirements (from 60 to 30 days)
- **Simplified documentation** for trademark licensing contracts with already registered parties
- **New service codes** in the Fee Table
- **Possibility of partial recording** — the contract can be recorded even with minor pending issues

For contracts recorded under the old rules, it is recommended to check whether any supplementation is necessary.

## Step-by-step: how to register the contract with the INPI

### Step 1: Prepare the documentation

Before starting, gather:

- **Contract signed** by all parties (digital or physical)
- **Power of attorney** from the legal representative, if applicable
- **Proof of GRU payment** (issue in step 2)
- **Documents of the parties** (CNPJ or CPF)
- **Registration certificate** of the trademark, patent, or industrial design, if applicable

The INPI provides the [Contracts Manual](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/contratos-de-tecnologia-e-de-franquia) with detailed checklists for each type.

### Step 2: Issue and pay the GRU

1. Access [e-INPI](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/e-inpi) and log in with your gov.br account
2. Navigate to "Issue GRU"
3. Select the corresponding service code (see table above)
4. Enter the applicant's CNPJ/CPF
5. Generate the GRU and pay at any bank (barcode)

Paying the GRU is a prerequisite to file the application. Keep the GRU number — it will be needed in the e-Contratos form.

### Step 3: Access e-Contratos and fill out the form

1. Access the [e-Contratos](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/contratos-de-tecnologia-e-de-franquia) system
2. Log in as "Attorney or Individual with a power of attorney instrument"
3. Select "New Application"
4. Choose the contract type (licensing, supply, franchise, etc.)
5. Fill in:
   - Data of the contracting parties
   - Subject of the contract (clear description of what is being licensed)
   - Financial clauses (royalties, payment method)
   - Term of validity
   - Paid GRU number
6. Attach the scanned documents (contract, power of attorney, certificates)
7. File the application

The generated application number is the reference for follow-up.

### Step 4: Monitor the progress

The INPI publishes the Industrial Property Journal (RPI) weekly on Tuesdays with the dispatches for contract processes. You can monitor in two ways:

1. **Process search:** use the [public search](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/contratos-de-tecnologia-e-de-franquia/busca) tool by application number, protocol, party names, or CPF/CNPJ
2. **My Applications:** on the e-Contratos platform, include the process in "My Applications" to receive email notifications for each update

The INPI may request additional documents — it is essential to check the RPI weekly and respond to requirements within the deadline (30 days under the new rules).

### Step 5: Receive the registration certificate

After approval, the INPI issues the **Registration Certificate**, which must be kept together with the original contract. This certificate proves enforceability against third parties and enables the tax deductibility of royalties.

## Average processing times

The INPI does not publish a specific time target for technology contracts, but the average time observed in 2025-2026 is:

| Type | Average time |
|------|-------------|
| Trademark licensing | 30-60 days |
| Patent licensing | 60-120 days |
| Technology supply | 60-90 days |
| Technical assistance services | 30-60 days |
| Franchise | 45-90 days |
| Assignment | 30-60 days |

Contracts with complete documentation and a well-filled form tend to be processed more quickly. Unanswered requirements within the deadline are the main cause of delays.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to register the contract before starting to pay royalties?**

Yes. Registration with the INPI is a prerequisite for the deductibility of royalties in the Corporate Income Tax (IRPJ) and for remittance abroad. Ideally, file before payments begin.

**My contract is international. Do I need a translation?**

Yes. Contracts written in a foreign language must be accompanied by a sworn translation into Portuguese.

**What if the contract is not registered?**

The contract remains valid between the parties, but does not produce effects vis-à-vis third parties. Royalties paid without registration may be disallowed by the Federal Revenue Service, and the company is left unprotected in intellectual property disputes.

**Does the licensing registration prevent trademark cancellation?**

Yes. A trademark licensing registered with the INPI counts as effective use of the trademark, serving as a defense in a cancellation proceeding. See the [complete guide to trademark cancellation]({{< relref "posts/caducidade-marca-inpi-guia-2026/" >}}).

## Conclusion

Registering technology transfer contracts with the INPI is a mandatory procedure that many companies neglect — whether due to lack of knowledge or the bureaucracy involved. The consequences of not registering range from tax disallowance to loss of IP rights in legal disputes.

With the digitization of e-Contratos and the 70% discount for SMEs, the cost of registration dropped to around R$ 104 for most types. The process, which previously required physical travel to the INPI headquarters, is now 100% digital.

The main recommendation: **register the contract before the first royalty payment**. The cost is low, the process is digital, and the risk of not registering is far greater than the administrative effort.
