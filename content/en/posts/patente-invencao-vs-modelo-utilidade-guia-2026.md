You created a product or an improvement to something that already exists — and now you need to decide: file it as an **invention patent (PI)** or as a **utility model (MU)**? The wrong choice can mean 5 fewer years of protection, unnecessarily higher costs, or a rejected application for requiring a level of inventiveness your object does not have.

The good news: the Industrial Property Law (Law No. 9.279/96 — LPI) defines both institutions clearly, and the decision depends on a few objective questions. This guide compares the two types of patents based on the law, on INPI's 2026 official fees, and on practical examples — so you can file the right type on the first attempt.

## What the law defines: Art. 8 vs Art. 9 of the LPI

The starting point is the text of the LPI:

> **Art. 8.** An invention that meets the requirements of novelty, inventive activity, and industrial application is patentable.

> **Art. 9.** A utility model is patentable when it is an object of practical use, or part thereof, susceptible to industrial application, presenting a new shape or arrangement, involving an inventive act, that results in a functional improvement in its use or manufacture.

Notice the central difference: the invention protects new **products and processes**, with **inventive activity** (non-obvious to a person skilled in the art). The utility model protects only **the shape or arrangement** of an object of practical use — and requires only an **inventive act**, a lower level of inventiveness, as long as it results in a **functional improvement** in use or manufacture.

In practice, the MU is the classic protection for "improvements": the object already exists, but you found a new shape or arrangement that makes it more functional. For an overview of the other types of protection (industrial design, trademark, software, plant variety), see our guide comparing [software patent vs computer program registration]({{< relref "posts/patente-software-vs-registro-programa-computador-brasil/" >}}).

| Criterion | Invention Patent (PI) | Utility Model (MU) |
|----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Legal basis** | Art. 8 of the LPI | Art. 9 of the LPI |
| **Protected subject matter** | Product or process | Shape or arrangement of an object of practical use (functional improvement) |
| **Inventive requirement** | Inventive activity (non-obvious to a specialist) | Inventive act (lower level) |
| **Term** | 20 years from filing | 15 years from filing |
| **Renewable?** | No | No |

| **Filing fee (other PJ)** | R$ 285 | R$ 220 |

| **Technical examination** | Required, generally more complex | Required, tends to be simpler |
| **Can it protect a process?** | Yes | No |

## How to decide: 4 objective questions

### 1. Is it a process, method, or composition?

If your creation is a manufacturing process, a method, a chemical compound, or a composition, the answer is straightforward: **utility models do not protect processes**. Art. 9 requires an "object of practical use" — that is, something physical, with shape. Processes can only be protected by an invention patent.

### 2. Is the object radically new?

If nothing similar exists — it is a new category of product, an unprecedented mechanism, a technical solution with no precedent — the natural path is the **invention patent**, with its requirement of full inventive activity and 20 years of protection.

### 3. Is it a functional improvement to a known object?

If the object already exists and your contribution is a **new shape or arrangement** that improves use or manufacture — a more ergonomic handle, a more efficient seal, an arrangement of components that makes assembly easier — the **utility model** is, in general, the right choice. You pay less, have a less complex examination, and the inventiveness requirement is lower.

### 4. How much protection time do you need?

The term is the final trade-off: 20 years (PI) vs 15 years (MU), both counted from the filing date (Art. 40 of the LPI), with no renewal. For products with a short life cycle — consumer goods, utensils, packaging — the MU's 15 years are usually enough. For long-cycle technologies (pharmaceuticals, industrial equipment, telecom), the PI is worth it.

## Practical examples

| Example | Most suitable type | Why |
|---------|--------------------|---------|
| New tool handle with anatomical grip | MU | Shape improvement to a known object |
| Bottle cap with a new sealing system | MU | New arrangement that improves functionality |
| Folding mechanism in a chair | MU | Arrangement of parts with functional improvement |
| New chemical compound or metal alloy | PI | New product with full inventive activity |
| Method of manufacturing a component | PI | Process — MU does not protect processes |
| Electronic circuit with unprecedented architecture | PI | Radically new technical solution |

A single product can combine several protections: a bottle can have an **MU** (functional cap), **industrial design** (ornamental shape), and **trademark** (name). The most common confusion is between MU and industrial design: the DI protects **aesthetic appearance**, while the MU protects the **functionality** of the shape. ## Costs in 2026: how much does each path cost?

The values below follow the INPI Patent Services Fee Schedule (INPI/PR Ordinance No. 12, of 2025, in effect since 12/20/2025). Individuals, MEIs, MEs, and EPPs pay 50% of the fee for most services.

| Cost | PI | MU |
|-------|----|----|
| Filing (PF/ME/EPP/ICT) | R$ 95 | R$ 75 |
| Filing (other PJ) | R$ 285 | R$ 220 |
| Total annuities (PF/ME/EPP) | ~R$ 9,800 | ~R$ 4,900 |
| Total annuities (other PJ) | ~R$ 19,600 | ~R$ 9,800 |

The annuity difference is the biggest weight in the decision: maintaining an MU costs **half** of what it costs to maintain a PI over its lifetime. Since annuities become due as early as the 3rd year from filing (Art. 84 of the LPI), even while the application is still in the examination queue, it is worth projecting this cost from the start. Details on deadlines, GRU codes, and restoration are in our [guide to filing a patent at the INPI]({{< relref "posts/guia-deposito-patente-inpi-2026/" >}}).

## The filing process is the same

The filing flow is identical for PI and MU: prior art search → filling out the application in the **e-Patentes** system → paying the GRU → publication → technical examination → grant. The difference lies in the form (application type) and the technical content — MU claims must demonstrate the functional improvement of the shape, while PI claims must demonstrate full inventive activity.

The complete step-by-step process is in our [guide to filing a patent at the INPI]({{< relref "posts/guia-deposito-patente-inpi-2026/" >}}). Before filing, do a prior art search — the MU also requires novelty, and its absence is a frequent cause of rejection. See how to hire or conduct [patent prior art searches]({{< relref "posts/inpi-credenciamento-busca-anterioridade-patentes/" >}}) and how to use the [International Patent Classification (IPC)]({{< relref "posts/busca-internacional-patentes-patentscope-espacenet-google-2026/" >}}) in your application.

## Common mistakes in the choice

1. **Filing something that is an MU as a PI** — the application faces the full inventive activity requirement and may be rejected, in addition to paying higher fees. If the contribution is a shape improvement, the MU is the appropriate route.
2. **Filing something that is a PI as an MU** — protection is limited to the claimed shape; a competitor can design around it with another shape or a different process. Product/process inventions lose scope as an MU.
3. **Believing that MU protects processes** — it does not. Processes require a PI.
4. **Ignoring novelty in the MU** — the inventive act is lower, but novelty is fully required. Without a prior art search, the risk of rejection is high.
5. **Confusing MU with industrial design** — one protects functionality, the other protects appearance. For purely aesthetic creations, the DI is the right route.

## Conclusion

The choice between an invention patent and a utility model boils down to three questions: **is it a process?** (PI), **is it a radically new object?** (PI), **is it a functional shape improvement?** (MU). When doubt remains, an accredited industrial property agent can assess the specific case — the cost of that consultation is small compared to the cost of filing the wrong type and losing 5 years of protection or the entire application.
