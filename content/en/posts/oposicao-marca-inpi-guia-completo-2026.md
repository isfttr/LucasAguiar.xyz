When the INPI publishes a trademark application in the Industrial Property Journal (RPI), a **60-day** window opens for anyone with legitimate interest to contest the registration. This contestation — the **opposition** — is the main defense instrument for the owner of an earlier trademark and the cheapest and most effective moment to block a conflicting application.

This guide covers the complete procedure: who can oppose, which grounds to use, how to file, how much it costs, what happens next, and how the applicant should react.

## What is trademark opposition

Opposition is an administrative procedure provided for in [art. 158 of the Industrial Property Law (LPI, Law 9.279/96)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm): once the application is published, any interested party may submit, within **60 days**, their reasons against the registration.

Unlike what many people imagine, opposition **is not an appeal** — it is a stage prior to examination. It serves to bring the examiner information he or she may not have, mainly about earlier trademarks that conflict with the application. The INPI does not decide the opposition in isolation: it enters the records and is considered in the merit examination of the application (art. 159 of the LPI).

## Who can oppose

Art. 158 of the LPI requires **legitimate interest**. In practice, this means:

| Opponent situation | Can they oppose? | Typical ground |
|---------------------|-----------|-------------------|
| Owner of an earlier registered trademark | ✅ Yes | Art. 124, XIX (conflict) |
| Applicant of an earlier pending application | ✅ Yes | Art. 124, XIX and art. 129 |
| Company that uses the mark without registration | ✅ Yes (with proof of use) | Art. 124, XIX / bad faith |
| Third party without prior right | ⚠️ Only for absolute bars | Art. 124, I to XVIII (e.g., generic, descriptive sign, contrary to morality) |
| Direct competitor | ✅ Yes | Art. 124, V, VIII, XIX, XXIII |

For relative grounds (conflict with an earlier trademark), the opponent must prove prior right — registration, application, or use. For absolute bars (e.g., generic or descriptive mark), anyone can oppose, because the interest is diffuse: no one can appropriate a sign that the community needs to use.

## The deadline: 60 days from publication

The opposition deadline is **60 calendar days**, counted from the date of publication of the application in the RPI. It is non-extendable — there is no request for extension.

Two details that frequently cause errors:

1. **The count starts at the publication of the application**, not at filing. The application is only published after the initial formal examination phase is completed.
2. **Untimely opposition is not considered.** If the INPI understands that the opposition arrived after the deadline, it is ignored (not considered) and the examination proceeds normally. The only way out of the loss is to actively monitor the process and act within the window.

Therefore, monitoring the RPI is an essential part of managing a trademark portfolio. Those who have a registered trademark and do not monitor new publications may silently lose the chance to oppose. ## Grounds: what to allege in the opposition

The opposition must indicate the bars of **art. 124 of the LPI**. The most used in practice:

| Item | Bar | Practical example |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| XIX | Reproduction/imitation of a third party's trademark that causes confusion | "NIKKE" application for clothing vs. registered NIKKE |
| V | Imitation of a third party's business name | Application identical to an already established company name |
| VIII | Sign that reproduces another's trademark for a different product/service, with possibility of harm | Famous mark used in a different segment |
| XXIII | Necessary/common sign or one that induces false indication | Generic industry term, misleading geographic name |
| VI | Generic, necessary, common, or descriptive sign | "DELIVERY" for delivery service |
| III | Sign contrary to morality and good customs | Offensive term |

Two grounds have a special rule (art. 158, §2 of the LPI): if the opposition is based on **item XXIII (bad faith)** or **art. 126 (well-known mark)**, the opponent must prove, within **60 days after filing**, that they have already filed their own trademark application. Without this proof, the opposition is not considered. In other words: anyone who wants to oppose based on bad faith must first file their trademark.

For well-known marks (art. 126), the owner must also have a registration or application in Brazil — or at least prove notoriety and filing. The [INPI published updated guidelines on well-known marks and the Madrid Protocol]({{< relref "posts/inpi-manual-marcas-alto-renome-protocolo-madri/" >}}), which serve as a reference to substantiate the opposition.

## How to file: step by step in e-Marcas

The opposition is filed through the INPI's **e-Marcas** system, with the "Opposition" petition. The step by step:

1. Access [e-Marcas](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/marcas/e-marcas) with gov.br login.
2. Select the **"Opposition"** service.
3. Enter the number of the published application (the process you want to contest).
4. Attach the petition with the grounds (art. 124 of the LPI), proof of prior right (registration certificate, filing receipt, proof of use) and, when applicable, the proof of art. 158, §2.
5. Generate the boleto/GRU and pay the fee.
6. Follow the publication of the opposition and the notification of the applicant in the RPI.

**One opposition per class.** If the contested application covers multiple classes, it is necessary to assess whether the conflict affects all of them — the fee is per class and the petition must be specific for each one.

## What happens next: the applicant's response

Once the opposition is filed, the INPI **notifies the applicant** to respond within **60 days** (art. 158, §1 of the LPI). This response is the **response to the opposition** — the moment for the applicant to rebut the opponent's arguments.

The response strategy depends on the case:

- **Contesting the conflict** — demonstrating that the signs are not confusing (visual, phonetic, and conceptual differences; distinct classes; peaceful coexistence in the market).
- **Contesting the opponent's right** — pointing out lapse of the earlier registration, lack of use, bad faith of the opponent him/herself, or that his/her mark is generic/descriptive.
- **Presenting evidence of use and distinctiveness** — the applicant can prove that his/her mark is already used and known in the market, which weighs in the confusion analysis.
- **Not responding** — risky option: the opposition remains in the records and the examiner decides based on it (art. 159). Silence does not mean automatic dismissal, but it increases the risk of rejection.

**Practical tip:** both the opponent and the applicant should follow each RPI publication in the process. Any examiner requirement (art. 159) also has a 60-day deadline to respond — failure to comply leads to definitive dismissal. ## The decision and the appeal

Once the examination is concluded, the INPI issues a decision granting or rejecting the registration (art. 160 of the LPI). If the opposition was upheld, the application is rejected; if it was dismissed, the registration is granted and the opponent lost the chance to block it administratively.

The decision may be appealed **within 60 days** (art. 212 of the LPI), addressed to the INPI's appellate instance (3rd Vice-Presidency). The appeal has suspensive and full devolutive effect (art. 212, §1), meaning: it suspends the effects of the decision until judgment.

After administrative remedies are exhausted, the dispute may proceed to the Judiciary — via a nullity action (art. 165 of the LPI) for the side that lost the registration, or an action to annul the granted registration. For those who have a registered trademark under attack, the mechanisms of [cancellation for non-use]({{< relref "posts/caducidade-marca-inpi-guia-2026/" >}}) also come into the strategic picture.

## Strategy: when to oppose and when not to oppose

Opposition is cheap and fast compared to a lawsuit — R$ 520 versus the costs of a years-long process. But it requires judgment:

- ✅ **Always oppose** when there is a real conflict with an earlier registered trademark or one with proven use, especially if the conflicting application was recently published and the market is the same.
- ✅ **Oppose with evidence** — opposition without documents (certificate, proof of use, notoriety) has much less strength.
- ⚠️ **Assess coexistence** — if the marks have coexisted for years without confusion, the opposition may be dismissed and the opponent also becomes exposed to bad-faith challenges.
- ❌ **Do not oppose without prior right** — relative grounds without registration, application, or proven use are usually rejected.

For owners facing counterfeiting in the market, opposition is also a preventive enforcement tool: blocking third-party applications that try to register conflicting marks avoids future conflicts. ## Conclusion

Opposition is the most underutilized tool in the Brazilian trademark system: the 60-day window after publication is the moment of lowest cost and greatest chance of blocking a conflicting application, before it turns into a registration. Mastering the procedure — deadline, grounds of art. 124, proof of prior right, e-Marcas, and costs — separates those who merely file trademarks from those who effectively protect their portfolio.

On the other side, the applicant who receives an opposition has 60 days to present a solid response; ignoring the notification is handing the decision to the opponent.
