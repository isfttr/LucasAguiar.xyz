Keeping a patent in force in Brazil costs money every year. The annuity (or annual fee) is the maintenance fee that the owner must pay to INPI to prevent the application or the granted patent from lapsing. Non-payment is the number one cause of patent extinction in Brazil — and it is also the most avoidable.

This guide consolidates everything you need to know about patent annuities at INPI in 2026: when they become due, how much they cost per year and per patent type, how to pay, what to do if you miss the deadline, and how the new Patent Services Module (being tested from July 27, 2026) will simplify the process.

## What are patent annuities and why they exist

The annuity is the annual fee owed to INPI to keep the patent application or the granted patent in force. It is provided for in Article 84 of the Industrial Property Law (Law 9.279/96 — LPI):

> **Article 84.** Applicants and patent holders are required to pay the annual fee from the beginning of the third year after the filing date.

The amount is progressive: it increases as the patent gets older, reflecting the growing economic benefit that a mature patent can generate. The annuity system exists in virtually all patent offices around the world (USPTO, EPO, JPO) as a way to fund the system and encourage abandonment of patents with low commercial value.

## When annuities become due

Annuities are due **from the 3rd year (24th month) of the filing date** of the patent application — not from grant. This means you may be paying annuities on an application that is still in the examination queue.

| Milestone | Deadline |
|-------|-------|
| 1st annuity | Due in the 3rd year after filing (24 months after filing) |
| Subsequent annuities | Every year, on the same anniversary date of the filing |
| Duration of the obligation | Until the end of the term: 20 years (PI) or 15 years (MU) from filing |

Important: the annuity is due even if the application has not yet been examined. INPI’s backlog (which can take 5 to 9 years for examination to begin) does not suspend the obligation to pay annuities.

## Grace periods, restoration and lapse

INPI offers a tolerance window for delays, but prolonged neglect leads to the definitive lapse of the patent.

### Ordinary term

The annuity must be paid by the due date (filing date + number of years). Payment may be made up to 6 months before the due date.

### Extraordinary term (Article 86, §1 of the LPI)

If not paid within the ordinary term, the annuity can be paid with a 50% fine on the amount within 6 months after the due date:

| Situation | GRU code | Amount |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| Annuity within ordinary term | 220 | Full amount |
| Annuity within extraordinary term (up to 6 months late) | 221 | Amount + 50% |

### Restoration (Article 87 of the LPI)

If the extraordinary term is also missed, INPI publishes the shelving decision (code 8.6 in the RPI). From then on, the holder has **3 months** to request restoration of the application’s processing (GRU 208) and pay the overdue annuity.

After those 3 months, if no restoration is requested, INPI publishes the final shelving decision (8.11) — the patent lapses and its content falls into the public domain.

| Stage | Deadline | Consequence |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| Ordinary payment | By the due date | — |
| Extraordinary payment | Up to 6 months after the due date | 50% fine |
| Shelving (decision 8.6) | After 6 months without payment | Application shelved |
| Restoration | Up to 3 months after decision 8.6 | GRU 208 + payment of the annuity |
| Final shelving (decision 8.11) | After 3 months without restoration | Patent lapse — public domain |

## How to pay: from PAG to the new Patent Services Module

INPI offers two payment methods, with the arrival of the new Patent Services Module in August 2026.

### PAG system (current)

The PAG system (available at [pag.inpi.gov.br](https://pag.inpi.gov.br/)) lets you issue the GRU for any patent service.

1. Access PAG with your gov.br login
2. Select “Patents” and the desired service code (220, 224, etc.)
3. Fill in the process number
4. Issue the GRU
5. Pay at any bank (barcode)

PAG will remain available during the transition period.

### New Patent Services Module (from August 2026)

INPI has launched the second version of the Patent Services Module (in testing from July 27 to August 14, 2026, with exclusive operation beginning on August 19). Among the new features is **integrated annuity management**:

- The system guides the user in issuing the GRU corresponding to the annuity due.
- On the personalized dashboard, you can view a table with the annuity deadlines.
- The module’s mailbox sends advance due-date notices.
- The system also provides guidance on the restoration fee when necessary.

For firms managing hundreds of cases, this feature significantly reduces the risk of missing deadlines.

See full details in the post about the [new INPI Patent Services Module]({{< relref "posts/inpi-modulo-servicos-patentes-novas-funcionalidades-2026/" >}}).

### Practical recommendations

- **Payment schedule:** mark on the calendar the filing anniversary date of each patent. Consider paying 30 days in advance to avoid unexpected banking issues.
- **Chronological tracking:** INPI does not send an individual due-date notice. Responsibility lies 100% with the holder.
- **Consolidation:** if you have multiple patents, centralize control in a spreadsheet or IP management system. INPI’s new Module promises to make this monitoring easier with the personalized dashboard.
- **Power of attorney:** if you use an IP agent, they can manage the payments — but the final legal responsibility always rests with the holder.
- **Category change:** if your company’s size has changed (for example, from an ME to a large legal entity), the annuity amounts change. Update your registration with INPI.

## Patent term × number of annuities

Many people get confused: an invention patent lasts 20 years from filing, but you pay 18 annuities (from the 3rd to the 20th year). This is because the first two years are covered by the filing fee.

For utility models (15-year term), there are 13 annuities (from the 3rd to the 15th year).

| Type | Term | Annuities to pay | Period |
|------|----------|-------------------|---------|
| Invention Patent (PI) | 20 years | 18 | 3rd to 20th year |
| Utility Model (MU) | 15 years | 13 | 3rd to 15th year |
| Certificate of Addition (CAd) | Until the end of the PI | Variable | Linked to the principal PI |

## Differences between application annuities and granted patent annuities

While the application is under examination (not granted), the annuity paid is the “patent application” annuity (code 220). After grant, the annuity is the “patent” annuity (codes 224 to 229). Fees for a granted patent are higher than those for an application.

This means that, in the year of grant, if the patent is granted after the application annuity has been paid, it may be necessary to top up to the patent annuity — check with INPI or your agent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to pay annuities during INPI’s backlog?

Yes. The obligation to pay annuities starts in the 3rd year after filing, regardless of whether the technical examination has started. The backlog does not suspend the obligation.

### Can I pay several annuities at once?

Yes, there is no legal impediment to paying multiple annuities in advance. The PAG system allows you to issue GRUs for whatever period you wish.

### Is the annuity deductible from income tax?

Yes. Patent annuities are deductible operating expenses for legal entities taxed under the Real Profit regime. Consult your accountant.

### What happens if I miss the restoration deadline?

The patent lapses permanently (final shelving 8.11). Its content falls into the public domain and can no longer be recovered. The only recourse is judicial, and the chances are remote.

### Will the new module charge more?

No. The new Patent Services Module is a digital platform — the fees (GRU) are the same as in the PAG system. The advantage lies in integrated management and automatic reminders.
