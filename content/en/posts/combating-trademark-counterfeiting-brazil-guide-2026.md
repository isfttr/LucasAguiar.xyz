Brazil's illegal market cost the country more than **R$ 473 billion in 2025** — smuggling, counterfeiting and piracy combined, according to the Fórum Nacional Contra a Pirataria e a Ilegalidade (FNCP). Of that total, R$ 326.3 billion were direct losses to industry and R$ 146.8 billion in tax evasion, across 15 sectors. In 2020, the damage was R$ 288 billion. For the owner of a registered trademark, this is not statistics: it is an unfair competitor selling fake goods, illegal marketplace listings and shipments held — or not — at customs.

This guide maps the legal toolkit available in Brazil to fight trademark counterfeiting: administrative measures (customs, government councils and police), civil measures (preliminary injunctions, search and seizure, damages) and criminal measures (private criminal complaint), with the legal basis for each and a practical step-by-step.

> In July 2026, [ABPI (Brazilian IP Association) joined the National Council to Combat Piracy and Intellectual Property Crimes (CNCP)](https://abpi.org.br/noticias/abpi-integra-o-conselho-nacional-de-combate-a-pirataria/), strengthening coordination between the private sector and public authorities. CNCP is the advisory body of the Ministry of Justice and Public Security created by [Decree No. 9.875/2019](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2019-2022/2019/decreto/D9875.htm) to shape Brazil's national plan against piracy, smuggling, related tax evasion and intellectual property crimes.

## Counterfeiting, piracy and smuggling: using the right terms

Before choosing a measure, it helps to know what the law actually calls each thing:

| Term | What it means | Legal basis |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| **Trademark counterfeiting** | Reproducing, imitating or altering a registered trademark without authorization, or selling/storing products bearing a counterfeit mark | Law 9.279/96 (Brazilian IP Law, "LPI"), arts. 189-190 |
| **Piracy** (legal sense) | Copyright infringement — software, music, movies, books | Laws 9.609/98 and 9.610/98 |
| **Smuggling / customs evasion** | Importing or exporting prohibited goods (smuggling) or evading import taxes (customs evasion) | Criminal Code, arts. 334-A and 334 |
| **False indication of origin** | Using a geographic name that misleads consumers about a product's true origin | LPI, arts. 178 and 198 |

The distinction matters in practice: CNCP's governing decree, for example, defines "piracy" strictly as copyright infringement, while trademark fakes are treated as counterfeiting. In everyday language, though, "piracy" has become a catch-all — which complicates communication with authorities and the correct legal framing of a case.

## Why trademark registration is the starting point

Without an INPI registration there is no counterfeiting — at most, unfair competition. The LPI gives the owner of a registered trademark the right to protect its material integrity and reputation (art. 130, III), and the crimes in arts. 189-190 presuppose a **registered** mark. The first step of any enforcement strategy is therefore to have the registration granted and maintained. ## The legal basis for fighting counterfeiting

| Measure | Legal basis | What it allows |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| Customs seizure of counterfeit goods | LPI, art. 198; Customs Regulation (Decree 6.759/2009), arts. 605-606; TRIPS Agreement, arts. 51-60 | Customs authorities retain goods bearing counterfeit, altered or imitated marks, or false indications of origin, ex officio or upon request |
| Search and seizure | LPI, arts. 200-201; Code of Criminal Procedure | Seizure of counterfeit goods and materials, including in preliminary proceedings before the main action |
| Preliminary injunction | LPI, art. 209, § 1 | Urgent relief to stop the infringement |
| Damages | LPI, arts. 209-210 | Compensation with lost profits calculated under the criterion most favorable to the injured party |
| Crime of reproducing/imitating a mark | LPI, art. 189 | Penalty: 3 months to 1 year detention, or fine |
| Crime of selling/storing counterfeit goods | LPI, art. 190 | Penalty: 1 to 3 months detention, or fine |
| Crime of unfair competition | LPI, art. 195 | Various conducts, including customer diversion and misuse of another's sign |

## Administrative measures: customs and government councils

The fastest and cheapest route starts at the border. Under art. 198 of the LPI, customs authorities may **seize, ex officio or upon request**, during the customs check, products bearing counterfeit, altered or imitated marks, or false indications of origin. The Customs Regulation (Decree 6.759/2009) details the procedure:

- **Art. 605**: customs may retain the products, ex officio or upon request, during the customs check.
- **Art. 606**: after retention, the trademark owner is notified and has **10 business days** from notice to file the criminal complaint and request the **judicial seizure** of the goods. The deadline may be extended once, for an equal period, in justified cases (mirroring art. 55 of the TRIPS Agreement).

In practice, this means the owner must have an active channel with the Federal Revenue Service and counsel ready to file the judicial measure within the deadline — retention alone does not destroy the goods; it only opens the window to judicialize.

At the institutional level, **CNCP** (Decree 9.875/2019) is the forum coordinating government and civil society: it produces studies, maintains statistics linked to the national public security system, proposes inspections at ports, airports and borders, and coordinates awareness campaigns. ABPI's entry into the Council (July 2026) strengthens private-sector technical input into these policies.

Specialized police units also matter — such as the Delegacia de Repressão aos Crimes contra a Propriedade Imaterial (DRCPIM) of São Paulo's Deic — along with Federal Police operations triggered by the owner's report.

## Civil measures: speed is everything

When the infringement is located (physical store, warehouse, marketplace), the owner can:

1. **Extrajudicial notice** — often resolves cases against good-faith small retailers, but rarely against organized counterfeiters.
2. **Preliminary injunction** — art. 209, § 1 of the LPI allows the court to order, on an urgent basis, the **suspension of the infringement** to prevent irreparable or hard-to-repair damage. This is the most common tool against marketplaces and websites that keep selling after notice.
3. **Search and seizure** — under arts. 200-201 of the LPI, governed by the Code of Criminal Procedure, including preliminary proceedings to collect evidence before the main action.
4. **Damages action** — arts. 209-210 guarantee compensation, with lost profits calculated under the **criterion most favorable to the injured party**, among: (i) what the owner would have earned without the infringement; (ii) the infringer's profits; or (iii) a hypothetical license fee.

The critical point in the civil route is **evidence preservation**: test purchases with invoices, timestamped screenshots, photos of the fake next to the genuine product, and sealed samples. Without this, an injunction has weak support.

## Criminal measures: private prosecution and Brazilian peculiarities

Trademark counterfeiting crimes have two peculiarities that surprise those coming from other jurisdictions:

- **Private prosecution**: under art. 199 of the LPI, the crimes in the Title (including arts. 189-190) are prosecuted **only upon the victim's complaint** — the injured owner must file the criminal complaint. The exception is art. 191 (misuse of official arms, coats of arms and insignia), which is publicly prosecuted.
- **Low penalties**: 3 months to 1 year detention (art. 189) and 1 to 3 months (art. 190). As minor offenses, Law 9.099/95 applies, with the possibility of a **plea bargain-like transaction (transação penal)** — which in practice turns the criminal case into a negotiation and formal-record tool more than an effective punishment.

Still, the criminal route has strategic value: the complaint enables preliminary search and seizure, pressures the infringer into settlement, and builds a judicial track record useful in repeat civil cases. For foreign owners, [international trademark protection through the Madrid Protocol]({{< relref "posts/inpi-manual-marcas-alto-renome-protocolo-madri/" >}}) secures the Brazilian registration based on the international application — the prerequisite for any local enforcement measure.

## E-commerce and social media

Much of the counterfeiting has moved online, and fighting it requires a dedicated strategy:

- **Brazilian Internet Bill of Rights** (Law 12.965/2014, art. 19): application providers are liable for third-party content only if, after a **specific court order**, they fail to take action to remove it. The judicial route is therefore the standard path against recalcitrant marketplaces.
- **Platform voluntary programs**: Amazon Brand Registry, Mercado Livre's Brand Protection Program and AliExpress' Intellectual Property Protection (IPP) allow faster reporting and removal of counterfeit listings, without a court order. Each platform has its own registration and evidence rules — a standardized dossier pays off.
- **Continuous monitoring**: automated searches for the mark plus common misspellings ("iphone", "i-phone", "iPh0ne") are the starting point for detecting fake listings before they scale.

## Step-by-step: the owner's checklist

1. **Keep the registration current** — without an INPI registration there is no counterfeiting. Watch renewal deadlines and [maintenance costs]({{< relref "posts/quanto-custa-registrar-marca-inpi-2026/" >}}).
2. **Monitor** — marketplaces, social media, trade fairs, ports and borders. Establish contact with the Federal Revenue unit in your region.
3. **Document and preserve evidence** — test purchases, invoices, timestamped screenshots, samples.
4. **Notify** — send extrajudicial notice to the infringer and the platform (brand protection program).
5. **Judicialize fast** — preliminary injunction or search and seizure; if applicable, build on an ongoing customs retention.
6. **Claim damages** — action for losses based on the criteria of art. 210 of the LPI.
7. **Consider the criminal route** — private complaint (art. 199) when authorship is known and a formal record of the infringement is valuable.

## What to watch

Anti-counterfeiting enforcement in Brazil is moving. Keep an eye on: (i) the renewed CNCP agenda with ABPI's participation and the national anti-piracy plan; (ii) case law on marketplace liability for counterfeit listings; (iii) customs and federal police operations at ports and borders; and (iv) the trajectory of the illegal market — from R$ 288 billion (2020) to R$ 473 billion (2025) — which makes enforcement an essential part of brand strategy, not an accessory cost.
