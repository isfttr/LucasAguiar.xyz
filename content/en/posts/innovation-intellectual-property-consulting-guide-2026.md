Intellectual property and innovation consulting turns intangible assets — trademarks, patents, industrial designs, software and know-how — into measurable competitive advantage. It also prevents the opposite scenario: losing a right to a missed deadline, a poorly drafted application, or a portfolio with no strategy. This guide explains what this kind of consulting delivers, when to hire it, what it costs, and how to choose the right professional.

## What IP and innovation consulting is

It is the specialized service of identifying, protecting, managing and monetizing intangible assets — and connecting that protection to the company's innovation strategy. While a traditional lawyer acts on specific disputes, the consultant acts earlier: designing the protection strategy, analyzing feasibility, managing the portfolio and capturing economic value from the rights.

The work combines three fronts:

- **Technical**: prior art searches, drafting and prosecution of patent applications, registration of trademarks, industrial designs and software.
- **Strategic**: aligning the IP portfolio with the business plan, market and competition.
- **Economic**: valuation of intangibles, licensing, technology transfer agreements and use of IP as credit collateral.

## What a consultant delivers

The scope varies with company maturity, but the most common services are:

**1. Portfolio diagnosis and strategy**
A survey of what the company already protects, what it should protect, and what is exposed. The output is a prioritized plan with costs and deadlines.

**2. Prior art search and feasibility**
Before filing a patent or a trademark application, you need to know whether it has a chance. Prior art searches avoid spending money on applications that will be rejected — and they are mandatory to qualify as a search provider at Brazil's INPI.

**3. Registration and prosecution at the INPI**
Trademarks, patents (invention and utility model), industrial designs, computer programs and geographical indications. Includes case follow-up until grant, with deadline and fee management.

**4. IP due diligence**
In M&A, funding rounds and tenders, the buyer or investor needs to know that the intangible assets exist, are valid and actually belong to the company. Due diligence maps this in a technical report.

**5. Licensing and technology transfer agreements**
Structuring contracts that monetize IP without giving up control — and registering those contracts at the INPI, which is mandatory for them to be effective against third parties.

**6. Industrial policy, incentives and innovation**
Support in using public policy instruments: special regimes, innovation incentives and support programs that require IP protection as a counterpart.

## When to hire

Some signs indicate a company needs IP consulting:

- You discovered that a competitor registered a trademark identical or similar to yours.
- The company innovates but never ran a prior art search before launching products.
- An investor, client or tender requires proof that the intellectual property belongs to the company.
- You want to know whether a technology or brand has economic value — to sell, license or use as collateral.
- Registration deadlines are passing with no one accountable.

The cost of hiring after the problem happens (opposition, nullity, litigation) is always higher than the cost of preventing it.

## How much it costs

There is no single price list — it depends on the service and complexity:

- **Prior art search**: from US$ 100 to US$ 600 per search, depending on depth and scope (national or international).
- **Portfolio diagnosis**: from US$ 600 to US$ 3,000, depending on the number of assets.
- **Trademark registration**: fees from ~US$ 200 to US$ 500 per class (official INPI fees are separate and vary by company size).
- **Patent drafting and filing**: from US$ 1,000 to US$ 4,000, depending on technical complexity.
- **IP due diligence**: usually charged per project, from US$ 1,500 to US$ 8,000.
- **Recurring consulting (retainer)**: from US$ 400 to US$ 2,000/month for continuous portfolio management.

## How to choose a consultant

Check four things: technical or legal background in the field, proven experience with the type of asset you need to protect, transparency about costs and deadlines, and — for patents — technical qualification compatible with the invention's field. Ask for reports of past work and avoid anyone promising guaranteed grant: no INPI registration is guaranteed.
