Cachaça is one of the Brazilian products with the oldest relationship between reputation and territory: the name of the production region has always carried commercial significance — from Paraty to Salinas, through the historic towns of Minas Gerais and Bahia. What many producers still do not know is that this relationship can be turned into an industrial property asset registered with INPI.

In July 2026, INPI published in the Industrial Property Magazine (RPI) No. 2898 the registration of the geographical indication (GI) **Circuito das Águas Paulista**, in the indication of origin (IP) category, for the pot-still cachaça produced in nine municipalities in São Paulo. With this recognition, Brazil now has 177 GIs — and cachaça has established itself as one of the categories with the highest number of registrations in the country. This guide explains, in practice, how the GI works for cachaça, which regions are already protected, and how producers can obtain their own registration.

## What is a geographical indication (GI)

GI is the industrial property category that protects the **link between a product and its geographical origin**, when that origin confers reputation, characteristics, or quality on the product. In Brazil, this legal mechanism is provided for in articles 176 to 182 of the [Industrial Property Law (Law 9.279/1996)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm) and is granted by INPI.

There are two types, and the difference is decisive when filing an application:

| Type | What it requires | Example in cachaça |
|---------|-------------|--------------------|
| **Indication of Origin (IP)** | That the geographical name has become known as a production center for the product | Salinas Region (MG), Abaíra Micro-region (BA), Circuito das Águas Paulista (SP) |
| **Denomination of Origin (DO)** | That the qualities or characteristics of the product are **exclusively or essentially** due to the geographical environment (natural and human factors), proven by technical reports | Paraty (RJ), Luiz Alves (SC) |

In practice: IP requires **renown** of the geographical name (documentary evidence that the region is recognized as a production center), while DO requires **technical evidence** that the territory — soil, climate, water, traditional techniques — influences the product. That is why DO is harder to obtain and, in general, more valuable.

A detail that surprises many people: Paraty is a **denomination of origin** (and not an indication of origin, as common sense suggests), while Salinas is an **indication of origin**. It is always worth checking INPI's official technical data sheet before stating the category of any registration.

## Cachaça GIs registered with INPI

According to the [official technical data sheets for granted geographical indications](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/servicos/indicacoes-geograficas/fichas-tecnicas-de-indicacoes-geograficas), Brazil currently has five cachaça and aguardente GIs:

| GI | State | Type | Grant |
|----|----|---------|-----------|
| Paraty | RJ | DO | July 10, 2007 (amendment approved on January 30, 2024) |
| Salinas Region | MG | IP | October 16, 2012 |
| Abaíra Micro-region | BA | IP | October 14, 2014 |
| Luiz Alves (cachaça and aguardente) | SC | DO | August 6, 2024 |
| Circuito das Águas Paulista (pot-still cachaça) | SP | IP | July 21, 2026 (RPI 2898) |

The most recent case illustrates the model well: the [Circuito das Águas Paulista](https://www.gov.br/inpi/pt-br/central-de-conteudo/noticias/inpi-concede-a-ig-circuito-das-aguas-paulista-para-a-cachaca-de-alambique) brings together more than 350 stills and producers across nine municipalities (Águas de Lindóia, Amparo, Holambra, Jaguariúna, Lindóia, Monte Alegre do Sul, Pedreira, Serra Negra, and Socorro), with a family tradition associated with rural tourism. The region has two of the three best cachaças in the state at the Concurso Paulista de Cachaça de Alambique, which had more than 150 competitors — exactly the kind of reputation that IP protects.

For producers, these technical data sheets serve as **reference models**: they show the documentary structure that INPI accepted in previous applications, including the technical specifications notebook and the delimitation of the area.

## Step by step: how to register a cachaça GI

GI registration is **collective** — it does not belong to an individual producer but to a representative entity (union, association, or institute) that administers it and defines, in the rules of use, who may use the GI and under what conditions. The complete step-by-step is in the [INPI geographical indications guide]({{< relref "posts/guia-indicacoes-geograficas-inpi-brasil/" >}}), but the essentials are:

**1. Organize the representative entity.** Bring together producers in the region into an association or union with bylaws that provide for representation of the collective. Without an entity, there is no application.

**2. Prepare the documentation.**
- For **IP**: prove that the geographical name is known as a production center — historical materials, news reports, publications, fair and competition certificates, awards.
- For **DO**: delimitation of the area, description of the product's qualities, technical and scientific reports demonstrating the influence of the geographical environment (soil, climate, altitude, traditional techniques), and description of the production process.
- In both cases: rules of use and an instrument proving the applicant's legitimacy.

**3. Pay the GRU fee.** The GI registration application costs **BRL 3,200.00**, reduced to **BRL 1,600.00** for ME (micro-enterprises), EPP (small businesses), MEI (individual micro-entrepreneurs), and non-profit entities (INPI/PR Ordinance No. 10/2025) — amounts that make GI accessible even to small producer associations.

**4. File through e-IG.** Since INPI/PR Resolution No. 233/2019, applications are exclusively electronic, through the e-IG system. The process follows the stages of formal examination, publication in the RPI (with the possibility of third-party submissions), and substantive examination, with grants typically taking between 6 and 18 months.

## What the GI allows — and what it does not

The GI protects the **geographical name** against improper use: no one from outside the delimited area may label their product with the protected name. It also adds commercial value — official recognition is used on packaging, exports, and rural tourism programs, as occurs with the "Rota da Cachaça" and "Trilha da Cachaça" in the Circuito das Águas Paulista.

But there are important limits:
- The GI **is not a trademark**: it does not belong to one producer; it is for collective use according to the rules.
- The GI **does not protect the formula** or the production process — that is the domain of patents.
- Improper use by those who do not comply with the rules is subject to administrative and judicial actions, but enforcement depends largely on the owner entity itself.

To understand where the GI fits within the set of protection instruments (patent, trademark, industrial design, copyright), see the [comparative guide to types of intellectual protection in Brazil]({{< relref "posts/tipos-protecao-intelectual-brasil-guia-completo/" >}}).

## What to watch in the coming years

Brazil is still in its infancy in GIs compared with Europe: of the 177 registered GIs, only 44 are denominations of origin (34 national and 10 foreign), compared with 133 indications of origin (132 national and 1 foreign). The trend is for DOs to grow as cachaça producers — especially in regions with notable altitude and climate, such as the highlands of Minas Gerais, Bahia, and São Paulo — provide technical proof of the territory's influence on the quality of the distillate.

For producers, the central message is this: the reputation built over generations around the name of your region can — and should — be protected as an industrial property asset. Pot-still cachaça has already become a GI case at INPI. The question is which region will be next.
